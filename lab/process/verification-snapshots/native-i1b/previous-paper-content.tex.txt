\section{The question and the historical record}
\label{sec:argument}

Nguyen and Polya's response is dated February 23, 2021. Its stated source
is Weinstein's video presentation of the 2013 Oxford lecture with 2020
follow-up material; the authors explicitly treat the proposal as
pre-quantum classical theory \cite[pp.~1--2]{NP}. The Geometric Unity
(GU) working draft is dated April 1, 2021 \cite{GU}. The later formulas
can help evaluate the current mathematical question, but cannot be used
to accuse the February response of overlooking text not yet released.

The argument of \cite[\S3.1, pp.~5--6]{NP} has three steps.
First, the authors interpret the proposed pure-trace Shiab contraction
using an identification
\[
 \ad(P)\otimes\mathbb C \simeq
 \Lambda^\bullet(T^*U)\otimes\mathbb C
 \simeq \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C} S(U).
\]
Second, they object that the required identification is not supplied on
the stated real objects. Third, they infer that complexifying the
connections and gauge group forces either nonunitarity or a Hamiltonian
unbounded above and below, citing Witten's complex Chern--Simons paper
\cite{Witten1991}. The introduction, p.~1, presents this as excluding a
physically sensible quantum theory.

We separate the real-structure question from the energy inference.
Equal-dimensional real vector spaces certainly admit abstract linear
isomorphisms; the relevant question is compatibility with the specified
representations, real structures and operations, rather than dimension
alone. We grant for this audit that a proposed real identification may
need correction. Even then, using complexified bundles in an algebraic
construction does not by itself declare every complex component to be an
independent real physical field. That declaration requires a real slice,
action, constraints and gauge transformations. The later GU draft
explicitly uses degree-dependent factors of $i$ and the real group
$U(64,64)$ inside complex matrices \cite[pp.~23--24, 42]{GU}.
An indefinite unitary group, its complexification, and a positive physical
Hilbert space are three different objects. The draft's notation does not
identify them.

There are two readings of the last step:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Literal universal reading:} complexifying the gauge group
alone excludes positive unitary quantum dynamics with energy bounded below.
\item \emph{Charitable dynamical reading:} the intended propagating gauge
sector is an ordinary real Yang--Mills theory for the complexified group,
with an indefinite invariant kinetic form and physical modes in its
negative directions. Its standard quantization then has a positivity or
energy problem.
\end{enumerate}
The second reading is plausible: the response discusses dynamical
connections, and \S3.4, p.~8, explicitly invokes GU's promised recovery of
Yang--Mills equations. It is not a premise written or proved in \S3.1.
Indeed, the equation actually reproduced in \S2.3, p.~5, involves a Shiab
contraction of curvature, augmented torsion and a Hodge term; it is not
an ordinary curvature-square Yang--Mills action. Neither a literal-only
rebuttal nor silent insertion of a conventional kinetic term settles the
intended GU question.

\section{The Yang--Mills obstruction with its hypotheses exposed}
\label{sec:ym}

Let $G$ be a connected Lie group whose real Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$
has a nondegenerate, $\operatorname{Ad}(G)$-invariant symmetric bilinear
form $\beta$. On
$\mathbb R_t\times\mathbb T^d$, $d\geq2$, use the flat metric of signature
$(-,+,\ldots,+)$, a trivial bundle, real connection components and
\begin{equation}
 S_{\YM}=-\frac1{4g^2}\int\beta(F_{\mu\nu},F^{\mu\nu})\,dt\,dx,
 \qquad g^2>0. \label{eq:ym}
\end{equation}
With $E_i=F_{0i}$, the energy and Gauss constraint are
\begin{align}
 H&=\frac1{2g^2}\int_{\mathbb T^d}
 \left(\sum_i\beta(E_i,E_i)+
 \sum_{i<j}\beta(F_{ij},F_{ij})\right)dx, \label{eq:H}\\
 D_iE_i&=0. \label{eq:Gauss}
\end{align}
Nondegeneracy of $\beta$ allows the usual identification of canonical
momentum with $g^{-2}\beta(E,\cdot)$. All these assumptions belong to this
comparator, not to GU by default.

\begin{proposition}[Negative energy survives the ordinary Gauss constraint]
\label{prop:negative}
If some $X\in\mathfrak g$ has $\beta(X,X)<0$, the Hamiltonian
\eqref{eq:H} is unbounded below on smooth Gauss-compatible initial data,
even after ordinary gauge equivalence. If $\beta$ has both signs, the
Hamiltonian is also unbounded above.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose a nonzero smooth divergence-free real vector field $u$ on the
torus and take
\[
 A_i=0,\qquad E_i=\lambda X u_i.
\]
Then $F_{ij}=0$ and $D_iE_i=\lambda X\partial_i u_i=0$, whereas
\[
 H=\frac{\lambda^2\beta(X,X)}{2g^2}
       \int_{\mathbb T^d}|u|^2dx\longrightarrow-\infty.
\]
Gauge transformations preserve $H$ by invariance of $\beta$, so taking
the gauge quotient cannot remove these values. Using a positive direction
instead proves the assertion about the upper bound. For a nonconstant
transverse example one may choose $u_1=\sin x^2$ and $u_i=0$ for $i\ne1$.
\end{proof}

This is an exact classical result, not a finite-difference instability
or a proof about an unspecified quantum domain. It demonstrates why the
negative internal direction is not merely the removable timelike
polarization of ordinary compact Yang--Mills theory. On a one-color
abelian subsector, $[X,X]=0$, so the negative sign is already visible in
an ordinary transverse Maxwell mode.

For example, if a complex Lie algebra carries a nondegenerate complex
symmetric invariant form $b$, its underlying real vector space admits
\[
 \beta_c(X,Y)=\operatorname{Re}\bigl(c\,b(X,Y)\bigr),\qquad c\ne0.
\]
It is real nondegenerate: vanishing against both $Y$ and $iY$ forces
$b(X,Y)=0$ for every $Y$. Moreover,
$\beta_c(iX,iX)=-\beta_c(X,X)$, so its signature is split. This explains
the kinetic-sign problem when this form and these real independent fields
are actually used in \eqref{eq:ym}. It says nothing about a theory with
a different action or physical reduction.

The usual quantum alternative is equally concrete at the linear level.
A negative transverse oscillator quantized on positive $L^2(\mathbb R)$
has
\[
 \widehat H=-\tfrac12(\widehat p^{\,2}+\omega^2\widehat q^{\,2}),
 \qquad \operatorname{spec}(\widehat H)
 =\{-\omega(n+\tfrac12):n\geq0\}
\]
in units $\hbar=1$. It is self-adjoint and generates unitary time
evolution, but is not bounded below. Exchanging the roles of annihilation
and creation operators to reverse the ladder gives a negative one-particle
norm if the same adjoint and canonical commutator are retained. This
explains the conventional dilemma; it is not a classification of all
possible contours, representations or constrained quantum theories.
Witten's introductory comparison makes the corresponding action-dependent
point \cite[report pp.~2--3]{WittenReport}.

\section{What Witten's construction does and does not say}
\label{sec:witten}

Witten studies three-dimensional Chern--Simons theory for the
complexification $G_{\mathbb C}$ of a compact group $G$, not a propagating
Yang--Mills curvature-square theory. His action pairs a
complex connection $\mathcal A$ with its complex conjugate in two
Chern--Simons terms, with couplings $t=k+is$ and
$\widetilde t=k-is$. In the real-$s$ branch relevant here, $k$ is
quantized and $s$ is real, with $t,\widetilde t\ne0$ for the displayed
connection formulas (for example $k=s=1$); the physical reality condition and the
choice of polarization are part of the construction. No assertion about
arbitrary complex couplings is needed. The alternative imaginary-$s$
positive construction is explicit only in genus one with restricted
parameters; no all-genus positivity assertion from that branch is used.

In canonical quantization on $\Sigma\times\mathbb R$, with $\Sigma$
a closed oriented surface, the time component of the connection imposes
flatness. The phase space is a moduli space of flat complex connections,
not the unconstrained space of transverse Yang--Mills waves. The
Hamiltonian of this pure theory is a constraint and vanishes on reduced
physical states. Thus the time evolution of this fixed, source-free
closed-surface problem is the identity. A zero Hamiltonian is bounded
below, but this does not imply that the state space is empty or that all
topological observables are trivial. Boundary terms, puncture/source
insertions and chosen boundary dynamics require a separate analysis;
one may not export $H=0$ to those problems or to a propagating theory.

The precise theorem used here concerns a \emph{connection on a Hilbert
bundle over Teichm\"uller space}, the parameter space of auxiliary complex
structures on $\Sigma$. The fiber is described as $L^2(\mathcal M,L^k)$,
where $\mathcal M$ is the compact-group moduli space used in the chosen
polarization and $L^k$ its quantization line bundle. For real $s$, the
specified corrected connection $\delta^Q$ is unitary and projectively
flat, with central curvature of type $(1,1).$ The pairing has the form
$\int_{\mathcal M} H(\chi,\psi)\,d\mu$, where $H$ incorporates the
regularized Laplacian determinant; conjugation by $H^{1/2}$ identifies it
with the ordinary positive $L^2$ pairing. This is unitary
\emph{parameter-space transport}, not a theorem identifying a
four-dimensional physical-time Hamiltonian. Projective flatness also
retains a scalar-phase issue; it is not literal flatness.

There is a further rigor qualification. Witten gives heuristic reasoning
for the quantum correction to the connection, then proves the stated
properties of the selected formulas. Moduli-space singularities,
noncompactness and the definition of general functional integrals are
not thereby eliminated. In particular, the identification of the reduced
state space uses the dense stable locus and the corresponding
Narasimhan--Seshadri description; it is not an unrestricted assertion
about every singular stratum. The paper supplies a mathematical construction
and a substantive counterexample to the group-only slogan in its stated
quantization setting; it is not a complete axiomatic construction of
interacting QFT for every complex group. The positive pairing and the
closed-surface zero-Hamiltonian argument are separate ingredients.
Neither should be replaced by the bare word ``unitary.''

All detailed formula locators in this paragraph refer to the
44-page IASSNS-HEP-89/65 report: \S3, p.~8 (closed surface);
\S4, pp.~19--21 (correction and theorem); \S4.1, pp.~22--23,
Eqs.~(4.12) and (4.16) (pairings) \cite{WittenReport}.
The journal bibliographic identity is verified, but its typesetting was
not independently inspected; no journal page is used as a formula locator.

\section{Testing both transfers against GU's displayed action}
\label{sec:gu}

\subsection{The relevant source-native objects}

The April draft uses the fourteen-dimensional metric bundle
$Y=\operatorname{Met}(X)$ over a four-dimensional base, a metric-dependent
connection, an auxiliary gauge field $\varepsilon$, and an
adjoint-valued one-form $T$ measuring displacement of one connection from
a gauge-rotated reference connection $B$. This $T$ is not assumed to be
ordinary spacetime torsion. The source's $(7,7)$ ambient metric and
$U(64,64)$ spinor construction are its declared setting; we do not derive
the ambient signature here or identify its multiple time directions with
an observed Lorentzian Cauchy problem.

Write $\mathcal S$ for the Shiab contraction. The first bosonic action
is built from the pairing of $T$ with the Shiab image of a
connection-path curvature combination, together with a Hodge quadratic
term. Schematically, suppressing only the source's pairing/Hodge
placement and bracket normalization, its structure is
\begin{equation}
 I_1^B\sim
 \langle T,\mathcal S(F_B+\tfrac12D_BT+\tfrac13[T,T])\rangle
 +\tfrac\kappa2\langle T,*T\rangle.
 \label{eq:i1}
\end{equation}
The source gives the full formula in Eq.~(9.4), p.~44
\cite{GU}. Equation~\eqref{eq:i1} is used only to identify its terms and
derivative orders, not to derive numerical coefficients or field
equations. It is first order in the distortion derivative when the other
fields are held fixed, with a metric-dependent contraction and a quadratic
term. It is not the action \eqref{eq:ym}; nor does the presence of
Chern--Simons-like cubic terms make it pure three-dimensional
Chern--Simons theory.

The second bosonic action is displayed as
\begin{equation}
 I_2^B=\|\Upsilon^B\|^2,\qquad
 \Upsilon^B=\mathcal S(F_A)+*\kappa T,
 \label{eq:i2}
\end{equation}
with the source's norm and reality conventions. The associated second
order term is labeled Yang--Mills-like, but contains
$d_A^*\mathcal S^*\mathcal S F_A$ \cite[p.~45,
Eqs.~(9.7), (9.11)--(9.15)]{GU}. The stars denoting Hodge operators
and adjoints must not be silently equated, and the symbol $\|\cdot\|^2$
on an indefinite carrier does not certify positivity. Also, the printed
identification of $\Upsilon^B$ with the variation of $I_1^B$ is a claim
to check for the chosen contraction, not permission to identify any two
variational owners. We keep $I_1^B$, the printed residual, and its square
separate.

\subsection{Why neither kinetic sign follows from these formulas alone}

For a twice continuously differentiable residual $R$ and a smoothly
varying symmetric pairing $Q$,
$S(\phi)=Q_\phi(R(\phi),R(\phi))$ has, at $R(\phi_0)=0$,
\begin{equation}
 D^2S_{\phi_0}(v,w)
 =2Q_{\phi_0}(DR_{\phi_0}v,DR_{\phi_0}w).
 \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
This follows by differentiating twice; every other term contains
$R(\phi_0)$ and vanishes. Consequently the kinetic form of a squared
residual depends on the image of its linearization. An indefinite
ambient $Q$ need not be negative on that image, while writing a square
does not make its restriction positive. If one freezes the Shiab,
Hodge operator and reference structures in \eqref{eq:i2}, the
connection variation contains $\mathcal S d_Aa+*\kappa\delta T$.
Moving those structures introduces additional terms. Its principal
form cannot be read off from the sign of an internal trace alone.
Nor does a zero of this residual automatically give a stationary
background for a different action or their sum.

To apply \cref{prop:negative} or its local mode version to GU one must
establish the following bridge:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A definite real action and an actual stationary background, together
with the relevant real field variations and time direction.
\item The reduced kinetic or canonical energy form, obtained by varying
that action and imposing its constraints and gauge identifications,
including the observation map to four dimensions.
\item A nonzero negative-energy direction in the allowed physical domain,
with a map identifying its energy with the Yang--Mills comparator (or a
direct negative-mode proof for the native action).
\end{enumerate}
A negative finite matrix before reduction is insufficient. Conversely,
absence of this bridge does not prove that the action avoids the
obstruction. In the inspected source, the proposed deformation complex
is discussed as a construction to obtain, and the four-dimensional
observation and field interpretation remain separate steps
\cite[\S\S10--11, pp.~47--51]{GU}. No common reduced physical Hamiltonian,
positive state completion and invariant domain is constructed there.
The required hypotheses are therefore \emph{unestablished}, not proved
false.

For the Chern--Simons transfer, the corresponding burden is stronger
than a resemblance of action terms. One would need a source-derived
constraint/reduction or a proved equivalence to a quantizable positive
sector, with the real structure, symplectic form, measure, pairing and
domain transported. One must then identify the generator of observed
physical time and show that interactions preserve this sector. A pure
closed-surface topological sector with $H=0$ cannot itself explain the
propagating four-dimensional dynamics GU seeks. A coupled theory would
need its own energy and unitarity argument. No such transfer follows
from complexification or the Shiab terminology.

\subsection{The maximal-compact proposal and the pairing trap}

The draft itself acknowledges a difficulty. Page~29, footnote~5, recalls
a Bar-Natan--Witten modification of the Hodge operator involving the
noncompact complement, while saying the technique has not been
reconstructed \cite{GU}. This is a genuine source pointer, not an
implemented repair. Bar-Natan and Witten \cite{BNW} work in
\emph{perturbative Chern--Simons theory}. Their compact reduction yields
an involution $T_{\mathrm{BNW}}$, equal to $+1$ on the compact algebra
and $-1$ on its complement, and a positive $*T_{\mathrm{BNW}}$-based
pairing in their trace convention. This is their $T$ in Eq.~(4.4),
not their separate form-grading operator $J$. They also change the gauge-fixing operators and
analyze compensating local counterterms. Section~3 explains why keeping
the old operators while changing only the pairing does not suffice.
The inspected calculation is the leading, one-loop stationary-phase
analysis about isolated irreducible flat connections (preprint \S2,
pp.~5--6; \S4, pp.~14--23), not an all-orders physical unitarity theorem.
Their analysis does not establish positivity of the Hamiltonian of GU's
propagating fields.

An elementary finite-dimensional control makes the missing step visible.
\begin{lemma}[A positive replacement pairing need not be conserved]
Let $\eta=\eta^*$ be invertible and let a real-time generator $A$ obey
$A^*\eta+\eta A=0$. Suppose $W=\eta J$ is positive Hermitian. Then
\[
 A^*W+WA=\eta[J,A].
\]
Thus the new norm is conserved precisely when $[J,A]=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $A^*\eta=-\eta A$ and factor $\eta$.
\end{proof}
For example,
\[
 \eta=J=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad W=I
\]
preserves the indefinite form but not $W$; its eigenvalues are $1$ and
$-1$. Choosing $W$ does not remove its growing solution. This matrix is
a control on an inference, not a model of GU or of the Bar-Natan--Witten
operators. In field theory, preservation of a common operator domain
and compatibility with interactions add further requirements.
Even conserved positivity alone does not imply a lower energy bound,
as the negative oscillator already shows.

\section{Adjudication, contribution and next result}
\label{sec:verdict}

\paragraph{Established mathematics and imported construction.}
The conventional indefinite Yang--Mills action has the explicit
Gauss-compatible unbounded-energy family of \cref{prop:negative}.
Witten's specified real-$s$ Hilbert-bundle connection has a preserved
positive pairing and projective flatness; separately, pure closed-surface
Chern--Simons evolution has zero physical bulk Hamiltonian. These
statements concern distinct actions and notions of transport.

\paragraph{Interpretation of the objection.}
As a universal claim about complexification, the last paragraph of
\S3.1 is too strong and its cited source explicitly distinguishes the
relevant counterexample. As a warning about an ordinary propagating
Yang--Mills sector with an indefinite kinetic form, it identifies a real
conditional obstruction. The response does not establish that this
sector, with a surviving negative physical mode, is the GU sector.
Therefore \S3.1 does not establish the claimed GU energy/unitarity no-go.
This is a failure of an applicability proof, not a finding that the
underlying positivity concern is irrelevant.

\paragraph{Correction to the proposed rebuttal.}
The statement that complexification alone does not settle positivity
survives. A claim that citing Witten thereby defeats the intended
GU-specific objection does not survive a charitable reading. Witten's
parameter-space theorem must not be presented as a theorem about
nontrivial physical-time evolution, and the zero-Hamiltonian statement
must retain its pure closed-surface setting. Neither a reference to
Chern--Simons theory nor an auxiliary positive pairing constructs a
positive, stable GU physical sector.

\paragraph{Contribution and limits.}
The contribution combines an expository reconstruction of premises, an explicit
constrained Yang--Mills witness, separation of the two unitary notions,
and an action-specific transfer audit with the conditional native-action
research summary in \cref{sec:curved}. The quantization results belong to
Witten and to Bar-Natan--Witten; the supporting calculations here are
elementary and no novelty is claimed for them. A claim to have resolved
GU-specific positivity would be premature. No proof assistant
formalization of Witten's theory is claimed. Existing formal checks of
conditional logical implications cannot supply the physical hypotheses
or construct a quantum theory.

\paragraph{The next discriminating result.}
The selected reconstruction now has a constraint-compatible quadratic
energy obstruction; another unconstrained sign scan is unnecessary.
A proposed physical repair must specify an admissible sector and its
map from the native fields, explain why the negative family is excluded,
and prove compatibility with the remaining equations, interactions and
observed energy on one domain. The conjugation-fixed real sector is a
consistent candidate with an explicit pure-base Hessian and a surviving
metric-acceleration block. Its transported boundary constraints must decide
whether the conditional real-sector energy obstruction applies. Completing
that reduction would test a proposed restriction; it would not remove the
obstruction in the unrestricted action. Neither a positive pairing alone
nor a group-theoretic designation supplies the missing energy theorem.
