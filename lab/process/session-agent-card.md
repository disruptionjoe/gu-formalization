## Hazards that actually fired (one line each)

- **Imported formulas:** verify against the citation's e-print LaTeX, never rendered HTML (a rendered fetch
  hallucinated a swapped equation). Ask: *could my cross-checks even detect a transcription error?* — the
  Stelle swap survived four weeks because both its checks were swap-symmetric.
- **Silent Hermitization:** `0.5*(B+B^dag)` before eigencounting manufactures integer signatures — check the
  anti-Hermitian fraction first (`ghost_parity_krein.py:76` case).
- **Scramble vacuity:** before reading any scramble/deformation result, confirm the target subspace actually
  moves (`||P' - P0||`) — the 192 is pointwise FIXED under every internal-gamma scramble.
- **Horn tags:** `(9,5)` results are conditional on the OPEN `SIGNATURE-AMBIENT` fork; `J_quat` does not
  exist on `(7,7)`. Say which horn where the headline lands.
- **Undisclosed normalization:** spectral-radius-self-normalized deviations read as raw ones; disclose.
- **Status lines:** after a hostile verify, the judge's licensed-strength sentence is usually the best
  status line — adopting it verbatim with a `licensed-by:` pointer beats re-authoring. The strength palette
  the repo already uses: PROVED / COMPUTED / CONDITIONAL / LOCATED / ASSERTED.
- **Machine tokens are citations:** fingerprint/verdict strings should encode licensed strength, not
  headline strength (the `w_equals_mirror` case).
- **Touching an old certificate?** Adding a firing control in its guarded error class is cheap and catches
  real ones (the rational-triviality `CP^1` control is the exemplar; the Theorem-3 sympy certificate could
  not fail on the hypothesis it dropped).
