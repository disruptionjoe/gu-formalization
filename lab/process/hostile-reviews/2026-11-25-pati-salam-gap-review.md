## 2. Flux exhaustiveness and possible hidden projective blocks

The exact-sequence argument is valid: commuting holonomies in the quotient lift to triples whose pair commutators lie in the **same** diagonal Z2. An independent SU4 Z4 flux, or a weak-factor flux uncorrelated with the SU4 sign, is not in this bundle category.

An alternating 3-by-3 form over F2 is either zero or rank two. Every nonzero form can be reduced to one anticommuting pair plus a radical generator. Elementary basis changes and permutations over F2 lift to unimodular integer operations on torus generators. Although this may change the rectangular presentation of the metric, it preserves the subgroup generated by holonomies, hence the common fixed algebra and the zero-eigenvalue conclusion.

I challenged the possibility of an overlooked four-dimensional irreducible projective representation. It is excluded by the central normal operators `U1^2`, `U2^2`, `U3`. Their simultaneous spectral subspaces are invariant. After rescaling the first two operators on one such subspace, the generated algebra is the complex Clifford algebra on two anticommuting involutions, which is M2(C). Any irreducible block has dimension two; reducible representations split completely because the operators are unitary. This also handles non-root-of-unity scalar phases. There is no missing continuous family of higher-dimensional irreducibles.

For a two-dimensional weak factor there is one irreducible block and no traceless compact commutant. For SU4 the multiplicities sum to two, so either two inequivalent blocks give `u1+u1` before trace removal, or a repeated block gives `u2`. The scalar trace equation removes precisely one real dimension. Thus the SU4 fixed dimensions are one and three, respectively, and the weak factors add zero. Both are realized by the supplied Pauli controls, including the determinant-one conditions. This is a complete multiplicity argument rather than an inference from examples.

For zero flux, simultaneous unitary diagonalization in each SU factor gives a common torus of total rank five. The supplied simple-spectrum diagonal control reaches this bound. The text correctly avoids generalizing the toral assertion to arbitrary simply connected compact groups.

## 3. Physical Hessian and the strongest counterconstruction

For the inherited positive curvature-square energy at a flat connection, the quadratic form is `||d_A a||^2`. On the covariant Coulomb slice it is the covariant Hodge Laplacian. Compact flat T3, a unitary flat adjoint local system and the declared H2 domain give a nonnegative operator with compact resolvent. Decomposing the commuting adjoint holonomies into characters shows that only the trivial characters contribute harmonic one-forms. Hence the linearized kernel is `h tensor H1(T3;R)` with real dimension `3 dim(h)`.

A nonzero parallel H produces `H dx^j`, which is orthogonal to every infinitesimal gauge derivative on the compact torus. It cannot be exact: an exact harmonic form has zero norm by integration by parts. Moreover, this single-generator deformation remains flat at finite small amplitude. Changes of its Wilson-line conjugacy class cannot all be removed by continuous or large gauge transformations. Therefore an actual neutral zero direction survives; this is stronger than a gauge-fixing artifact.

**Material issue found and corrected:** for `h=su2`, the residual constant stabilizer is continuous. General triples of harmonic coefficients need not commute, can be obstructed from remaining flat and can acquire quartic energy. The full gauge quotient is singular, so its smooth dimension is not `3 dim(h)`. The revised §4 states this explicitly. It preserves the correct Hessian count and the genuine one-H flat deformation without claiming all quadratic zero directions are exact moduli. The same distinction should remain attached to future summaries.

This theorem rules out a strictly positive **full classical quadratic** gap throughout the declared flat PS category. It does not rule out a charged-sector gap, an interacting quantum gap, a nonflat condensate, or another physical action/domain. The text preserves each exit and does not mistake a neutral gauge mode for a phenomenological failure.
