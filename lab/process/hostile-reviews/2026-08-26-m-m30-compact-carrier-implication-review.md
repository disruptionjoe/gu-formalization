## The strongest supporting case

a fixed `(6,4)` fibre,

\[
(3,1)+(6,4)=(9,5),\qquad (1,3)+(6,4)=(7,7).
\]

So JP2 legitimately says that, inside that frozen product model, the base-sign
choice and ambient-signature choice are not independent planning parameters.

`GL(4,R)/O(3,1) -> RP^3` uses the indefinite metric orbit, while the positive-
definite metric cone is contractible. If the carrier's four-plane were already
proved to be the very same metric-orbit object, a compact Riemannian horn would
indeed fail to host that spine.

## The hostile case

That last bridge is absent, and later corrections make the omission decisive.

1. **Different typed objects.** The signature of a point
   (g\in\operatorname{Met}(X)), the signature of the DeWitt metric on the
   metric fibre, and the signature of a chosen four-dimensional summand in an
   ambient Clifford module are three distinct data. The cited files establish
   no map identifying the compact carrier's `(4,0)` summand with the metric
   orbit whose indefinite component supplies the `RP^3` spine.
2. **The fixed-fibre assumption is not convention-neutral.** The later
   signature-equivariance review reproduced the arithmetic but showed that
   `g -> -g` moves between two `GL(4,R)` orbits while the filed fibre form stays
   fixed. It rejected the proposed retyping and recorded a source/repository
   sign-convention mismatch. JP2 remains usable only as conditional planning
   arithmetic; it cannot carry JP1's physical exclusion by itself.
3. **The count edge is conditional.** The true 13-dimensional link still
   requires closure, stable framing and a class construction. Its possible
   receptacle cannot retroactively identify two upstream quadratic modules.
4. **A direct countermodel to the implication exists at the type level.** One
   may specify a compact positive four-plane in an ambient representation
   comparator and, separately, a Lorentzian metric-family object with the
   indefinite `O(3,1)` orbit. That pair may or may not be physically coherent,
   but it satisfies every currently stated type declaration and violates
   JP1's claimed logical implication. Ruling it out requires an exhibited
   source/action-owned identification, not the shared word “base.”
