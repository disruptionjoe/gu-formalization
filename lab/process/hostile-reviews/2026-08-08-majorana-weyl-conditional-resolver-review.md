# Hostile review: the Majorana-Weyl conditional does not yet resolve the ambient signature

## Verdict

**`CANDIDATE_FALSIFIED`.** The exact implication survives:

```text
fibre inertia = (6,4)
AND the ambient matter carrier is required to be ordinary real Majorana-Weyl
IMPLIES ambient signature = (7,7), base signature = (1,3).
```

That implication is not yet a resolver for `SIGNATURE-AMBIENT`. The receipts do
not independently establish either load-bearing selection premise, and the
downstream Kramers claim is typed to a realized literal index that GU has not
constructed. The arithmetic fact is retained; its proposed disposition is
rejected. `SETTLED` was unavailable and no row, canon verdict, or public posture
changes here.

## Attack A1 — row collapse fires

`layer0_semantics`: `SIGNATURE-AMBIENT` asks which ambient horn the construction
selects. `REAL-CLIFFORD-FORM` asks which real Clifford algebra the released
source uses. The registry explicitly keeps them distinct and says the latter is
settled only as the **author-asserted conditional reconstruction burden**.

The MW certificate says the reality requirement is independently motivated by
the draft's printed real multiplicities. Within the allowed receipts, however,
those multiplicities are evidence that the author wrote a K77 carrier; they do
not establish a selection principle that forces an otherwise open ambient
geometry to admit ordinary real MW spinors. The source reinspection likewise
records authorial K77 usage and calls the push-up to `Y` open. It does not derive
“the ambient matter carrier must be MW” independently of the source's K77
choice.

Therefore using `REAL-CLIFFORD-FORM` to supply the reality premise would import
one row's verdict into the other. The mod-eight implication remains correct,
but it cannot serve as an independent resolver until the MW requirement is
owned without assuming the horn it is meant to choose.

## Attack A2 — “fibre pinned” outruns the certificate

`tests/signature_fork_equivariance_defect.py` fixes
`LAM = 0.5` (“trace-reversal coefficient, as filed”). It proves that, **at that
fixed input**, `G(-g)=G(g)` and that the chosen form has inertia `(6,4)` on both
base orbits. Base-sign invariance is not a derivation of `lambda`, the trace-line
sign, or the decision to use that member of the DeWitt family.

The same receipt explicitly calls TT positivity an imported physical input.
Curt's transcript calls the trace contribution either `(1,0)` or `(0,1)`
“depending on a choice”; Weinstein's “proper sign” supplies a Pati-Salam
criterion, not a derivation from the GU action. The exact value `lambda=1/2`
need not itself be unique for the inertia argument, but these receipts neither
classify the admissible coefficient range nor derive the load-bearing trace
sign.

So the candidate has moved a convention one level upstream: after choosing the
DeWitt member/sign, MW chooses the base horn. That is useful conditional
geometry, but it does not make the base a consequence of GU alone.

## Attack A3 — the parity consequence is mistyped without an observable

`canon/no-go-quaternionic-parity-generation-sector.md` proves an evenness
theorem for a GU-native Hermitian carrier commuting with the quaternionic
antiunitary structure, under the **literal-index reading**. The same canon file
states that the larger generation count remains under-determined: an
`index/2` reading can reach three while leaving the rank free, and an external
non-quaternionic carrier can evade the wall.

No listed receipt constructs the GU operator whose index is the observed family
count. Therefore “`(9,5)` forbids three generations” overstates the theorem. The
supported statement is narrower: `(9,5)` forbids an odd literal index for the
specified J-commuting Hermitian operator class. Conversely, `(7,7)` merely
removes this Kramers obstruction; it does not construct or force three.

This is exactly the invalidation clause already carried by
`PD-SIGNATURE-PARITY`: if generation count is not the index whose parity is
constrained, the downstream link does not fire.

## Attack A4 — requirement versus preference

A quaternionic K95 carrier is mathematically available, and the MW certificate
itself says the MW condition is “a requirement GU adopts rather than a
theorem.” The Curt receipt says a `Spin(5,9)` bundle can also match what is
required for the Standard Model, while the authorial surfaces assert K77 but do
not derive the MW requirement from a horn-neutral construction.

On the allowed evidence, ordinary real MW is therefore a conditional design
requirement or authorial preference. That is sufficient for a conditional
lookup theorem, not for an independent fork resolver.

## Attack A5 — prior art

`prior_art`: the quaternionic parity theorem already contains the K77

## Analytic lens

Kramers evenness is an operator theorem: it needs the relevant Hermitian
operator, its antiunitary symmetry and the domain on which the index/kernel is
defined. The canon receipt establishes that theorem for its declared
quaternionic class, but the candidate supplies no generation operator or
physical index map. Absence of Kramers doubling on K77 is absence of one
obstruction, not an existence or uniqueness theorem.

The finite DeWitt-block positivity check also is not a proof of positive energy,
reflection positivity, a self-adjoint physical operator, or a closed domain.
None of those analytic notions may be used to upgrade the fibre choice here.

## Symplectic lens

The DeWitt form is kinetic data on metric variations. Positivity of the
five-dimensional spatial traceless block after fixing the coefficient and
splitting lapse/shift is a useful physical control, but it is not yet the
reduced covariant phase-space form derived from the selected GU action. No
constraint quotient, presymplectic reduction, boundary condition, or action
Hessian in the listed receipts selects `lambda=1/2` or the trace sign.

Thus the TT test can reject a sign under an imported criterion; it cannot turn
that criterion into an internally derived GU pinning.

## What settlement would still require, and its cost

At minimum:

1. derive or explicitly assign ownership to the DeWitt trace-sign/inertia
   selection from the GU action/source datum, rather than from a fixed
   `lambda=1/2` fixture plus imported TT preference;
2. derive an ordinary real MW carrier requirement from a horn-neutral GU
   construction or observation map, independently of the source's prior K77
   assertion;
3. construct the actual GU generation observable/operator and prove its index is
   the observed family count in the Kramers theorem's domain;
4. prove the needed branch-native analytic and reduced phase-space properties;
5. then run a separate deliberate settlement and triage the 29 canon surfaces
   plus the historical fork declarations, including the currently unknown id.

That is at least three scientific gates (fibre/action selection,
carrier/observation selection, and generation-index realization) followed by a
separate propagation/migration act. It is not one more arithmetic certificate.

## Dissent / strongest defense of the candidate

If `named_resolver` is defined weakly as “a condition which, when supplied,
selects a horn,” the candidate qualifies: the fixed-fibre plus MW condition
does exactly that. But under that definition almost any unowned binary
preference is a resolver, and the registry ceases to distinguish a discovery
mechanism from relabeling the fork as an input. This review uses the stronger,
program-useful standard: the condition must be independently motivated and
owned, and its claimed physics consequence must have an object in the theorem's
domain.
