# Hostile review

## One convenient excess-four partition mistaken for exhaustion attack

The probe exhausts every ordinary partition through multiplicity seven and
finds `[n-2,2]` as the only GL/U primitive excess-four partition. It separately
includes loxodromic realification, two independent excess-two collisions,
orthogonal zero-primary excess four, and zero-plus-nonzero excess two. These
are the complete primitive and additive sources of total excess four.

## Loxodromic centralizer counted at complex rather than real dimension attack

The loxodromic primary is a realification. Its complex excess-two
`[n-1,1]` partition contributes real excess four. The census includes `15`
such rows and exact signed grading controls for chain sizes one and two.

## Collision pairs ordered or overlapping attack

The two nonzero excess-two descriptors are an unordered multiset. A capacity
check requires disjoint block occurrences, including four singleton blocks
for two `[1,1]` collisions. The two primary parameters remain distinct; making
them equal would create a higher-excess partition outside this layer.

## Semisimple zero row mislabeled mixed attack

The four-dimensional `[1,1,1,1]` zero primary has no nilpotent part. An
initial scope audit identified this seam. The final census includes such a row
only when the nonzero complement already contains a nontrivial Jordan block.
Exactly `24` fully semisimple rows are excluded as K88 coverage.

## Imaginary sign characteristics erased attack

The primitive imaginary count retains both chain signs independently, and the
double-collision count retains every compatible pair. Coarse form signature
never replaces the sign-characteristic data.

## Nonoptimal zero gradings suppressed attack

All `621` signed zero-primary grading allocations are tested. Only `237` have
fixed centralizer dimension two; all `384` adverse allocations remain in the
control count. Every signed structural row has at least one optimal choice,
and the global complement search independently requires balanced signature.

## Parameter equality invalidates anticommutation attack

The reversing identity is checked on the assembled matrices after the selected
Jordan chains receive equal spectral parameters. Every one of the `673`
representatives still satisfies `RX+XR=0`, `X^TQ+QX=0`, orthogonality and the
balanced restricted signature.

## Finite-field rank mistaken for an upper bound attack

Finite-field row reduction supplies lower bounds only. The exact primary
centralizer formulas fix ambient kernel dimension `11`, while the explicit
fixed-centralizer computations give dimension `2`. Those characteristic-zero
upper bounds match the computed ranks, forcing ambient/fixed/moving ranks
`80/40/40` and moment rank `89`.

## Two passing layers mistaken for a uniform theorem attack

K91 and K92 support the half-excess fixed-centralizer pattern, but do not prove
it for higher partitions and collision combinations. Centralizer dimension at
least `13` remains open, as do zero-neighborhood coverage, surjectivity and
RSAP.

## Strongest contrary construction

The `384` nonoptimal zero-primary gradings are exact contrary allocations:
they place too much centralizer in the fixed subalgebra. They do not obstruct
membership because the same signed orbit admits an optimal balanced grading,
but they prevent any claim that an arbitrary reversing involution is sharp.

## Weakest reproducibility seam

Completeness depends on the canonical real skew-adjoint primary grammar and
the direct-sum centralizer theorem, not on enumerating continuous spectral
orbits. Reproduction should therefore attack the partition-excess inventory,
parameter-group capacity checks, and signed zero-primary enumeration before
trusting the aggregate count `673`.

## Verdict

Accept complete centralizer-eleven mixed structural coverage and sharp map
rank `89`. Advance to centralizer dimension `13` while testing—but not
assuming—a uniform fixed-centralizer parity theorem. Do not claim a zero
neighborhood, surjectivity, RSAP, source selection or physical consequence.
