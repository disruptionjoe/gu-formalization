### 0.2 Five problem-matched lenses, run inline

**L1 — Protocol design.** A protocol is only as good as its trigger. Four of
these five concerns propose a *policy*; policies without a firing condition are
the dropped-commitments class AR-1 measured. Before proposing any protocol,
ask what fires it and who reads the firing. This lens is why every proposal
below is written as an activation condition with an owner, and why item 15's
proposal defines conflict narrowly rather than as "disagreement".

**L2 — Release engineering.** "Thundering herd" is a load metaphor, and load
metaphors import assumptions about *simultaneity* and *automaticity*. Neither
transfers for free: a ledger row does not discharge itself when a condition
flips, because a human-authored mint moves it and records its grant. Before
accepting the metaphor, check whether the discharge is automatic. It is not,
and that finding is what dissolves item 14.

**L3 — Consensus / reconciliation.** Split-brain requires two authorities
answering the SAME question. The first job is not to write an arbitration rule
but to check whether the question is one question. SG-1's layer finding says it
is two. This lens converted item 15 from "who wins" to "what is the exact
proposition on which they could disagree" — a much smaller and much more
useful object.

**L4 — Register governance.** Registers fail in two ways: rot (entries go
stale) and *non-enrolment* (the object was never entered). The repository has
strong anti-rot machinery — gates, required-id pins, next_check dates. It has
almost nothing against non-enrolment, because non-enrolment is invisible to a
gate that iterates over entries. SA-1 measured exactly this failure one day
ago. This lens is what found item 20's real content.

**L5 — Adversarial reading (the brief's named attack).** *Are 15/19/20 solving
problems that cannot occur under existing serialization and mint discipline?*
Run first, not last, and it lands partially on all three: the mint's
grant-recording rule, the `OVER_DETERMINED` four-disposition vocabulary, and
the three fallback reason kinds each already cover a large share. The residue
after the attack is what each card reports, and in item 19's case the attack
removed the main clause entirely. Where the attack does NOT reach: mint
discipline governs the FORM of a change, never its INDEPENDENCE.

## 2. CARD 14 — thundering herd

> *"If bit 2 resolves, a dozen conditional rows discharge simultaneously. No
> staged-mint plan exists for a mass discharge event."*

**(i) Strongest reconstruction.** Bit 2 is named as the selector across the
tree. A single resolution would make a large set of conditional rows
dischargeable at once, forcing one mint to adjudicate a dozen retypes in a
single pass — exactly the batch-pressure condition under which adjudication
quality degrades and errors enter the ledger together.

**(ii) What the repo holds — THE COUNT, exact.**

The repository defines the bit-2 condition family itself, as Grant-poset node
G6 in `lab/methods/gu-base-categories.md` §2, verbatim:

> `G6 | {SC-CHI-01 VEV if} | ... bound by ST-1 to SG4 bit 2. In v0.259 this set
> occurs on the DEMAND side: NEEDS rows blocked on the vacuum/phase selection
> (LT-GR2d, RA-G3, AC-F1)`

**THREE ROWS. Not a dozen.** All three are `NEEDS / MISSING_CONSTRUCTION`, out
of 87 rows in v0.259.

A deliberately loose second count — every row whose `revival_trigger` string
mentions VEV, vacuum, or chiral — yields **eleven**: `AC-A1, AC-F1, RA-A1,
RA-A2, RA-A4, RA-A8, RA-D2, RA-D4, RA-E1, RA-E6, RA-G3`. Still not twelve, and
this is not the bit-2 family: it is the vacuum-adjacent family, and the same
methods file records G3-vs-G6 as **deliberately incomparable** — *"no ledger
record identifies the two condition families. They are kept incomparable
here."* Merging them to reach "a dozen" would be the exact identification the
repository refuses to make.

**(iii) The dig — the concern fails at its premise.** Over all 87 rows, the
number discharged by a bit-2 resolution **alone** is **ZERO**. Computed, not
asserted: a row is bit-2-alone-dischargeable iff every content word of its
trigger's head demand lies in a closed phase-object vocabulary. Three planted
controls (`"the selected varpi VEV phase"`, `"a resolved SG4 bit 2"`, `"the
decoupling phase selection"`) all fire under the same evaluator, so the zero is
a measurement rather than a blind detector. Look at what the three G6 rows
actually demand:

| row | `revival_trigger` |
|---|---|
| `LT-GR2d` | "a native or explicitly external normalized observer functional inserted into the selected action with an exact zero-mode Ward/constraint derivation" |
| `RA-G3` | "a stationary odd-form VEV in the located 126 channel" |
| `AC-F1` | "a physical chiral carrier with a derived index" |

Every one demands a **construction**. Bit 2 — which phase is realized — is at
most a conjunct of each. **The causality in the concern runs backwards:** bit 2
does not discharge these rows; the construction that discharges them (a built,
stationary source-action vacuum) is what would resolve bit 2, as a by-product.
There is no reachable world-state in which "bit 2 resolves" arrives *before*
the constructions and triggers a herd.

And the staged-mint plan said not to exist ran the day before the concern was
raised. IM-1 minted v0.258 → v0.259 with **thirteen** row changes in one pass,
including a three-row simultaneous conditional cascade (`AC-A1`
`NEEDS/MISSING_CONSTRUCTION` → `SAME/DERIVED_CONDITIONAL` carrying
`GRANT-ACA1-C1`, with `AC-A2`/`AC-A3` following), under: a mint-record table
naming the authorizing artifact for **every** change; three written
adjudications with grounds and reversal conditions; a pre-read of every
tripwire's firing condition *before* any edit; and the standing rule in
`migration_policy` — *"DERIVED_CONDITIONAL -> DERIVED is forbidden; every
status move records its grant."* A mass discharge is what the mint **is**.

**(iv) Verdict: DISSOLVES**, on two independent grounds, either sufficient.
(a) The family is three rows, not a dozen, and zero of them fires on the bit
alone. (b) The staging mechanism exists, is gated, and has a dated receipt
handling a larger simultaneous batch.

**(v) Register item.** None proposed. One honest residual, recorded not
proposed: G3-vs-G6 is deliberately incomparable, so a future receipt
identifying the two families would grow the bit-2 family and stale this count.
The probe pins the count, so the staleness is detectable rather than silent.

**(vi) Fidelity delta.** "A dozen" is wrong by 4× against the exact family (3)
and by one against the loosest defensible reading (11) — and that loose reading
is the merge the repository explicitly refuses. "No staged-mint plan exists" is
false; IM-1 is the plan, dated one day earlier. What survives from the
one-liner is a question it did not ask: nothing prevents a future artifact from
*claiming* bit 2 resolved without the construction. That is card 20's problem,
and it is the real one.

## 3. CARD 15 — split-brain

> *"Lane 1 (action-side) and a source-side reading could resolve bit 2
> differently. No reconciliation protocol is declared for conflicting
> resolutions of the one selector everything routes to."*

**(i) Strongest reconstruction.** Two surfaces can pronounce on bit 2: Lane 1,
the action/BFV lane, by constructing the vacuum; and the source, by its own
decoupling statement. Nothing declares which wins on disagreement about the one
object the whole tree routes to — so the tree could silently carry two
incompatible values of its own selector.

**(ii) What the repo holds.**

- The action side is explicitly owned. ST-1 §5 V1: *"**FACT ABOUT THE SELECTED
  VACUUM** = SG4 bit 2 ... Owned by Lane 1 (the action/BFV lane). Not decided
  here, by design."*
- The source side is explicitly hedged at its register locus. `SC-CHI-01`,
  polarity `ASSERTS`, notes: *"Conditioned in the same passage on no VEV
  'pulling the various sub-fields of varpi to values significantly above zero',
  and introduced as 'The idea being explored here' - hedged strength."*
- A reconciliation vocabulary **exists and is in live use**: the
  `OVER_DETERMINED` verdict family with four allowed dispositions
  (`GENUINE_FALSIFICATION` / `FORK_ARTIFACT` / `SCOPE_ERROR` /
  `STALE_PREMISE`), and six `over_determined_escalations` entries in v0.259,
  each with a named owner and a status — two already adjudicated (`LT-GR1b`,
  `LT-SM3b`), four `ESCALATED_PENDING_DISPOSITION`.

**(iii) The dig — most of the stated conflict dissolves under card 13's fix.**
SG-1's finding is that bit 2 is a **phase** bit: a Layer-2 question about which
vacuum is dynamically realized. The source cannot answer a phase question,
because answering it requires the unbuilt action; and the declaration-content
question the source *can* answer is a different question, now re-routed by the
applied diff. So "Lane 1 and a source-side reading resolve bit 2 differently"
is, post-fix, largely a category error — they are not both resolving bit 2.

What survives is narrower and real. `SC-CHI-01` does not merely *read*; it
states a **condition on the phase** — no VEV pulling the sub-fields of `varpi`
significantly above zero. If Lane 1 exhibits a stationary vacuum with
`varpi`-VEV significantly above zero, the source's stated condition and the
constructed vacuum disagree about the same object. That is a genuine
over-determination, it has exactly the shape the escalation surface was built
for — and **bit 2 has no escalation entry, because it has no ledger row of its
own.** The machinery exists; the selector is not plugged into it.

**(iv) Verdict: LIVE-MODERATE**, and PARTIALLY **ALREADY-COVERED** — the
four-disposition adjudication vocabulary lives at
`lab/process/conditional-physics-ledger-v0.259.json` `over_determined_escalations`
and `taxonomy.verdict_kinds.OVER_DETERMINED`. Live only as the missing entry
and the missing conflict definition.

**(v) PROPOSED — reconciliation protocol (proposal only, not applied).**

```
id: SG4-BIT-2-RECONCILIATION   owner: SG4 axis, referred to the integration owner
1. Exactly two surfaces may pronounce on bit 2: Lane 1 (a constructed stationary
   vacuum) and SC-CHI-01 (the source's stated condition). No third surface.
2. Post-SG-1 they answer DIFFERENT questions: Lane 1 answers which phase is
   realized; SC-CHI-01 states a condition ON that phase. Neither overrides the
   other by rank, because neither is answering the other's question.
3. CONFLICT is defined narrowly and only as this: Lane 1 exhibits a stationary
   vacuum with varpi-VEV significantly above zero. Prose disagreement, differing
   emphasis, and differing hedge strength are NOT conflict.
4. On conflict: file an over_determined_escalations entry, owner
   sg4-bit-2-selector, restricted to the four standing dispositions.
5. Until adjudicated, no consumer may cite bit 2 as resolved.
```

**(vi) Fidelity delta.** The one-liner assumes both sides answer the same
question; SG-1's layer finding says they do not, and the README now says so.
It also says "no reconciliation protocol is declared" — the *vocabulary* is
declared, gated, and carries six live entries; what is missing is an entry for
this selector and a definition of what counts as conflict. Sharpened, the
concern is not "no protocol" but "the protocol exists and the highest-fan-out
object is not enrolled in it" — the same shape as card 20, and the same shape
as SA-1's dated entry-gap failure.

## 4. CARD 19 — no fallback typing

> *"If bit 2 is permanently SOURCE-SILENT, every routed row needs an
> `EXTERNAL_DATUM` retype. That contingency has no owner and no trigger."*

**(i) Strongest reconstruction.** The routed rows sit as
`MISSING_CONSTRUCTION`, which asserts a construction is owed. If the source is
permanently silent on the phase selection and no construction can extract it,
that type is a standing false promise: the rows imply reachable work that is
not reachable. Nobody owns noticing, and nothing fires the retype.

**(ii) What the repo holds.**

- The retype target **exists**. `EXTERNAL_DATUM` is a live `NEEDS` reason kind
  in the v0.259 taxonomy, currently carried by two rows (`RA-F1`, `AC-F5`). So
  is `PROVEN_UNSUPPLYABLE`.
- A **strictly better** one exists, minted **2026-08-17** — one day before this
  concern was raised: `SOURCE_DECLARED_OPEN__NO_MECHANISM_SUPPLIED`, defined as
  *"The row's head demand is an object the source names as an open problem and
  explicitly declines to supply a mechanism for ... AND the repository's only
  named construction route is register-tracked as blocked"*,
  `strictly_more_indebting_than: MISSING_CONSTRUCTION`, with a non-discharge
  rule: *"A row carrying this kind stays in NEEDS, stays counted in the
  denominator, stays in next_work_queue, and is NOT discharged, softened,
  deferred or excused. Every instance MUST carry a debt_note."*
- `taxonomy.unknown_kind_rule: NEW_KIND_REQUIRED__FORCED_FIT_FORBIDDEN` already
  forbids cramming a source-silent row into an ill-fitting existing kind.
- The contingency **shape** exists: `lab/process/upgrade-program-register.yaml`
  — schema `{id, title, origin, owner, status, activation, next_check}`, status
  vocabulary `[QUEUED, ACTIVE, DONE, DECLINED]`, gated by
  `process_gates/upgrade_program_register_audit.py`, built because *"queue it as
  a rider on the next touch is the dropped-commitments class AR-1 measured"* —
  and it already carries an IV-class activation shape.

**(iii) The dig — the concern names the wrong kind, and its main clause is
covered.** `EXTERNAL_DATUM` is the **wrong** fallback for a permanently
source-silent selector. An external datum is a number you go and measure: it is
a *discharge route*. A source that is silent plus a construction route that is
blocked is precisely `SOURCE_DECLARED_OPEN__NO_MECHANISM_SUPPLIED`, whose whole
design is that it opens no discharge route and is strictly more indebting.
**Retyping to `EXTERNAL_DATUM` on permanent source-silence would be a softening
dressed as an honesty move** — the ledger would look more honest while
acquiring a discharge route that does not exist.

What is genuinely uncovered is the smaller half, and the one-liner names it
correctly: no register item watches bit 2, and no stated condition fires the
retype. The register is the declared home for exactly this, and bit 2 is not
in it.

**(iv) Verdict: LIVE-MODERATE**, with the main clause **ALREADY-COVERED** at
`lab/process/conditional-physics-ledger-v0.259.json`
`taxonomy.verdict_kinds.NEEDS` + `taxonomy_extensions`. Live only as owner and
trigger.

**(v) PROPOSED — fallback trigger, as an upgrade-register item (proposal only).**


**(vi) Fidelity delta.** Three corrections and one confirmation. The proposed

> *"Bit 2 is now so load-bearing that any artifact claiming to resolve it would

> review before such a claim integrates. A critical selector deserves a
> critical-selector protection."*

**(i) Strongest reconstruction.** An artifact claiming to settle bit 2 would
propagate immediately: the README vocabulary block, three ledger rows, the
`L4` / `LA3` / `N1` / `N3` entries of the base-category reference, PCX-1's armed
firing condition, CR-B, CS-1, ST-1. No rule requires independent review before
that claim integrates, so a single agent's single pass could move the tree's
most load-bearing object.

**(ii) What the repo holds — and this is the card that lands.**

- **The mechanism exists, and bit 2 is not in it.**
  `lab/process/layer0-fork-registry.yaml` carries exactly **13** Layer-0 forks
  (10 open, 3 settled): `CARRIER-SPLIT`, `SIGNATURE-AMBIENT`,
  `REAL-CLIFFORD-FORM`, `IMPOSTER-LABEL-AB`, `TWO-IN-REPO-2PLUS1`,
  `KINEMATIC-VS-PHYSICAL-CARRIER`, `EPS-CHARACTER`, `SECTION-VS-OBSERVERSE`,
  `J-RED-OWNERSHIP`, `VERTICAL-FROBENIUS-TRACE`, `GENERATION-COUNT-CODOMAIN`,
  `NUMBER-14-METRIC-VS-EXTERIOR`, `SOLDERED-AD`. **None is the
  chirality-content fork or SG4 bit 2.** Verified by a scanner whose power is
  demonstrated on a planted `SG4-BIT-2` entry, so the absence is a measurement.
- **The registry's settled-row schema IS a critical-selector protection**:
  `settled_side`, `settled_how`, `settled_at`, `settled_by` (non-empty,
  repo-relative paths that must resolve); plus `fork_depth_audit.py`'s
  `REQUIRED_FORK_IDS` (6 ids) so a row cannot be quietly deleted; plus
  `fork_stack_threshold: 3`, which forces an explicit
  `fork_stack_acknowledged:` with a stated reason once dispositions stack.
- **The precedent is dated, receipted, and in this same tree, one day earlier.**
  SA-1 §8: *"The mechanism of the failure is the one the Layer-0 fork registry
  was built to catch, and the fork was never entered in it."* `SOLDERED-AD` was
  declared 2026-08-14, ranked the most-flagged open object across seven
  surfaces, promoted verdict-load-bearing — and entered only at IM-1 on
  2026-08-17.
- **An IV-class pass exists as a named precedent**: `IV-20260815`, cited in
  `VERIFICATION.md` as the review that caught three archaeology probes able to
  accept a red baseline. `upgrade-program-register.yaml` already carries
  `activation: any independent review pass (IV-class)`.
- **The elevated bar already exists for one destination only.** AGENTS.md:
  *"New findings do not promote themselves to `canon/`. A later independent
  verification must check both the result and proposed wording."* Bit 2's
  consumers are not canon — they are the tree README, the ledger, and the
  base-category reference. None is covered.
- **PCX-1 supplies the reusable tripwire shape**: a typed, armed
  `FIRING CONDITION` naming exactly the receipt that would fire it, with the
  probe's contrary controls being precisely the configurations in which the
  same evaluator fires.

**(iii) The dig, hostile direction first.** *Is this already solved by mint and
serialization discipline?* Partly, and the parts are real. Mint discipline means
a bit-2 resolution cannot reach the ledger without a mint-record entry naming
its authorizing artifact and an adjudication with grounds and reversal
conditions. `kill_target_claim_audit.py` forces the claiming artifact to name
its target claim; `typed_carrier_declaration_audit.py` forces it to type its
carrier with an explicit `CHIRALITY=` value from CN-2's closed vocabulary — so
a bit-2 claim cannot even be *written* in 2026-08-17+ frontmatter without
declaring which chirality reading it means. That is genuine protection and it
should be credited.

But **none of it is a bar**. Every one of those governs the *form* of a claim,
not its *independence*. An artifact can satisfy all of them and still be one
agent's one pass on the highest-fan-out open object in the tree — which is
precisely the SG4-bit-2 shape, and precisely what SA-1 measured going wrong.

Second finding, sharper, and the one I did not expect. The fork registry is not
merely an unused option here. Its **depth counter is supposed to be counting**
how many dispositions have stacked on this open fork. Because bit 2 is not
registered, the counter reads **zero**, and `fork_stack_threshold: 3` has never
fired — while at least ten joe-directed files route to it. The gate built to
catch exactly this failure is blind to the object that most needs it, and has
been for the life of the tree.

**(iv) Verdict: LIVE-HIGH.** The highest-value item of my five.

**(v) PROPOSED — elevated bar (proposal only; `lab/process/` untouched).**

```
1. Enter in lab/process/layer0-fork-registry.yaml:
     - id: SG4-BIT-2-PHASE
       title: which varpi-VEV phase the (unbuilt) source action selects
       horns: ["chiral / unbroken (decreased-VEV)", "massive / super-Higgs"]
       status: open
       fan_out: "high -- 10 joe-directed files route to it; 3 ledger rows (G6);
         base-category L4/LA3/N1/N3; PCX-1's armed firing condition"
       sources: [SC-CHI-01, ST-1 s5 V1, SG-1 s7.4, gu-base-categories.md s2 G6]
2. Add SG4-BIT-2-PHASE to fork_depth_audit.py REQUIRED_FORK_IDS, so the row
   cannot be quietly deleted and the depth counter starts reading the real stack.
3. BAR: any artifact claiming to settle it needs settled_by paths that resolve
   AND an IV-class pass on both result and wording -- AGENTS.md's canon rule,
   extended to this one fork. Failure precedent it prevents: SA-1 s8.
```

**(vi) Fidelity delta.** The one-liner is right about the risk and wrong about
the remedy's novelty. It asks for a new "critical-selector protection"; the
repository already built one — the Layer-0 fork registry, its settled-row
schema, its depth counter, its required-id pins, its gate — and simply never
enrolled this selector. It also **understates the cost**: the failure is not
only that a bad claim could integrate, but that a live gate has been silently
reporting zero stack depth on a ten-file-fan-out fork. And "IV-class independent
review" is the right shape but the wrong *sole* remedy: IV is a pass that
happens *after*; the registry entry is what makes the object visible *before*.

A row whose trigger is silent about a dependency it actually has would be

## 7. POSTFLIGHT — five lenses, after the work

**P1 — Protocol design.** Every one of the three proposals ended up shaped as
*enrolment in an existing mechanism*, not as new machinery. That was not the
plan going in; it is what the evidence forced. It is also the cheaper and more
durable outcome: a new protocol needs its own gate, while an entry in a gated
register inherits one.

**P2 — Release engineering.** The load metaphor in item 14 failed for a reason
worth carrying forward: ledger rows are not consumers of an event bus. Nothing
in this repository discharges itself. Any future concern phrased as
"X fires and N things happen automatically" should first be checked against
`migration_policy` — *"every status move records its grant"* — which makes
automatic movement impossible by construction.

**P3 — Consensus / reconciliation.** The most useful move in card 15 was
refusing to write an arbitration rule until the *proposition* was located.
"Who wins" was unanswerable and would have produced a rule nobody could fire;
"a stationary vacuum with varpi-VEV significantly above zero contradicts
SC-CHI-01's stated condition" is a single testable sentence with an owner.

**P4 — Register governance.** The finding I would keep if I kept only one:
**gates iterate over entries, so non-enrolment is invisible to every gate.**
SA-1 measured it on 2026-08-16 for SOLDERED-AD; I measured the same failure on
2026-08-17 for a bigger object. Two instances in two days is a pattern, and the
pattern has no owner. The registry cannot detect what it does not contain, and
nothing currently asks "what load-bearing forks are missing from this file?"

**P5 — Adversarial reading.** Running the hostile attack *first* changed three
of five verdicts before I wrote a word of proposal, and removed item 19's main
clause entirely. It also produced the session's one uncomfortable result: the
strongest concern (20) is strongest not because a mechanism is missing but
because an existing gate has been reporting a false zero. That is worse than a
gap, and I would not have found it by looking for gaps.
