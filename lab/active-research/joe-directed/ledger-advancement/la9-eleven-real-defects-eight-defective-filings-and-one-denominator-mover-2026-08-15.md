### 2.1 The one formal object: trigger well-formedness

For a row with verdict `V` and revival trigger `T`, `T` is **well-formed** iff

1. **satisfiable** — the set `T` names is non-empty in the row's declared scope;
2. **not already satisfied** — the program has not already supplied a member;
3. **responsive** — every member of `T`, when supplied, changes `V`;
4. **independent** — `T` is not a restatement of another row's trigger.

Negating each clause gives four failure modes. Every one of them is instantiated
in the filed batch, and the filings named three of the four while typing the
fourth incorrectly.

| mode | meaning | rows | count | who filed it |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| **∅** unsatisfiable | *void clause* — cannot fire | `RA-B1..B5`, `RA-C1` | 6 | LA-1 |
| **⊤** already satisfied | *illusory clause* — fires at will | `AC-A5` | 1 | LA-2, LA-5 |
| **NR** non-responsive | *surplusage* — fires, nothing moves | `AC-D1..D5` | 5 | LA-3 |
| **DEP** dependent | *restatement* — fireable only via another row | `AC-A4` | 1 | LA-5, **mis-typed as ∅** |

**13 of 82 active rows — 15.9% — carry a trigger that fails at least one clause.**
That number is a floor, not a census: only the filed rows were checked. A full
sweep of all 82 triggers is the obvious follow-on and is *not* claimed here.

Two things this predicate buys that the eleven separate filings did not:

- It makes `AC-A5` and `RA-B1..B5` **the same defect with opposite sign**, so one

### 2.3 Mode NR — the comparator lattice, reproduced

> **Routing.** This subsection computes the conventional 4d SM anomaly comparator.

### 4.1 Strongest overclaim in this artifact

well-formedness" is a statement about *the thirteen rows that were filed*, and a
reader will hear it as a measurement of the ledger. It is a **floor**. The other

Runner-up: calling `AC-A4` "dependent" rather than "unfireable" is a *sharpening*

### 4.2 Strongest contrary reading

**"This is a linter, and the ledger does not need one."** The case is real and it
deserves stating at full strength. Not one verdict moves. Not one grant is
supplied. Most of the batch lands in a field — `revival_trigger` — that the
ledger's own published meter never reads: `progress`, `verdict_counts` and
`coverage` are all functions of `verdict` and `axis` alone. A ledger whose
triggers were all blank would report an **identical** meter. On that reading,
thirteen rewritten strings change nothing the program publishes, and the batch is
maintenance dressed as research.

**Two answers, one weak and one not.**

The weak one: the triggers are the ledger's *falsifiability surface* — they are
what says how the program could find out it was wrong. Six rows advertise a
revival route that cannot fire and five advertise one that fires without doing
anything, so the ledger overstates its own falsifiability on 11 rows. True, and
exposed to a hostile reader — but still epistemic hygiene, not progress.

The one that actually bites: **the premise is false as of today.** LA-5 computed a
*revival incidence channel* over the `ANOMALY_CONSISTENCY` axis and got **rank 10
of 10, full** against discharge's rank 7 — the finding that the ledger "is a
better instrument than it is an actuator." That number is computed **from
`revival_trigger` strings**. So a defective trigger now corrupts a published
result, not merely a description. `AC-A5`'s `⊤` trigger and `AC-D1..D5`'s `NR`
trigger both contribute columns to that rank. The moment the ledger's triggers
became load-bearing for a measurement — which happened earlier today, in LA-5 —
the linting objection stopped holding. It held on 2026-08-14. It does not hold
now.
