### Lens P3 — measurement theory: construct validity of a one-dimensional atom

`rank` and `minimum cover` are properties of an *instrument*. An instrument
whose scale merges several constructs reports a lower dimensionality than the
domain has — that is the definition of a construct-validity failure, not a
finding about the domain. The discriminant test is simple: if `A` is one
construct, its 18 certificates should be paraphrases of one another. §2.1 shows
they are not; they partition into four supports of sizes 7 / 4 / 2 / 2 plus 3
that denote nothing open at all.

### Lens P4 — evidence law: chain of custody and polarity of a cited exhibit

A citation must assert the proposition it is cited for. The ledger marks
settled results with `EXACT`, `REJECTED`, `KILLED`, `RETRACTED`. A certificate
carrying one of those markers asserts the object **is established**, so it
cannot simultaneously witness that the row **waits on** it. This is mechanical
and I applied it before any hand judgement — see §2.1. It fires three times,
and it does **not** fire on the tight certificate `LT-SM6`, which is the
control that the test has discrimination rather than just severity.

### Lens P5 — graph theory: a cut vertex is only as real as its weakest edge

Reach through a DAG is not a measurement of the DAG's *nodes*; it is a
measurement of its *edges*. LA-4's `b9` has exactly one outgoing edge, `b9 ≺
b1`, and LA-4 states plainly that no ledger row asserts it. A single unstated
edge therefore carries 26 of the 28 rows in the headline figure. That is the
maximal concentration of evidential load on an uncited premise that this graph
can have, and it is the first thing to attack.

### Lens P6 — project-network scheduling: precedence type, not precedence existence

CPM distinguishes **finish-to-start logical precedence** (B consumes A's
deliverable) from **risk precedence** (A's negative outcome invalidates B's
premise). They imply different repairs: the first says re-order, the second
says record the assumption and keep the parallelism. LA-4's queue finding
asserts the first. §4 tests which one the queue text actually supports.
