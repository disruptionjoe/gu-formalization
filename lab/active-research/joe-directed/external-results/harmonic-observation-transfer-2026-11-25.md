Theorem1.1 concerns U(t)=exp(-it|D|), |D|=sqrt(-Delta), on Euclidean R^n spatial slices, integrated over t∈[1,2]:

    ||U(t)f||_Lp(R^n×[1,2]) <= C ||f||_W^{s_+(p)+epsilon,p}(R^n),
    s_+(p)=max(0,(n-1)/2 - n/p), n>=2, 2<=p<infinity.

At p=2, epsilon=0 is allowed: this is the L2 unitary bound, not a positive Sobolev smoothing gain. For p>=2n/(n-1), its gain relative to the *fixed-time Lp loss* is every amount below1/p. It is not a uniform gain1/p in an arbitrary ambient L2 graph norm.

The paper claims Kakeya/Nikodym maximal results and Fourier extension estimates for sphere, compact paraboloid and truncated cone in strict ranges. “Fourier restriction” restricts a Fourier transform to a frequency surface (equivalently estimates an extension operator). GU's observation restricts an ambient field to a physical-space section and contracts tensor components. The shared word does not identify the two maps.

**AppendixE must not be omitted.** TheoremE.1 extends the annular estimate to a patch of a simple extremal eigenvalue of a **constant real-symmetric n×n pencil in n spatial dimensions**, with definite transverse Hessian; equality of state and spatial dimensions is used explicitly in its proof. It is not a general variable-coefficient Lorentzian theorem or a theorem for arbitrary symmetric-hyperbolic systems. The family079 Lean scope page does not separately advertise formalization of AppendixE; do not claim its Lean coverage from the top-line family link.

The repo's new semisimple six-slot principal system has repeated characteristic eigenvalues and many more state components than three observed spatial dimensions. Its auxiliary positive principal symmetrizer is useful evidence, but does not make it an E.1 instance, supply a full variable-coefficient propagator, or provide the missing normal-field extension.

## 2. Derivative budget, with spaces kept explicit

### Unrestricted positive-chart trace

The Nov24 owned result supplies the exact local threshold: H^s in14 real coordinates restricts to a codimension10 section into H^(s-5) if s>5. At s=5 ordinary Sobolev trace fails as a general estimate. A first-order graph norm is not automatically H1 for a nonelliptic operator; even if H1 control were supplied, the standard trace route still needs **more than four extra normal derivatives**. For both arrows of an unrestricted first-order Sobolev complex D:H^s→H^(s-1), the ordinary sufficient trace route requires s>6, more than five beyond H1. These are thresholds for that route, not a universal necessary regularity statement for every constrained space.

### Why the advertised local-smoothing gain cannot simply be spent ten times

In an explicitly hypothetical one-time13-space flat wave comparator, let the observation plane retain time plus three spatial coordinates. Its spatial codimension is10. Composing the claimed local-smoothing estimate with ordinary W^{r,p} normal trace requires r>10/p and initial W^{r+s_+(p)+epsilon,p} control. Here s_+(p)=max(0,6-13/p). The resulting sufficient initial order is strictly greater than

    max(10/p, 6-3/p).

At p=13/6 the minimum numerical order is60/13≈4.615, **in W^{s,13/6}, not H^s**. This must not be advertised as an L2 energy estimate. Converting H^s data into that Lp space has an additional integrability cost. The 1/p gain relative to fixed-time Lp loss cannot by itself pay the10/p normal trace cost. Taking p→infinity does not solve the problem: the retained initial-data loss tends to6 derivatives, and the theorem excludes p=infinity.

This composition is only a sufficient generic route. Better solution-restricted trace theorems can exist; failure of this budget is not their impossibility proof.

### A sharper actual-solution benchmark (optional, separately derived)

For the flat positive one-time half-wave u(t,x',y)=exp(it|D|)f with x'∈R3, y∈R10 and Schwartz f∈S(R13), direct Fourier coarea gives

    ||u(t,x',0)||_L2(R_t×R3) <= C ||f||_{dot H^(9/2)(R13)}.

This is a comparator calculation, not a GU result and not attributed as an OpenAI theorem. Write Fourier variables (eta,zeta), set r=sqrt(tau²-|eta|²) for tau>|eta|. The trace Fourier transform is, up to constants,

    qhat(tau,eta)=tau*r^8 integral_{S9} fhat(eta,r omega) d omega.

Cauchy–Schwarz on S9 and d tau=(r/tau)dr yield the bound by

    integral |fhat(eta,zeta)|² |(eta,zeta)|*|zeta|^8 d eta d zeta
      <= integral |fhat(xi)|² |xi|^9 d xi.

Frequency dilation makes9/2 the sharp homogeneous initial L2 Sobolev order for this global plane trace. The same high-frequency loss obstructs a uniform bounded trace from ordinary L2 or H1 initial data on a finite observation time interval after time-translating the focusing packet. This does not alone prove nonclosability on every solution space. On a finite time window, ordinary ambient trace plus wave evolution gives a sufficient initial-data H^s route with s>5; the direct solution estimate reaches9/2. These are distinct input spaces, not an identification of global ambient and initial norms. The direct bound still needs3.5 derivatives beyond H1 energy regularity (and4.5 beyond L2 data). The native first-order solution graph norm would reduce to ||u|| where Du=0; it does not manufacture this initial-data norm.

This comparator has **spacelike normal conormals**: no nonzero conormal to the time×R3 plane is characteristic for the ordinary one-time wave. It is not the native normal (6,4) geometry.
