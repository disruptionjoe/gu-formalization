## 2. The scalar Newton theorem

Work over a complete discretely valued field `K=k((t))`, with valuation ring
`R=k[[t]]`. For nonzero

```text
a=t^alpha a_0 + ...
b=t^beta  b_0 + ...
c=t^gamma c_0 + ...
```

and nonzero leading coefficients, the characteristic polynomial has valuation
points

```text
(lambda degree, t valuation) = (0,beta+gamma), (1,alpha), (2,0).
```

If the middle point lies below the line joining the endpoints, the two lower
hull slopes give `alpha` and `beta+gamma-alpha`. Otherwise that point is not a
lower-hull vertex and both roots have valuation `(beta+gamma)/2`. This proof
does not require diagonalizability.

### Fixed-heavy branch

For `alpha=0` and `beta+gamma>0`, one root remains fixed and the other has
valuation `beta+gamma`. Thus:

- strict bilateral integer `t^2`: `(beta,gamma)=(1,1)`;
- one-sided algebraic rivals: `(0,2)` and `(2,0)` also have slopes `(0,2)`,
  but one cross leg never becomes small;
- a fixed heavy root plus a direct linear light root occurs when
  `beta+gamma=1`; and
- if either cross leg is exactly zero, the scalar pencil has an exact
  protected zero rather than a small nonzero root.

### Direct branch

\[

\qquad

Balanced cross legs with `beta=gamma=1` give linear roots. If

## 6. Planted counterexamples

These exact controls prevent the valuation theorem from being overstated.

### Nonzero cross blocks can compose to zero

Take

\[
A=I_2,\qquad
B=t\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C=t\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Both cross blocks are nonzero and have valuation one, but `CA^-1B=0`. The
characteristic polynomial is `lambda^2(lambda-1)^2`; no nonzero slope-two
root exists.

### Rank-one and delayed compositions differ

With `A=I`, `B=tI` and `C=t diag(1,0)`, there is one exact zero and one
slope-two light mode. Replacing `C` by `t diag(1,t)` changes the light slopes
to two and three. Leading rank deficiency alone does not decide between an
exact zero and a delayed mode.

### A singular heavy block mixes mechanisms

For

\[
A=\operatorname{diag}(1,0),\qquad B=C=tI,
\]

the four slopes are `0,1,1,2`: one fixed-heavy pair and one direct pair.
Calling the whole pencil a seesaw would discard the singular sector.

### Nilpotent composition produces a zero Jordan chain

For

\[
A=I,\qquad B=tI,\qquad
C=t\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\]

the same characteristic polynomial `lambda^2(lambda-1)^2` occurs, but the
zero eigenspace has dimension one. Thus algebraic and geometric zero
multiplicity must both be checked.

### Singular rescaling can fake the entry pattern

The balanced pencil

\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&t\\-t&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

and the singularly rescaled pencil

\[
\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-t^2&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

have the same characteristic polynomial and two linear roots. Entry
valuations `(1,1)` versus `(0,2)` are not invariant under a singular
`t`-dependent basis change. Only regular unimodular changes preserve the
declared scale interpretation.
