# AR-3 — how much of our output is re-derivation

## Verdict first, unsoftened

**Under the primary rule, `6` of `44` artifacts in the window contain a
substantially re-derived result: a rate of `3/22`. Under the strictest
alternative it is `1/11`; under a rule that counts disciplined reproduction as
duplication it is `21/44`. The spread from `6/44` to `21/44` is the whole
measurement — it is exactly the width of the discipline-versus-waste boundary,
and anyone who quotes one of those numbers without the other is quoting a
choice, not a finding.**

Three results, in decreasing order of how much they move.

**1. Four of the ten confirmed re-derivation members were never caught by
anyone, and this sweep found them.** The brief named four cases, all
self-disclosed. Self-disclosure is the visible tail. The two undisclosed
clusters are:

- **`(49,42)`** — the inertia of the invariant trace form on `so(7,7)`.
  Computed **2026-08-08** in `selected-k77-residual-pairing-invariance` as the
  inertia of *"Clifford grade 2"* of `Cl(7,7)`. Re-derived **seven days later**
  by `BD-A`, which lists it inside its own *narrow novelty claim*, and by
  `BD-B` and `BD-D`. **None of the three names the 2026-08-08 owner.** `BD-D`
  then reproduced it `[R]` crediting **`BD-A`** — so the misattribution has
  already been laundered one hop into the record.
- **`I_1^B : G x MET(X^{1,3}) -> R`, draft p.43 eq (9.1)** — the object
  `BD-C`'s title presents as its central source determination. It was
  transcribed on **2026-06-25**, with the same page and the same equation
  number, in an hourly research-cycle file, and filed with
  `identity_status: quarantined_context_not_target_identity`. **Fifty-one
  days.** `BD-C` does not cite it; no artifact in the window does.

**2. The retrieval failure is not mostly a search-behaviour failure.** Of the
ten members: `4` were findable by an obvious search, `4` were structurally
buried, and `2` were unfindable in principle because the prior work did not yet
exist. Two burial mechanisms are mechanical, reproducible, and demonstrated in
the probe:

- **Hard wrap.** RB1's operative sentence — *"Positivity is neither used nor

  returns **zero** hits on the file that contains it. Whitespace-normalised

- **Regex metacharacters.** `MET(X^{1,3})` is the single most distinctive

**3. The novelty grep already fires preflight in this channel, and it still

**at preflight the number does not exist yet.** A concept-level sweep retrieves
objects and claims; it cannot retrieve a value the work has not produced. This

## 0. PREFLIGHT — six specialist lenses, run inline

### Lens P1 — information retrieval: what is the actual retrieval task?

The task is **known-item retrieval with an unknown query**. The agent knows the
*object* it is about to work on but not the *string* under which a prior result
was filed, and — critically — not the *value* it is about to produce. Classical
IR splits this into recall failure (the document exists, the query misses it)
and vocabulary mismatch (the document indexes the concept under different
terms). This lens predicts, before any measurement, that vocabulary mismatch
will dominate in a corpus of ~3,900 hand-written markdown files with no
controlled vocabulary and no result index. It predicts correctly: `(49,42)` is
filed under *"Clifford grade 2"* and sought as *"the `so(7,7)` trace form"*.
Λ²(ℝ^{7,7}) ≅ 𝔰𝔬(7,7) is an isomorphism every party knows and neither party
indexed.

The lens also issues a warning that shapes the whole method: **a zero-hit exact
substring search is not evidence of absence, and the repository already knows
this** — `OT-2`, `BD-B` and `CG-1` all state the rule explicitly before
claiming novelty. So exact-string absence cannot be my detector either.

### Lens P2 — bibliometrics and citation analysis: what does the citation graph measure here?

In bibliometrics, independent rediscovery is detected as *bibliographic
coupling failure*: two documents on the same object that share no references
and do not cite each other. That transfers, with one correction. In a
literature, non-citation is weak evidence — authors read without citing. Here
it is strong evidence, because this channel's own standing rule requires the
prior-art table. **An artifact that carries a prior-art table and omits an owner
did not know about that owner.** That converts a soft bibliometric signal into
a near-hard one, and it is why `K6` and `K9` are safe findings: `BD-A` and
`BD-C` both carry extensive attribution and neither names its true first owner.

The lens also warns about **citation laundering**: once `BD-D` credits `BD-A`
for `(49,42)`, the true owner becomes unreachable by following references. One
hop has already happened. That is the mechanism by which a rediscovery becomes
permanent.

### Lens P3 — deduplication and record linkage: which blocking key actually works?

Record linkage needs a blocking key: a cheap signature that puts true duplicates
in the same bucket. I tested three over the whole corpus.

| blocking key | candidate pairs | genuine new clusters | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| rare shared 7-grams (lexical near-duplicate) | 182 | **0** | wrong instrument |
| bare integers, 3–7 digits, in ≤4 files | 48 | **0** | pure noise (page and line numbers) |
| structured signature pairs `(a,b)`, both ≥10, in ≤6 files | 7 | **1** (`(49,42)`) | the only one that worked |

The 7-gram result is the important negative and it is structural, not
incidental. **Lexical near-duplicate detection finds copying. Independent
re-derivation is the exact case where the text does *not* match** — different
author, different route, different vocabulary, same answer. Of the 182 n-gram
pairs, the overwhelming majority were *properly cited quotation*, most of it
from shared primary-source transcripts. Deduplication technology is aimed at
the opposite of the phenomenon under study, and any future attempt to automate
this should not start there.

What did work is the **exact-value key**: a derived scalar or tuple is a
near-unique fingerprint of an object-plus-computation, is cheap to extract, and
survives paraphrase. That single observation is the seed of the §6 remedy.

Precision must be reported honestly: the signature key returned `(49,42)` (true),
`(48,42)` (**false positive** — the traceless Gauss pairing of an unrelated
construction, numerically coincident) and `(16,144)` (already cited). One in
two on the novel candidates. A detector at that precision is a triage aid, not
an oracle, and must not be run unattended.

### Lens P4 — corpus linguistics: what does the corpus do to its own searchability?

Two properties of the corpus defeat naive search, and both are measurable.

1. **Hard wrapping at ~76 columns.** Prose is wrapped, so any sentence longer
   than a line is invisible to line-oriented search. The single most quotable
   sentence in this whole investigation — *"Positivity is neither used nor
   available"* — is split across a newline in RB1 and returns **zero** `grep`
   hits in the file that contains it. Every agent that has ever grepped a
   sentence in this repository has been searching a mutilated index.
2. **Mathematical notation collides with regex syntax.** `MET(X^{1,3})`,
   `so(6,4)`, `Λ^2`, `(d+1)/(3(d-1))` all contain metacharacters. The braces in
   `{1,3}` are the worst case, because the pattern does not error — under
   `grep` it silently means *one to three carets* and returns nothing. A silent
   zero is indistinguishable from genuine absence, and the standing rule tells
   agents that zero hits are not evidence of novelty — which is right, but does
   not tell them the search was broken.

This lens fixes the method: **every search in this sweep runs on
whitespace-normalised text with fixed-string matching.** Anything else measures
the corpus's formatting rather than its content.

### Lens P5 — knowledge management: where does a result actually live?

The repository has a claim register (`source-claim-register.yaml`, 2,881 lines),
a routing method, a ledger, `CURRENT-STATE.yaml`, `DERIVATION-PROGRESS.md` and
an improvement register. It has **no result index**. Every derived value lives
in exactly one place: prose or a table inside the artifact that produced it,
frequently one row of one table inside a 1,000-line file.

This lens predicts the burial mode precisely, and the prediction is confirmed:
`(49,42)` is one cell of a five-row table on line 73 of a conditional-build
artifact; the eq-(9.1) transcription is one row of a six-row table on line 91 of
an hourly research-cycle file. Both are *correct*, *dated*, *machine-generated*
and *unreachable* — not because they are hidden, but because nothing indexes
them by what they are.

The lens supplies the sharpest sub-finding. The eq-(9.1) row carries
`quarantined_context_not_target_identity`. That tag was correct **for the
question the hourly cycle was asking** (is this the `D_GU^ε` identity? no). It
then functioned, for fifty-one days, as an instruction to every later reader
that the row was irrelevant. **A scoped negative tag became an unscoped
suppression.** That is a knowledge-management defect with a specific name and a
specific fix, and it is worth more than the rate.

### Lens P6 — epistemics of independent confirmation: when is a repeat a gain?

This is the lens that decides whether the measurement is honest, so it is
stated as a rule before any counting.

A repeat is **not** waste when:

- **(a) it is commissioned redundancy.** The channel deliberately runs
  concurrent routes. `BD-B` records that had `BD-A` not run, it would have
  shipped a complete sweep of one of three objects while presenting it as *the*
  sweep. Two routes converging is the designed error-detector, not a leak.
- **(b) it is reproduction before use.** Artifacts here re-run prior results
  tagged `[R]` as a certification step before building on them. `MC-1` tags
  every such check *"W168 REPRODUCED (not new)"*; `LA-8` re-derives `MD-1`'s
  `E1/E2/E3` in sympy and credits `MD-1` in full; `BD-D` reproduces every one of
  `BD-A`'s inertias and moves not one of them. This is what makes results here
  trustworthy and calling it waste would destroy the practice.
- **(c) it changes grade.** `AC-1` computed what `W222` had flagged
  `FROM-MEMORY`. An assertion becoming a computation is a gain, not a repeat.

A repeat **is** waste when the later party performed the derivation itself, an
earlier artifact already held it, and the later party did not have it in hand at
derivation time. The operational test, in decreasing strength: the artifact says
so; or the artifact never names the owner anywhere while carrying an otherwise
thorough prior-art table.

The lens issues one warning I obeyed throughout. **The bias in this task runs
toward over-counting**, because disciplined reproduction is loud, well-labelled
and easy to grep, while genuine re-derivation is quiet and often invisible. A
sweep that counts labels will over-report waste by a factor of roughly three —
`21/44` versus `6/44` — and will do so while damaging the exact practice that
makes the corpus trustworthy. Every classification below therefore had to clear
the *derived-it-himself-and-did-not-hold-it* bar, not merely the
*appears-twice* bar.

This is the split that matters more than the rate.

Read that carefully: **the findable cases were all actually found.** In every
one of `K1`–`K4` the agent's own prior-art sweep located the owner and said so.
The repository's search behaviour, where search can work at all, is good. The

**The four buried cases were found by nobody.** They are not a search-effort
problem. No amount of diligence retrieves `(49,42)` when the target is indexed

amount of diligence retrieves a row that has been tagged as not-the-target-

cases a better grep does not touch.

### 2.1 Two burial mechanisms, demonstrated

Both are asserted in the probe, in both directions.

**Hard wrap.** In `explorations/rb1-source-repo-current-musical-2026-07-30.md`:

```text
$ grep -c "Positivity is neither used nor available" <rb1>
0
$ tr '\n' ' ' < <rb1> | grep -c "Positivity is neither used nor  *available"
1
```

The sentence is there. Line-based search cannot see it. The corpus is
hard-wrapped throughout, so this applies to **every** multi-word result
statement in ~3,900 files.

**Regex metacharacters.**

```text
$ grep -rl "MET(X^{1,3})" --include="*.md" .     ->  0 files
$ grep -rlF 'MET(X^{1,3})' --include="*.md" .    ->  6 files
```

Under `grep`, `{1,3}` is an interval quantifier applied to the literal caret, so
the pattern means *`MET(X` followed by one to three carets followed by `)`* —
which occurs nowhere. It does not error. It returns a clean, confident zero.
Pasted into Python's `re` the same pattern raises `nothing to repeat` and does
not compile at all. Two of the six files it silently hides are from June 2026,
and one of them is `K9`'s owner.

## 3. HOSTILE REVIEW — run on my own result

**The charge: the rate is an artifact of the inclusion rule.** Largely
sustained, and here is the spread.

| rule | definition | numerator | rate |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `R2a` | uncited **and** not concurrent — pure retrieval failure only | 4 | **1/11** |
| `R0` | only clusters the repository itself caught | 5 | **5/44** |
| `R1` | **primary** — derived it again without holding it | 6 | **3/22** |
| `R3` | broad — disciplined reproduction counted as duplication | 21 | **21/44** |

`R3 − R1 = 15/44`. **The rule choice moves the answer by more than three times
the answer.** Anyone quoting `6/44` as "the rediscovery rate" without that
sentence is over-claiming.

**Which rule is right?** `R1`, and the reason is not aesthetic. `R3` counts
`BD-D` reproducing `BD-A`'s inertias `[R]` before using them as *waste*. That
reproduction is why `BD-D`'s numbers can be trusted, and the day it stops
happening the corpus stops being checkable. `R3` also counts `LA-11`
re-deriving `OT-1` — the re-derivation that **caught `OT-1`'s error**. A metric
that scores error-detection as waste is worse than no metric.

**Charge 2: `K5` is commissioned concurrency, not waste.** Partly sustained.
`LA-7` and `LA-11` were both commissioned the same day, and the brief itself
says deliberate concurrency is the system working. Against that: the harm is
documented and concrete — `LA-9` concluded *exactly one* denominator-mover
existed, which was false, and the disposition packet had to warn that applying
either delta alone gets `83` when the truth is `84`. `R2a` drops `K5` entirely
and the rate falls to `1/11`. **Both numbers are in the table; readers who
think concurrency is free should use `1/11`.**

**Charge 3: `n = 44` over two days is too small, and 08-14 and 08-15 are not
the same regime.** Sustained on the second half, and it cuts against me.
Prior-art discipline is not uniform across the window: **7 of 21** artifacts on
08-14 carry a prior-art section, versus **14 of 23** on 08-15. The practice
tightened mid-window. Since undisclosed re-derivation is *harder to detect* in
artifacts with no prior-art table, my method has **lower recall on the 08-14
half**, and 5 of my 6 counted artifacts are from 08-15. The measured rate is
therefore biased **downward** on the earlier day, and `6/44` should be read as a
**floor**, not an estimate.

**Charge 4: the sweep's own recall is unmeasured.** Fully sustained, and it is
the most serious limitation here. I found two undisclosed clusters. I have **no
estimate of how many I missed**, because the only detector that worked —
exact-value matching on structured signature pairs — can only find results that
(a) are numeric, (b) are written as a parenthesised pair, and (c) survived
paraphrase. A re-derived *theorem* with no distinctive number is invisible to
every instrument I ran. **The true undisclosed count is bounded below by 2 and
above by nothing I can compute.** The n-gram detector, which would have been the
natural complement, is structurally incapable of the task (Lens P3), so its zero
yield is not reassurance.

**Charge 5: `K6` might be a numeric coincidence, like `(48,42)` was.** Rejected,
and the arithmetic settles it. `49 + 42 = 91 = C(14,2) = dim Λ²ℝ^{7,7} =
dim 𝔰𝔬(7,7)`. The maximal compact `𝔰𝔬(7) ⊕ 𝔰𝔬(7)` has dimension `21 + 21 = 42`,
so the invariant form is negative on exactly 42 directions and positive on 49.
Both artifacts report the invariant form on the same 91-dimensional space with
the same inertia. It is one object under two names. By contrast `(48,42)` is a
90-dimensional traceless Gauss pairing from an unrelated construction, and I
report it as my detector's false positive rather than quietly dropping it.

**Charge 6: you counted `BD-A` twice — once for `K1`, once for `K6`.** Not in
the numerator. The rate counts **distinct artifacts**, not members. `BD-A`
contributes 1 to a numerator of 6 whether it carries one re-derivation or two.
Members are reported separately (10) because the day-gap and findability
distributions are member-level facts.

## 4. THE REMEDY — what should fire before the work

The current rule fires **after**: derive, grep, disclose. The honest constraint
on any replacement is stated first, because it kills the obvious proposal.

> **A preflight grep cannot search for the value the work has not yet
> produced.** `BD-A` and `BD-D` both ran their novelty sweep *before writing a
> line of the construction*, exactly as a preflight rule would demand, and
> neither could have found `(49,42)`, because at preflight `(49,42)` did not
> exist. Any remedy that consists of "grep earlier" is already implemented and
> already failed on the cases that were never caught.

So the remedy is three staged parts, only one of which is a search.

### Phase A — preflight *object* retrieval (before construction)

Search on **carrier identity**, never on the expected conclusion. From the brief
alone, enumerate:

1. **Every carrier, under every spelling the repository uses.** For `BD-A` that
   is `so(7,7)`, `𝔰𝔬(7,7)`, `Λ²ℝ^{7,7}`, `Λ^2 V_14`, `bivectors of Cl(7,7)`,
   **`Clifford grade 2`**, `so(14) split real form`. The synonym set is
   generated from the repo's own `lab/process/layer0-fork-registry.yaml` and
   homonym registries, which already exist for exactly this purpose.
2. **Every dimension the carrier forces, before any computation.**
   `dim so(7,7) = 91`; `dim(Λ¹ ⊗ ad) = 4 × 91 = 364`. These *are* computable at
   preflight and they are highly distinctive. A preflight search for `91`
   co-occurring with `inertia` would have hit the 2026-08-08 table, which prints
   both.
3. **Run every search whitespace-normalised and fixed-string.** Not `grep`.
   This is a one-line change and it recovers every hard-wrapped sentence in the
   corpus.

**What Phase A still misses, honestly.** The synonym table only expands
vocabulary someone already noticed was synonymous. `Λ²(ℝ^{7,7}) ≅ 𝔰𝔬(7,7)` has
to be *in* the table for `Clifford grade 2` to be reachable — and you add that
entry only once you know the identification, which is precisely what the
searcher lacks. Phase A shrinks the buried class; it cannot empty it.

### Phase B — post-derivation *value* check, before the novelty claim is written

Once every number exists and **before** the claim ceiling is drafted: take each
derived scalar, tuple, dimension and inertia; search the corpus for it verbatim
in normalised, fixed-string form; and require an explicit disposition for every
hit — *prior art, credited* / *different object, and here is why* / *genuinely
absent*. Mechanical, cheap, and it is exactly what would have caught `(49,42)`
in all three artifacts.

**What Phase B does not buy.** It fires after the derivation, so the compute is
already spent. What it saves is the **false novelty claim** — which is the more
damaging error, because `BD-D` has already laundered `(49,42)` one hop by
crediting `BD-A`, and one more hop makes the true owner unrecoverable.

### Phase C — the structural fix: a result index

Neither A nor B helps if the earlier result is one table cell in a 1,000-line
file. Every probe in this repository already emits exact results and already
prints them. Harvest them into one append-only table:

```text
value | object (canonical name + aliases) | owner artifact | date | probe | grade
```

Phase B then becomes an index lookup instead of a corpus grep, and Phase A can
search the index by *carrier* rather than by string. This is the only part of
the remedy that addresses the buried class rather than the sequencing class.

Two rules make it work, and both are cheap:

- **Scoped negative tags must carry their scope.** `K9` was lost to
  `quarantined_context_not_target_identity` — a tag that was *correct for the
  question being asked in June* and read as *irrelevant to everything* for fifty-
  one days. Written as `not_the_D_GU_identity__DOES_record_the_action_domain`,
  it is a finding rather than a suppression.
- **The index is written by the probe, not by the author.** Anything requiring
  discipline at write time decays.

### What no retrieval step can fix

`K5` is unreachable by any of the above. `LA-7` and `LA-11` were written
concurrently; no search finds a sibling that does not yet exist. The only fixes
are **scheduling** (serialise commissions that touch a shared quantity) or
**registration** (a commission declares the objects it will touch *before*
starting, and a second commission touching the same object is told). The ledger
denominator is exactly the kind of shared quantity that warrants registration.
This is a coordination fix and it should not be confused with a retrieval one.

## 5. POSTFLIGHT — five lenses, run inline

### Lens Q1 — measurement theory: is `3/22` a rate or a ratio of conveniences?

It is a **conditional floor**, and it should be quoted that way. Conditional on:
the window, the exclusion of meta-audits, rule `R1`, and the recall of a
detector whose recall is unmeasured. Charge 4 in §3 is the binding one — the
numerator counts what was *found*, and the only working instrument was
exact-value matching on numeric tuples. A theorem re-derived under different
words with no distinctive number cannot appear in the numerator, and I cannot
bound how many of those exist. **Every honest use of this number carries the
word "at least".**

### Lens Q2 — survivorship bias: what does the visible tail imply about the invisible one?

Of the ten members, six were self-disclosed and four were not. Self-disclosure
requires the artifact to run a prior-art sweep at all — and only **31 of 44**
artifacts in the window carry any prior-art discipline, splitting **7/21** on
08-14 against **14/23** on 08-15. The thirteen artifacts with no prior-art
section are the ones where an undisclosed re-derivation would be least visible,
and my detector found none there — which is far more likely to reflect the
detector's blindness than those artifacts' cleanliness. The observed
disclosed-to-undisclosed ratio of `6:4` is therefore an upper bound on
disclosure, not an estimate of it.

### Lens Q3 — incentive design: what does publishing this number do?

A rediscovery rate is a metric an agent can game two ways: cite everything
loosely so nothing looks re-derived, or stop reproducing prior results so
nothing looks duplicated. The second is catastrophic and the `R3` column is
where it would show up as an improvement. **This artifact should never be used
to drive `R3` down.** If anything, `R3 = 21/44` is a health indicator to be
*maintained*: it says roughly half the channel's artifacts re-run something they
depend on before depending on it. The number to drive down is `R2a = 1/11`, the
uncited-and-avoidable class, and only that one.

### Lens Q4 — cost accounting: what did the duplication actually cost?

Less than the count suggests, and the honest accounting is unflattering to my
own headline. `K1` cost four lines of construction — `BD-A` says so directly:
*"That is the whole construction. It is four lines … the work was in deciding
what to build, not in building it."* `K6` cost one congruence computation inside
a probe that had to run anyway for `(189,175)` and `(273,91)`, which **are**
new. `K3` and `K9` cost no compute at all; they cost a *wrong premise* — a
ledger row and a revival trigger written as `MISSING_CONSTRUCTION` for an object
the repository had held since June.

So the dominant cost of re-derivation here is **not wasted compute**. It is
**mis-filed status**: rows that say unbuilt about built objects, and novelty
claims that have to be walked back inside the artifact that makes them. That
reframing changes what the remedy is for — Phase C is worth more than Phases A
and B combined, and none of the three is primarily about saving cycles.

### Lens Q5 — corpus metrology: does this artifact make the file more honest, and in which direction?

Downward, which is the correct sign. It adds four previously-unrecorded
re-derivations, corrects one attribution that had already propagated one hop
(`BD-D` credits `BD-A` for `(49,42)`; the owner is 2026-08-08), and demonstrates
two mechanical retrieval defects that call into question every zero-hit novelty
grep ever run in this repository — including, necessarily, the ones inside this
artifact. It moves no row, changes no verdict, and touches no grade. Its most
useful output is not `3/22`; it is the sentence that a preflight sweep cannot
see an uncomputed value, and the observation that the corpus's own formatting
has been silently defeating its own searches.
