## 0. PREFLIGHT — seven problem-matched lenses, applied before the build

1. **Lexicography / terminography (ISO 704-style).** Distinguish homonymy
   (one form, unrelated senses), polysemy (related senses), allography/variant
   (one sense, many forms), and paronymy (near-forms). Consequence: the
   register has a `kind` type system (`homonym`, `near_collision`,
   `prefix_collision`, `transcription_variant`, `not_a_homonym`), each sense
   carries its own definition and home namespace, and the disambiguator is a
   prescribed designation. Where a lexicographer would say "polysemy"
   (`positive`), the register says so in a `note` and types by HAZARD — no
   result transfers between senses — because that is what the incidents cost.
2. **Terminology-management practice.** Termbases die when they are not on the
   authoring path. Consequence: entries are keyed by the exact token a reader
   would grep; the disambiguator is the first actionable field; the gate
   PRINTS its census in CI output so the register surfaces without being
   sought.
3. **Corpus linguistics.** Usage claims need word-boundary counts, attestation
   loci, and exact quotes. This lens caught a live bug during this very build:
   case-insensitive substring counting reports 9 "vial" in the gu-40
   transcript; the word-boundary count is 1 — the other 8 are `trivial(ly)`.
   That pitfall is now a planted false fact in the certificate and a mutation
   in its selftest.
4. **Ontology engineering.** The register maps FORMS to CONCEPTS; concept
   identity is the home namespace; the failure mode is cross-namespace
   transfer. Consequence: every sense names its namespace, and "no transfer"
   rulings are carried verbatim from the owning artifacts.
5. **Incident-driven safety registers (LASA/confusable-name lists).** Aviation
   and pharmacy confusables lists are seeded ONLY from error reports and pair
   each entry with a corrective barrier (tall-man lettering = our
   disambiguators). Alerting for ambient-language hazards is soft, not
   blocking. Consequence: no entry without a receipt; sensing is warn-only.
6. **Goodhart / gaming.** A red gate on prose words gets gamed or deleted;
   baselines must not become targets. Consequence: sensing baselines are dated
   observations; the gate hard-fails if anyone flips a sensor from
   `warn_only` to enforcing (that exact corruption is planted in its
   selftest and must be caught).
7. **Findability / information architecture.** SG-1's misroute is a
   findability failure: the hunter of a chirality selector found exactly one
   token spelled "chiral". Consequence: entries are keyed by token including
   case (`CHIRAL` vs `chiral`), and the register's own channel is excluded
   from its sensors so the register never warns about itself.
