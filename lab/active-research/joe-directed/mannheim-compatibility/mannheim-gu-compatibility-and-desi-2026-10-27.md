`Delta^2 r^n = n(n+1)(n-1)(n-2) r^(n-4)` admits
`1, 1/r, r, r^2` away from the origin. This explains the operator-level
possibility; physical source matching, boundary conditions and measured orbits
still decide which coefficients are admissible. It is not a new GU force law.

## Why this does not yet update a DESI prediction

For every smooth four-dimensional FLRW metric, with any spatial curvature,
`C_abcd = 0`. For an arbitrary compactly supported metric variation,
the first variation of `sqrt(-g) C^2` contains either `C` or `C^2` in every
term. It therefore vanishes on that background: the Bach contribution is zero.
This varies the **full action**, not a symmetry-reduced zero Lagrangian.

Consequently pure vacuum Weyl-squared gravity does not select an FLRW expansion
history. Adding just that term to a fixed action does not change its exact
FLRW background equations when remaining fields and couplings are held fixed.
The probe verifies all 256 algebraic Weyl components for arbitrary
`a_ddot/a` and `(a_dot^2+k)/a^2`; a vanishing Lorentzian scalar `C^2` alone
would not have proved the tensor claim. De Sitter and radiation-like flat
FLRW histories illustrate nonselection, not GU solutions. In pure Weyl gravity
such histories may be gauge-related; they do not establish physically distinct
cosmologies without a matter/clock prescription.

This does **not** mean conformal gravity has no cosmology. Mannheim's complete
matter tensor, including condensate curvature and potential terms, imposes
nontrivial equations through `T_ab=0`; see
[Cosmological Perturbations in Conformal Gravity, §5.4](https://arxiv.org/html/1109.4119v2).
Perturbations can have nonzero Weyl tensor and affect growth or lensing.
Other fourth-order actions, such as an `R^2` term, are not covered by the
Weyl-squared nonselection result.

A GU-derived or explicitly conditional background family can be tested using
its physical metric/redshift convention, `H(z)` and spatial curvature:

```text
D_H = c/H(z),     D_M = transverse comoving distance
BAO: D_H/r_d and D_M/r_d
Alcock–Paczynski: F_AP = D_M/D_H = D_M H/c
```

`F_AP` is ruler-independent. Distance-shape fits can also profile a declared
`H0*r_d` nuisance, so a complete early-universe derivation is not needed before
every useful test. Absolute calibration and a fully predicted acoustic ruler
require more. Use covariance and appropriate likelihoods; AP/full-shape
extraction includes templates, tracer and redshift-space assumptions that a
nonstandard perturbation theory must check. A fitted family is not a
parameter-free prediction.

As of this assessment, [DESI DR2 IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27410v3)
reports `D_H/r_d=8.600±0.066` and `D_M/r_d=39.32±0.33` at `z=2.33`.
Its CPL comparison gives a 2.7-sigma preference over Lambda-CDM with CMB,
and 3.2 sigma with CMB plus **DES-Dovekie**. These are named model comparisons,
not direct observations of a dark-energy substance or evidence for GU.
The existing [B3-DE correction](../../../../explorations/conditional-build/b3-de-likelihood-scope-disposition-2026-08-23.md)
already preserves the likelihood evidence while rejecting literal use of CPL
coordinates as pointwise observations. Effective `w(z)` in modified gravity
also depends on the GR reinterpretation and matter subtraction.

No updated GU `w0,wa`, dark-energy sign, dark-matter abundance or joint DESI
likelihood follows here. Galaxy-rotation fits and cosmological dark matter are
different tests; reproducing one would not settle growth, lensing or the CMB.
