## 4. Exact affine stabilizer theorem

Let the character labels be `X=F_3`. Genuine group automorphisms act by
`k\mapsto ak`, for `a\in F_3^\times`; hence `Aut(C_3)=C_2`, it fixes `0`, and
it exchanges `1` and `2`. For the explicitly enlarged label-equivalence
comparison, also allow every affine map

\[
k\longmapsto ak+b,\qquad a\in F_3^\times,\ b\in F_3,
\]

The six maps form `AGL(1,3)\cong S_3`. A translation `k\mapsto k+b` is
tensoring every character by `\chi^b`; when `b\ne0` it moves the trivial
character and therefore is not in `Aut(C_3)` and is not an automorphism of a
fixed `C_3`-module. For a multiplicity fingerprint
`m=(m_0,m_1,m_2)`, let this *declared enlarged equivalence group* act by
relabeling coordinates.

**Theorem.**

- A constant tuple `(v,v,v)` has full `S_3` stabilizer and one label orbit
  `{0,1,2}`.
- A tuple `(u,v,v)` with `u != v` has stabilizer
  `C_2={id,k\mapsto-k}` and two label orbits `{0}` and `{1,2}`.
- A tuple with three distinct entries has trivial stabilizer and three
  singleton label orbits.

The proof is exhaustive over the six affine label equivalences and is
reproduced exactly by the probe. Therefore `(14,12,12)`, `(8,6,6)`,
`(7,6,6)`, and `(16,12,12)` all have the occupied `{0}|{1,2}` stabilizer
orbit under that declared enlarged action. Their stabilizer is precisely the
genuine `Aut(C_3)=C_2`. The regular `(64,64,64)` control does not distinguish
an orbit under the enlarged action.

This theorem constructs no marked orbit. The unequal multiplicity distinguishes
the singleton in the fingerprint under the declared enlarged comparison, and
the genuine automorphism group already fixes that trivial-character label.
Neither statement turns an affine translation into a module automorphism or
identifies the singleton with a source sector.

## 6. Complex versus real meaning

Over `C`, the three central primitive idempotents are

\[
e_k=\frac13\sum_{j=0}^2\zeta^{-kj}g^j.
\]

They are orthogonal, sum to one, and inversion/conjugation fixes `e_0` while
exchanging `e_1` and `e_2`. Over `R`, only `e_0` and `e_1+e_2` are central
idempotents. Equivalently,

\[
\mathbb R[C_3]\cong\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb C,
\qquad
V_{Q,\mathbb R}\cong\mathbb R^{14}\oplus
(\mathbb R^2_{rot}\otimes\mathbb R^{12}).
\]

So the complex singleton-plus-conjugate-pair becomes one real trivial block
plus twelve copies of the two-dimensional rotation representation. The pair
is not two canonically separated real sectors. In particular, it does not by
itself reproduce two equivalent true-family modules plus the distinct source
imposter.

## 7. The canonical-quotient obstruction

The complex equivariant endomorphism algebra is

\[
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C[C_3]}(V_Q)
\cong M_{14}(\mathbb C)\oplus M_{12}(\mathbb C)\oplus M_{12}(\mathbb C),
\]

of complex dimension `14^2+12^2+12^2=484`. The real centralizer is

\[
M_{14}(\mathbb R)\oplus M_{12}(\mathbb C),
\]

also of real dimension `14^2+2(12^2)=484`. The equal number is a dimensional
coincidence across different base fields, not an algebra identification.

Many equivariant maps to the regular module exist:

\[
\dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{Hom}_{C_3}(V_Q,\mathbb C[C_3])
=14+12+12=38.
\]

But none is canonical. A natural rank-one quotient on an isotype would require
a covector invariant under the full `GL_n` acting on its multiplicity space.
Invariance under scalar matrices alone gives `f=lambda f` for every nonzero
`lambda`; choosing `lambda != 1` forces `f=0`. Applying this to all three
occupied isotypes proves that no nonzero one-line-per-isotype quotient is
natural under module automorphisms.

Therefore existence of rank-three equivariant quotients is not enough. A
choice of one would be precisely an added selector/external datum, which is
off limits in this conditional swing.
