# Curt's `(7,7)` explanation, and whether Weinstein corroborates it

## Layer-0 first, because it is the whole point

**Curt Jaimungal is the EXPOSITOR. Eric Weinstein is the AUTHOR.** The iceberg
video is Curt explaining his reading of GU. The repository has been calling both
"the source" — including in `REAL-CLIFFORD-FORM`'s `settled_how`, which cited
*"Curt/Eric's exact source-typed arithmetic"* as a single object. They are not one
object, they do not agree on notation, and only one of them is authorial.

## What Curt says, verbatim from the fetched primary

**Trace line as an acknowledged free choice** (`~00:40:29`):

> "The traceless part has a signature, in this case **3,6**, whereas the trace
> itself contributes either a **1,0 or a 0,1, depending on a choice**."

**The choice, attributed and deferred** (`~00:40:53`):

> "So we can either choose **4,6 or 3,7**. For geometric unity, **Eric chooses 4,6
> for reasons that will become clear later**, though there are generalizations of
> geometric unity with other choices."

**The sum** (`~00:41:27`):

> "With our choice of **4,6 for the vertical space and 1,3 for the horizontal
> space** from space-time, we get a total signature of **7,7**."

**And an aside about Eric's state of mind** (`~00:41:27`, `~00:42:06`):

> "Note, GU could have had a signature of **5,9** and **I believe Eric isn't
> sure** which of these is the sector of our universe in his theory."
>
> "the same is also true for a **spin 5,9** bundle **matching what's required for
> the standard model**."

**So Curt gives no reason.** He names the choice, attributes it to Eric, defers
the justification ("reasons that will become clear later"), and never supplies it
in this passage. *"I believe Eric isn't sure"* is the expositor's belief about the
author's state of mind — it is not the author declining to choose, and it should
never have been weighed as if it were.

**And Curt's stated blocks do not sum to his stated total.** `(4,6) + (1,3)` is
`(5,9)`, not `(7,7)`. Two sentences later he names `(5,9)` as a *different option
GU could have had* — when it is what his own arithmetic just produced. This is an
**expositor's slip**, and the repository made it load-bearing.

## What Weinstein says, in his own voice — and he does corroborate

`lab/sources/transcripts/portal-special-gu-first-look-2020-04-02.md:525`,
`02:42:20`:

> "the Pati-Salam theory, which is usually advertised as ... `SU(4)×SU(2)×SU(2)`,
> is really much more naturally **`Spin(6) × Spin(4)` when the trace portion of
> the space of metrics is put in with the proper sign if you're trying to generate
> the sector that begins as `X(1,3)`**. Remember `X^d`, where `d = 4`, is the
> generic situation. But you have all these different sectors. I believe that
> these sectors probably exist if this model's correct, but **we are trapped in
> the `(1,3)` sector**, so you have to figure out what the implications are for
> **pushing that indefinite signature up into an indefinite signature on the `Y`
> manifold**."

Four authorial facts, none of them Curt's:

1. **The trace-sign choice is real and the author's.** "the trace portion of the
   space of metrics is put in with the **proper sign**".
2. **There is a stated criterion for that sign, and it is Pati–Salam.** The sign
   is the one that makes `Spin(6) × Spin(4)` natural "rather than directly in the
   `Spin(10)`, `SU(5)` line of thinking." **This is the reason Curt deferred and
   never gave.** Note `Spin(6) × Spin(4)` against a `(6,4)` fibre.
3. **The base is `(1,3)`, twice, unhedged** — "the sector that begins as `X(1,3)`"
   and "**we are trapped in the `(1,3)` sector**."
4. **The push-up to `Y` is explicitly an open problem in the author's own words** —
   "you have to figure out what the implications are for pushing that indefinite
   signature up."

## And the 2021 draft closes it

`gu-2021-draft-s11-s12-extraction-2026-08-03.md:250`, eq (12.19), p.61, verbatim:

```text
ג∗(TY^{7,7}) = TX^{1,3} ⊕ N^{6,4}_ג
```

Base, fibre and total in **one equation, one notation**, and `(1,3)+(6,4)=(7,7)`
checks out. The draft's fibre is `(6,4)` — *this repository's* orientation, not
Curt's `(4,6)`.
