## The problem this fixes

A 25-lens council was synthesized to "a generation is an index, not an object" plus a three-way labor split.
That headline is right and it is also **lossy in a specific direction**: synthesis preserves each lens's
*claim* and discards its *mechanism*. The claim is the general part; the mechanism is the operational
consequence — and **the mechanism is where the falsifiable content lives.**

Worked demonstration. The ECMP lens was scored "low-medium, declines the count question." Its claim was
*"the three are exactly degenerate."* Its mechanism, recovered, is a four-step argument:

1. When the cost metric is **exactly** degenerate, the tiebreaker cannot be a function of the metric. This is
   a theorem in routing. Applied: net chirality identically zero, every maximal positive-norm subspace 50/50,
   so **any selector must be a function of something outside the Krein form.** *(Note this is INDEPENDENT of
   the parity argument: parity says the interior cannot produce an odd VALUE; degeneracy says the form cannot
   produce a SELECTOR at all.)*
2. At exact degeneracy you get **many** tiebreakers, not none — a hash is deterministic, consistent, and not
   metric-derived. Canon found exactly this: *"at spectral degeneracies (GU's three-generation target regime)
   C exists but is **non-unique**."* The repo files that as failure. **ECMP says non-uniqueness at exact
   degeneracy is the expected structure**, and the right move is to stop hunting for THE derived C-operator
   and instead **characterize the moduli of admissible C's and ask what selects within it.**
3. **Polarization.** The tiebreaker must be globally consistent or observers disagree about which half of
   each pair is physical.
4. But `w != 0` in `H^1(F;Z/2)` means **no global section exists.** So it must be fixed at a cut.

Four steps, three of them dropped in synthesis — and step 4 is the firewall-boundary conclusion reached from
routing theory, independently of the topology argument.

## The three cross-distance convergences (the most valuable output)

## Method note for future councils

**Store two things per lens, not one: the CLAIM and the OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE.** "Equal-cost" is the claim;
"therefore the tiebreaker is a hash, must be globally consistent, and rehashes on topology change" is what
makes it testable.

**And treat the synthesis as an INDEX, not a replacement.** When a new fact lands — e.g. "C is non-unique at
degeneracies" — the question is *which lens predicted this?*, not *does this fit the synthesis?* The 25-lens
artifact retains all 25; the loss happened in summary, not in the record.
