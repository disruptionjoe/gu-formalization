## Lens 1 — Research Director

**1.1 The program has been optimizing the map instead of the territory for
weeks.** Since 2026-08-22 the repository has produced roughly a dozen
artifacts. Every one of them — the descents, the benchmark bench, the owner
ledger, both requirement audits, the kill-typing ratchet — is *about* the
research rather than *being* the research. The last artifact that computed a
new physical fact rather than typing, ranking, or reconciling existing ones is
several days back. This is the single biggest drag and it is invisible from
inside, because map work produces commits, probes, and green gates at exactly
the rate real work would.

**1.2 "No newly enabled arc" has become a self-fulfilling scheduler state.**
Every frontier replay for a week concluded that nothing new was executable and
then selected maintenance. The conclusion was true only relative to an
enumeration that nobody was expanding — as proved on 2026-08-22, when
enumerating one more ledger obligation produced an executable arc immediately.
A frontier that only ever contracts will always report itself blocked.

**1.3 The program has no definition of a good week.** There is a ledger, a
delta instrument, an owner ledger, and now a velocity council, but no stated
target rate of anything: not ledger points, not derivations, not kills. Without
one, maintenance and physics are indistinguishable at review time, and the
cheaper one wins by default.

## Lens 2 — Operations / Scheduler Engineer

**2.1 The hourly loop's cheapest legal move is a reconcile, so it reconciles.**
Observed in one 24-hour window: `Reconcile CT-5 certificate currency`,
`Reconcile conditional-build latest result`, `Reconcile Pin14 T1 agenda state`,
`Reconcile typed carrier certificate surface`, `Reconcile Lean certificate
surface`, `Reconcile LT-GR8 D6 agenda currency`. Six maintenance commits, zero
new physics. The loop is not malfunctioning — it is doing exactly what its
selection rule rewards. **The selection rule is the defect.**

**2.2 Maintenance work creates the maintenance work.** Each swing writes an
artifact, a registry, a probe, and propagates to `CURRENT-STATE.yaml`,
`NEXT-STEPS.md`, and `RESEARCH-AGENDA.json`. Every one of those six surfaces is
a future staleness site, and pointer repair is itself a legal next swing. The
loop is partially a perpetual-motion machine fed by its own exhaust.

**2.3 Concurrency is handled by luck.** Three write-races in one day, resolved
by polling and waiting. There is no lease, no lane partition, and no declared
ownership of shared surfaces — `CURRENT-STATE.yaml` is written by every lane.
A cheap fix exists (partition by surface, or a lease file) and nobody owns it.

## Lens 3 — Working Physicist

**3.1 Nobody is deriving anything.** The last five Lean gates — L7, L8, L9, T3
and L10 — are finite algebra and repository hygiene. They are correct, they are
cheap, and they move no physics: the source-to-proof delta was unchanged by all
five, which is exactly why the delta instrument was created. Meanwhile the two
hard constructive questions (a stationary background; a positive physical
pairing) have had no serious attempt since the K-series.

**3.2 The owner-exhaustion result is being treated as a stop rather than a
result.** CBRS-1 ran roughly thirty candidate classes to owner-exhaustion. That
is a strong finding — it says the released material cannot supply a first
action of that shape. The correct response is a **necessity theorem** ("any
admissible owner must supply X, Y, Z, therefore either the source has an
unreleased term or the program needs a declared import"), which converts thirty
negative results into one positive structural statement. Instead the lane sits
parked waiting for a candidate that the same thirty results suggest will not
arrive.

**3.3 The physics is gated on one object and the program acts as if it has
seven.** The owner ledger's OWNER-B, D, E and F all presuppose OWNER-A: you
cannot have a stationary solution, a well-posed reduction, a matter coupling,
or a positivity structure for an action you do not have. Presenting them as a
seven-item scorecard makes the situation look more tractable and more
parallelizable than it is. *(Dissent noted against Lens 6, which values the
scorecard.)*

## Lens 4 — Epistemics / Verification Auditor

**4.1 The probes check prose, not physics.** In this session alone three
distinct probe defect classes surfaced *in probes I wrote*: wrapped-prose
substring checks that silently fail on reflow, crash-not-detection where a
mutation raises `KeyError` instead of failing a check, and a predicate
satisfiable by its own negation (`"conditional" in text` passes on
`"unconditional"`). The repository's `VERIFICATION.md` already documents this
family from earlier incidents. It keeps recurring because probe authorship has
no checklist and no linter.

**4.2 Green gates are being read as evidence of progress.** A swing ends with
`PASS n/n` and a clean gate sweep. That certifies the artifact is
self-consistent and propagated — it certifies nothing about whether the work
mattered. The delta instrument was the first attempt to separate these, and it
is one day old.

**4.3 The strongest negative results were mis-typed for ten days and nobody
noticed.** All three `GENUINE_FALSIFICATION` verdicts targeted claims the
source does not make; the gate that detects this was installed on 2026-08-13,
reported the exact problem in its own docstring, and its ratchet sat undriven
until today. **A gate nobody acts on is worse than no gate: it converts a known
defect into a permanent one with a green-looking process around it.**

## Lens 5 — Information Architect

**5.1 Surfaces rot because maintenance is manual and per-artifact.** The
requirements spec was maintained by hand-written status notes; that discipline
lapsed and six weeks of build-wave content went uncarried. The Lean certificate
inventory had the same failure and was fixed on 2026-08-22 by *deriving* the
inventory from the entrypoint instead of hand-maintaining it. **That fix is the
template and it has not been generalized.** Every hand-maintained list in this
repository is a scheduled outage.

**5.2 Artifact frontmatter has become unreadable.** Several `title:` fields run
to several hundred words with embedded verdicts, grades, and caveats. They are
unparseable by humans and only accidentally parseable by machines. The
information belongs in typed fields.

**5.3 Six propagation surfaces per swing is at least three too many.**
`CURRENT-STATE.yaml`, `NEXT-STEPS.md`, `RESEARCH-AGENDA.json`, a registry, a
probe, and often a delta. Most carry overlapping prose that must be kept
consistent by hand. This is the write-amplification tax on every piece of
science, and it is paid whether the science is large or trivial.

## Lens 6 — Portfolio Economist

**6.1 Cost per ledger point is not measured, so allocation is vibes.** Nobody
knows whether a Lean kernel, a benchmark descent, or a typing sweep buys more
movement per hour. Today's ranking was the first attempt and it is an estimate,
not a measurement.

**6.2 The cheap column is nearly exhausted and that is good news badly
handled.** Of 28 `NEEDS`, 21 are `MISSING_CONSTRUCTION`; only seven are small.
Once the cheap upgrades are done — kill typing (done today), stale premises,
small needs — the program will have nothing left that is both cheap and real.
That moment should be *planned for* as the point where the program either
attacks OWNER-A seriously or admits it is waiting on external input. Arriving
there unplanned will produce another month of maintenance.

**6.3 The scorecard is worth keeping and Lens 3 is wrong to disparage it.**
*(Dissent, held.)* Even if OWNER-A gates the others, naming five downstream
owners is what makes it possible to say "this result discharges nothing" and
to price a proposal before doing it. The dependency structure is a reason to
sequence the scorecard, not to discard it.

## Lens 7 — Source Custodian

**7.1 The binding constraint is input supply, and the program will not say
so.** CBRS-1 owner-exhaustion, `SA-G13`'s required-independent-assumption, the
`SC-CHI-51` bounded exclusion, and 21 `MISSING_CONSTRUCTION` rows all point the
same way: the released material does not contain a first action. Every artifact
carefully says "unbuilt" and none says "unbuildable from what we have."
**Those are different statements and the program has evidence for the second.**

**7.2 The source register is used defensively, not generatively.** It is
consulted to check that kills target real claims — correctly, as today showed.
It is never mined for claims that have *no* corresponding ledger row, which
would generate research rather than police it. There are 108 registered claims
and 85 ledger targets, and nobody has computed the difference.

**7.3 No decision procedure exists for declaring an import.** The posture
document explicitly permits a constrained external datum as a success mode.
`OWNER-G` now names four such data. But there is no gate, no council trigger,
and no criterion for when the program stops trying to derive something and
declares it imported. Without one, every candidate stays open forever at zero
cost, which is precisely why CBRS-1 has thirty of them.

## Lens 8 — Adversary

**8.1 This session is itself the failure mode it keeps diagnosing.** Twelve
artifacts, six of them audits of other artifacts, two of them audits of audits.
Today produced one repaired coverage gap, one restored currency set, one typing
ratchet — real, and all of it bookkeeping. **An outside reader would not be
able to tell this program from a well-run documentation project.**

**8.2 The delta instrument may become the next thing to game.** It was created
one day ago to prevent stable-proof work from masquerading as progress. Nothing
prevents a future swing from writing a delta statement that technically
"moves" by adding a demand, and the instrument's own probe checks that the
delta *exists*, not that it moved. Predict the failure now and design against
it.

**8.3 The most likely true state is that GU cannot be advanced further
without Weinstein.** Every independent line — action ownership, boundary law,
positivity, chirality realization — terminates in unreleased material. The
program's honest terminal move may be to write the exhaustion result and
publish it, rather than to keep finding cheaper work. *(Strong dissent from
Lens 6.2, which regards this as premature, and from Lens 3.2, which regards the
necessity theorem as the unattempted middle path.)*

## Action plan — prioritized, not averaged

Ordering is by expected effect on the program's ability to produce a real
result, not by cost. Dissents are preserved inline; refusals are explicit.

**A1. Change the hourly loop's selection rule so maintenance cannot be
first-choice.** *(Lens 2.1, 1.2; supported by 1.1, 4.2.)* Require every
scheduled run to attempt, in order: (i) an arc that moves the source-to-proof
delta; (ii) an arc that moves a ledger row; (iii) only then maintenance — and
when it selects maintenance, require it to record which of (i) and (ii) it
tried and why each was unavailable. This converts "no newly enabled arc" from
an unexamined conclusion into a logged claim that can be audited and
falsified.

**A2. Attempt the CBRS-1 necessity theorem.** *(Lens 3.2; the unattempted
middle path between 7.1/8.3 and continued candidate grinding.)* Convert thirty
owner-exhaustion results into one structural statement of what any admissible
owner must supply. Outcome is valuable either way: a theorem, or a precise
demonstration that the released material cannot contain one — which is what
Lens 7.1 says the program already knows but will not write.

**A3. Derive the hand-maintained lists.** *(Lens 5.1.)* Generalize the
2026-08-22 Lean-inventory fix: any list that enumerates repository contents
should be computed from those contents, not hand-written. Start with the
requirements-spec source set, whose lapse cost six weeks of currency.

**A4. Write the probe-authorship checklist and lint it.** *(Lens 4.1.)* Four
rules, all learned the hard way and all mechanical: normalize whitespace before
prose checks; never index into possibly-mutated structures without a default;
never write a predicate satisfiable by its own negation; assert your own
contribution plus non-regression, never global counters. A linter over
`tests/channel-swings/` can enforce three of the four.

**A5. Compute the register-minus-ledger difference.** *(Lens 7.2.)* 108
registered source claims against 85 ledger targets. Claims with no
corresponding row are either out of scope or unexamined research questions,
and nobody knows which. This is the one item on this list that *generates*
work rather than tidying it.

**A6. Write the import decision procedure.** *(Lens 7.3.)* State the criterion
under which a datum moves from "to be derived" to "declared import," with a
council trigger. `OWNER-G` already names the four candidates.

**A7. Plan the cheap-column exhaustion point now.** *(Lens 6.2.)* Name, in
advance, what the program does when the small `NEEDS`, stale premises and
typing sweeps are gone. Deciding it now is a five-minute act; deciding it in
the moment has historically produced a month of reconciles.

**A8. Harden the delta instrument against its own gaming.** *(Lens 8.2.)*
Require the delta statement to name which of its five components changed and
against which prior delta, so "moved" is checkable rather than asserted.

### Explicitly refused

- **Do not reduce the propagation surfaces** *(against Lens 5.3.)* The write
  amplification is real, but every one of those surfaces has caught a defect
  this week, and the correct fix is derivation (A3), not deletion. Refused as
  stated.
- **Do not shorten the frontmatter titles as a project** *(against Lens 5.2.)*
  Correct diagnosis, near-zero effect on velocity, high churn across hundreds
  of artifacts, and it would touch surfaces other lanes are writing. Do it
  opportunistically when an artifact is edited anyway.
- **Do not write the terminal exhaustion result yet** *(against Lens 8.3.)*
  A2 is the test that distinguishes "cannot be advanced without Weinstein" from
  "has not been attacked structurally." Publishing exhaustion before attempting
  the necessity theorem would be the same premature-closure error the program
  criticizes in others. **If A2 fails, 8.3 becomes the recommendation and
  should be executed without further delay.**

- **Lens 3.3 vs Lens 6.3** on whether the seven-owner scorecard clarifies or

  stated alongside it.
- **Lens 8.3 vs Lens 6.2 and 3.2** on whether the program is already at its
