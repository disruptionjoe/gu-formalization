## 3. Exact finite micro-world

The probe constructs a deliberately tiny constraint world:

```text
state = (three binary representative coordinates, one setting in {0,1,2})
symmetry = independent flips of the three binary coordinates
```

There are exactly 24 solutions, partitioned into three symmetry orbits of
eight representatives. The setting chooses an orbit; three external bits
choose one representative inside it. No representative is fixed by the full
flip group. This is the smallest useful executable picture of “dial chooses a
moduli component; tiebreaker chooses at a symmetry.”

Two hostile controls make the model discriminating:

1. a selector that returns the first enumerated solution changes when list
   order is reversed, exposing solver order as an undeclared input; and
2. a planted history-dependent `UPDATE` port is rejected as a third type,
   while nonbinary and unwitnessed mutations also fail.

The model establishes neither GU's actual solution variety nor a learning
process. It shows what a falsifiable interface test must detect.

## 4. Supplied split versus invariant kernel

Take the exact rational family

```text
Gamma(x,y) = x,
j_t(s) = (s,t s),
P_t(x,y) = (0,y-t x).
```

For all `t`, `Gamma j_t = 1`, `P_t` is a projector onto `ker Gamma`, and every
kernel point is fixed. But for `s != t`, `P_s != P_t`, with

```text
P_s - P_t = (j_t-j_s) Gamma.
```

This independently reproduces the live general theorem. It gives the sharp
question the port audit must answer: is `j_B` merely a representative choice
whose induced physical quotient is invariant, or does a prediction depend on
the supplied complement? The former removes it from the physical interface;
the latter exposes a real unowned input. Algebra alone does not decide.

## 6. Krein and learning inference corrections

The 96 opposite-signature mirror pairs remain a mathematically interesting
pre-collision configuration. A Hamiltonian-Hopf quartet shows capacity to
leave a symmetric state. It does not show where the system lands, that it
stays there, that one branch is preferred, or that any distribution over
branches exists. Stabilization additionally needs a selected generator plus
nonlinear saturation, damping/attraction or another landing theorem.

Likewise, a residual-square action is a least-squares form, not evidence of
learning by itself. A learning interpretation additionally requires at least
trainable or hidden variables, feedback/evidence, an update law with an arrow,
and held-out generalization or a rival discriminator. Those objects are not
constructed here.
