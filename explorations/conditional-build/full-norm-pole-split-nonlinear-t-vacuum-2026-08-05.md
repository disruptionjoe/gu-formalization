## 3. Conditional full-norm action placement

On either inherited TT polarization, the predecessor action was

\[
 L_0(h,v)=z h v+\frac{\kappa_1}{2}v^2+\tau h.
\]

The conditional full-norm horn contributes the direct Gauss/Einstein term

\[
 \frac{\alpha_{II}}{2}z h^2.
\]

Here `alpha_II` is the already-charged full-norm coefficient. It is not a new
fit. The quadratic field matrix is

\[
 J_{II}(z)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \alpha_{II}z & z\\
 z & \kappa_1
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det J_{II}=z(\alpha_{II}\kappa_1-z).
\]

For nonzero `alpha_II*kappa_1`, this has two distinct simple factors. The
metric response is

\[
 (J_{II}^{-1})_{hh}
 =\frac{\kappa_1}{z(\alpha_{II}\kappa_1-z)}
 =\frac1{\alpha_{II}z}
 +\frac1{\alpha_{II}(\alpha_{II}\kappa_1-z)}.
\]

Therefore:

- `z=0` is a simple massless Einstein pole with residue `1/alpha_II`;
- `z=alpha_II*kappa_1` is a distinct simple partner; and
- setting the direct Gauss/Einstein term to zero recovers `-kappa_1/z^2`, the
  predecessor's coincident double pole.

Eliminating `v` gives the same result directly:

\[
 E_h^{\rm eff}=\tau+\frac{h z}{\kappa_1}
 (\alpha_{II}\kappa_1-z).
\]

The inherited harmonic constraints and gauge quotient do not change because
the new factor multiplies each already-isolated TT polarization. Thus the
exact `10 -> 6 -> 2` carrier remains plus/cross. What changes is the operator
on each of those two modes.

## 4. Full nonlinear cyclic-transgression control

The already-owned finite noncommutative action grammar reduces to

\[
 I(x,y,q)=2(xyq+xy+xq+x+yq+y+q)
 +\frac12x^2-\frac12y^2+q^2.
\]

The mixed third derivative is exactly `2`, so the cubic is live. Its Euler
rows are

\[
\begin{aligned}
0&=x+2yq+2y+2q+2,\\
0&=2xq+2x-y+2q+2,\\
0&=2(xy+x+y+q+1).
\end{aligned}
\]

Set `a=2(q+1)`. The first two rows solve uniquely over the reals as

\[
x=-\frac{a(1+a)}{1+a^2},\qquad
y=\frac{a(1-a)}{1+a^2},\qquad
q=\frac a2-1.
\]

The final row becomes

\[
 \frac{a\,p(a)}{(1+a^2)^2}=0,
 \quad
 p(a)=a^4-2a^3+2a^2-6a+1.
\]

The `a=0` branch is `(x,y,q)=(0,0,-1)`. Exact Sturm/root-isolation gives
exactly two real roots of `p`, one in `(1/6,1/5)` and one in `(2,9/4)`; the
other two roots are non-real. Both real quartic branches have `x*y*q != 0`.
The resultant of `p` with the cubic-pairing numerator is `144`, so the cubic
pairing is nonzero on either branch. The `T wedge T` sector is therefore
genuinely live.

The Hessian determinant is also nonzero on the quartic branches: the exact
resultant is `-5439488`. But the Hessian has fixed diagonal directions `+1`
and `-1` everywhere. All these stationary branches are indefinite; the two
genuinely nonlinear branches are nondegenerate saddles.
