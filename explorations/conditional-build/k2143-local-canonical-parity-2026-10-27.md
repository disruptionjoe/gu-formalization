## 1. A genuinely different parity object

observable. We construct the parity of one local CANONICAL oscillator and
price its response in the actual global vacuum. This is a supplied positive
Hilbert scalar model, not GU grading, fermion parity or global particle parity.

Take the massive real scalar on R, omega=sqrt(-partial_x²+m²), m>0,
and real f in C_c^infinity(R) with ||f||_2=1. Use a unitary Fourier transform
for all inner products below and natural units. Pick an oscillator scale nu>0:

    q=sqrt(nu) phi(f), p=pi(f)/sqrt(nu), [q,p]=i,
    N_f=(q²+p²-1)/2, V=exp(i pi N_f).                              (1)

The regular one-mode CCR representation is an oscillator representation with
multiplicity. Thus the oscillator expression has a selfadjoint nonnegative closure on its
canonical Schwartz core, denoted N_f, V=V*=V^-1, and V
flips q,p. Parity is independent of nu: changing nu squeezes that oscillator
and parity commutes with squeezing. This statement concerns the parity,
not equality of the different N_f generators.

The bounded V belongs to the von Neumann algebra generated by the local
Weyl operators exp(i s phi(f)) and exp(i t pi(f)). It commutes with Cauchy
observables supported outside supp f. There is no factorization assumption
about the global vacuum, and a local algebra containing an oscillator
subalgebra need not itself be a type-I factor.

## 4. Exact finite free-field energy price

    beta =(omega^(1/2) R omega^(-1/2)-omega^(-1/2) R omega^(1/2))/2.

Complex conjugation is understood in beta's antilinear action. Smooth f and
m>0 make alpha a bounded finite-rank perturbation of I and beta bounded finite

Hilbert--Schmidt, so the transformation is unitarily implementable. The local
CCR implementation above fixes its phase as oscillator parity. Write
u1=omega^(1/2)f, v1=omega^(-1/2)f. Then

    beta=|v1><u1|-|u1><v1|,
    ||beta||_HS²=2(a_-1 a_1-1),
    E(V vacuum)=Tr(omega beta beta*)=a_-1 a_2-a_1.                 (4)

To verify the last formula, the two diagonal terms contribute a_1 and
a_-1 a_2, and the two cross terms each subtract a_1, since <u1,v1>=1
and <u1,omega v1>=a_1. All moments are finite for smooth compact f.
The energy is nonnegative because it is a weighted Hilbert--Schmidt norm;
it is strictly positive in this continuum setting. The unread measured
