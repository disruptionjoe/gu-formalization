## 1. The finite extension has a unique center and shape

For a Hermitian extension on a `d`-dimensional boundary channel write

```text
W = E I + W_0,       E=tr(W)/d,       tr(W_0)=0.       (1)
```

The decomposition is unique. On a fixed physical carrier and fixed K139 chart
`S=U^-1`, the scalar physical identity pulls back as

```text
S* (E I) S = E M,       M=S*S.                         (2)
```

This is exactly K166. By contrast, a general traceless shape contributes a
form perturbation `S* W_0 S`, which need not be proportional to `M`. Hence no
translation argument can remove it from residuals, gaps or complements.

## 2. Traceless shape changes a matched residual and relative gap

Use the exact fixed chart

```text
S=diag(2,1,1),  M=diag(4,1,1),  H_0=diag(-1,2,5).      (3)
```

Let `u=e_1` and perturb the physical operator by

```text
D_off=[[0,1,0],[1,0,0],[0,0,0]],   tr(D_off)=0.        (4)
```

At parameters `t=0` and `t=2`, the Rayleigh value of `u` stays `-1`, but the
matched regular-coordinate residual changes exactly from

```text
(R_0+M)u=(0,0,0)  to  (R_2+M)u=(0,4,0).               (5)
```

The generalized spectra are respectively

```text
(-1,2,5)  and  (-2,3,5),                              (6)
```

so the ground-to-next gap changes from `3` to `5`. The scalar center preserves
both quantities; the traceless shape does not.

