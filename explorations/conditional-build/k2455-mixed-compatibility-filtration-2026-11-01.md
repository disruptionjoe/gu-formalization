## Result and scope

The full mixed compatibility identities do not hold separately on every
passive-degree coefficient of an original compatible forcing. An explicit
original degree-four field below proves the distinction. K2452's fixed
passive-degree inverse survives, because its induction solves the highest
remaining degree of the residual, not every uncorrected input coefficient.

There is also a universal limitation on extending K2452's scalar primitive:
no finite fixed bank of constant hooks, each multiplied by a scalar function
and each individually ordinary-null, can solve arbitrary fourteen-variable
scalar forcing at all degrees. This removes that particular shortcut to
unrestricted analytic inversion. It leaves the full joint inverse open:
ordinary responses may cancel between generators, or hooks may change with
degree. The result does not refute the conditional formal solution.

## 1. Every nonzero constant hook has a proper ordinary-null covector space

Use the original conventions

    h_ijk=-h_ikj, h_ijk+h_jki+h_kij=0,
    (A_xi h)_ij=sum_a eta_a xi_a (h_iaj+h_jai).

If A_xi h vanishes for every xi, coordinate covectors give h_iaj=-h_jai.
Together with last-two antisymmetry this makes h fully alternating.
The cyclic identity then gives 3h=0, so h=0 in characteristic zero.
Hence for any nonzero hook

    S_h=ker(xi -> A_xi h), dim S_h<=13.                  (1)

For a scalar polynomial f, D(hf)=A_df h. In coordinates extending a basis
of S_h the complementary symbol columns are independent. Thus D(hf)=0
forces every complementary derivative of f to vanish. Conversely dependence
only on S_h makes the ordinary response zero. In invariant language,

    D(hf)=0 iff f belongs to Sym(S_h).                  (2)

Here S_h is a subspace of covectors, used as linear coordinate functions;
it is not the subspace obtained by evaluating an arbitrary field at W=0.

## 2. Finite-bank exclusion at all degrees

Fix any M nonzero constant hooks h_j, independent of polynomial degree.
For scalar receiver degree n, each coefficient f_j has degree n+1.
Require D(h_j f_j)=0 separately for each j. By (1)--(2), the sum of the
available input dimensions is at most

    M*binom(n+13,12).

The full fourteen-variable scalar receiver has dimension binom(n+13,13).
The target-to-upper-domain ratio is (n+1)/(13M). Therefore whenever

    n+1>13M,                                           (3)

the scalar output cannot be surjective. This is dimension obstruction for
this specified linear family, not parameter counting on an unspecified
physical model. Jstar is the original degree-lowering scalar map and the
actual factor 4/(n+1) is nonzero, so neither can increase rank. Repeated-polar
restrictions, the boundary ideal E=0, or overlaps between summand images only
reduce the available dimension. The conclusion holds even if the chosen
hooks have real or complex coefficients, in characteristic zero.

K2452's particular h has dim S_h=11, giving the sharper bound
M*binom(n+11,10) when every selected hook has that kernel dimension.
This sharper statement is conditional on each kernel bound, not a claim
that every original hook has rank three.

**Internal verdict:** the finite fixed individually ordinary-null bank route
to arbitrary scalar forcing is excluded by (3). The full joint system is
not excluded. In particular D(sum h_j f_j)=0 does not imply each term is
ordinary-null. The same degreewise argument applies even if the hooks change with n:
surjectivity requires at least ceil((n+1)/13) individually ordinary-null
summands at degree n. A uniformly bounded number cannot escape by changing
hooks. With every kernel of dimension at most 11, the necessary number is
at least ceil(binom(n+13,13)/binom(n+11,10)), of order n^3.
An unbounded-size degree-dependent bank is not excluded by the fixed-M theorem.
Neither analytic divergence nor nonlinear nonexistence follows.

## 3. Original mixed compatibility counterfixture

Take the nonzero tracefree repeated hook

    h_5,5,1=1, h_5,1,5=-1,
    h_6,6,1=-1, h_6,1,6=1.

All omitted coefficients vanish. Direct original K-bank polarization is
zero. The only possibly overlapping repeated H0 coefficients agree at
indices 5 and 6, since both have lambda=-15/28 and owner coefficient3/7;
their combination is -15/168+(27/14)/70+(3/7)/420=-17/280.
Thus the two contributions cancel. The exact probe checks the full bank,
hook cyclic identity, trace and sigma condition, rather than only this
explanation.

Actual adapted covectors are dy2=e5, dy3=e6, dz0=-e1. Their symbols are

    A_dy2 h: F15=1,
    A_dy3 h: F16=-1,
    A_dz0 h: F55=-2, F66=2.

The allowed field V=h*y2^3*z0 has degree 4 and vanishes on E=0. Its ordinary
image has precisely

    F55=-2*y2^3, F66=2*y2^3, F15=3*y2^2*z0.              (4)

Its trace is zero. In the full double divergence the diagonal term is
-12*y2 and the off-diagonal15 term is +12*y2, so compatibility holds.
The second term uses both the actual dz0=-e1 sign and the raw symmetric
factor 2. Dropping either gives a nonzero residual.

But the passive-degree-zero coefficient F0 contains just the diagonal terms,
and C_EE F0=-12*y2!=0. Thus full compatibility does not imply coefficientwise
transverse compatibility. The highest passive-degree coefficient F1 does
satisfy its leading transverse compatibility. This fixture addresses the
actual original operator, not an abstract triangular surrogate.

## 4. Correct residual induction and what still needs an estimate

Write L=L0+N as in K2452, with N strictly lowering passive degree, and let
P_r select degree r. Let E be a fully compatible residual with maximal
passive degree r. The degree-r part of its full compatibility equation
C_EE E+C_EW E+C_WW E=0 is exactly C_EE P_r E=0;
terms with W derivatives lower the degree. The trace equation behaves the
same way. Solve V_r=D P_r E on the transverse compatible range, subtract
L V_r, and repeat. Subtraction of a full original image preserves full
compatibility and removes the leading degree. There are at most m+1 steps
for passive bound m, including boundary-ideal restrictions of the original
transverse inverse.

For one fixed blockwise off-range linear extension D, the resulting V
satisfies V=D(F-NV), so the finite Neumann expression in K2452 is valid.
Its individual algebraic summands may use that extension on off-range inputs;
they are not claims that each original coefficient F_r is compatible.
The previously proved m-dependent bound remains unchanged. Neither this
induction nor (3) supplies the missing geometric estimate uniform in m.

A concrete quantitative successor is to estimate the actual joint transfers
between adjacent passive/transverse levels, retaining both one- and two-W
lowering terms and their directional denominators. Binomial reweighting
alone is not such a proof: the two-W derivative term has two degree factors.
Alternatively use the full K2449 adjoint to prove a full-bank dual obstruction.
An inverse norm on a chosen subspace is not a lower bound for all choices.
