## Why this exists

CBRS-1 has run twenty-seven candidate classes to owner-exhaustion and the lane
sits parked awaiting a candidate that those same twenty-seven results suggest
will not arrive. The velocity council's Lens 3.2 named the alternative: an
exhaustion corpus of that size is *evidence about the shape of the problem*,
and converting it into a necessity statement turns twenty-seven negative
results into one positive structural claim plus a sharp question. This is that
conversion, and it is the council's designated discriminator: its outcome
determines whether the terminal-exhaustion write-up is warranted or premature.

## The theorem (classification grade)

**Let `P` be any candidate first-action owner packet admissible under the
CBRS-1 owner-before-evaluation rule. Then `P` must simultaneously satisfy:**

- **N1 — point closure.** Satisfy the complete point/field system: translation,
  spin-connection, and primitive-epsilon equations.
- **N2 — metric stationarity.** Satisfy the full intrinsic `MET(X)` variation,
  not merely the point equations.
- **N3 — non-orbit tangent.** Its complete Hessian kernel must strictly exceed
  the gauge orbit; equivalently a nonzero primitive/metric-admissible non-orbit
  quotient must exist.
- **N4 — primitive momentum closure.** The primitive-epsilon momentum
  divergence must vanish on the admitted field content.
- **N5 — globalization.** The pointwise/constant reconstruction must lift to a
  nonhomogeneous spacetime solution.
- **N6 — target-blind action ownership.** Every coefficient must be owned by
  the released action and frozen before target evaluation: no density-fitted
  closure, no unowned normalization.

**Each condition is individually binding**, in the precise sense that the
corpus records a candidate which satisfies the conditions upstream of it and
fails exactly that one:

Because each condition has such a witness, **none may be dropped**: a demand
specification that omits any one of the six admits a candidate the corpus has
already closed.

## The corollary that matters — the N2/N6 tension

`CBRS-1Y` is the sharpest datum in the corpus, and it is sharp precisely
because it is the one candidate that *succeeded* at the condition everything
else failed. Within its class the metric system **closed** — and it closed
**only** through a density-keyed second jet, that is, only by encoding the
target. `two_density_universality` is recorded `False`: the closure is not
universal across densities, it is fitted to one.

So the corpus contains a recorded instance in which **N2 and N6 are in direct
tension**: the only route to metric closure found in that class ran through a
violation of target-blindness. `CBRS-1Z` then failed target-blind primitive
owner admission for two further candidates, and `CBRS-1AA`/`CBRS-1AB` closed
the remaining normalization routes for lack of an owned coefficient.

**The open question, stated exactly:** is the `N2 ∧ N6` tension *generic* —
is metric closure within the released material achievable only by
target-keyed data — or is `CBRS-1Y` a feature of its particular one-function
radial class? Nothing in the corpus answers this, and it is the question whose
answer decides the lane.

## What this does and does not establish

**Establishes.** (i) Six jointly necessary conditions on any admissible owner
packet, each with a named binding witness. (ii) A demand specification a future
candidate must discharge in full, which makes candidates prosecutable before
they are built rather than after. (iii) That the twenty-seven closes are not a
scatter of unrelated failures but a structured obstruction set with a single
sharp tension at its centre.

**Does not establish.** (i) **Sufficiency** — nothing here says a packet
satisfying N1–N6 exists or would be admissible on further grounds. (ii) That
the released material cannot supply an owner; that is the open question, not a
result. (iii) Any verdict, canon, or claim-status movement — none occurs.

Note also what this does *not* license: the necessity conditions are
extracted from the recorded corpus and inherit its scope. Each witness carries
its own conditions (frozen class, branch, signature horn), and a candidate
built on a materially different carrier or real form may escape a condition by
changing the object rather than solving the problem — in which case
`PD-STRUCTURE-TRANSPORT` applies and the escape must be typed, not asserted.

The velocity council left Lens 8.3 (the program is at its terminus without

  candidate grind would have.
- The terminal-exhaustion write-up remains **refused**, and now for a stated

  is open.

**The lane's next question is therefore no longer "find another candidate."
It is: prove or refute that `N2 ∧ N6` is generically unsatisfiable.** That is
a materially different and much better-posed target than the one CBRS-1 has
been carrying, and it is reachable without waiting on new source material,
because it is a question *about* the released material rather than a request
for more of it.

## Route selection and hostile review

The census ran a classification lens (are the six families exhaustive over the
corpus, and is each witness correctly isolated), a claim-indexed lens (the
theorem targets an internal admission rule, not a source claim, and is typed
`NONE-NOT-A-KILL`), a scope lens (necessity conditions inherit the scope of
the witnesses that establish them; the `PD-STRUCTURE-TRANSPORT` caveat is
stated), an economics lens (does this beat another candidate grind — yes, it
reposes the lane's question), and a hostile reviewer.

The strongest attack is that "necessity conditions extracted from failures"
is just a restatement of the failures with a theorem's grammar. It is more
than that in one specific way: the **individual bindingness** claim is not a
restatement, because it required finding, for each condition, a witness that
passed the others — and two of those witnesses (`CBRS-1I` and `CBRS-1Y`) are
partial *successes* whose value was invisible while the corpus was read as a
list of kills. The second attack is that the N2/N6 tension is an
over-reading of one class; that is conceded and is exactly why the tension is
recorded as an open question rather than a result. The third is that this
should have applied the conditions to close CBRS-1 outright; it must not,
because necessity is not sufficiency and closing a lane on necessary
conditions alone would be the inverse of the error this repository polices.

No scientific ledger verdict, canon, source ownership, prediction credit, or
public posture changes.
