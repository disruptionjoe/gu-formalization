## Result

The corpus says what it is unusually clearly. Its front door calls it a
construction sandbox and says it “has not yet built” the source action. Its
`SPEC.md` names `S_IG` as the object to construct. The security-budget score is
explicitly a search lens, not a replacement action. This makes the first
custody distinction exact:

```text
git-tracked absorbed corpus = repository construction evidence
source-authenticated action packet = absent
completed coefficient-defined S_IG = absent
```

The existing work is still valuable. It supplies an explicit `Cl(9,5)`
finite-fiber arena, hard anti-import and anti-trap controls, a nonvacuous
finite-fiber BV/Koszul-Tate closure, a tau Schur-complement mechanism, a
necessary non-equivariant compensator route and boundary/families diagnostics.
But those pieces do not have one variational owner.

Against a twelve-object denominator, the corpus has no cell that qualifies as
a complete action. The action formula and coefficient table are absent; field,
constraint, BV and Noether data are partial; the complete Euler system,
coupled Hessian, formal adjoint, common closed operator domain, causal Green
form and three global families/boundary-Dirac objects are absent. The four
default buildbench rows are alternative candidate roles, not four terms that
may be unioned into an action.

The first non-admission locus is therefore before downstream physics:

```text
no real coefficient-complete action packet
  -> no complete field/constraint variation
  -> no action-owned Euler/Hessian/adjoint complex
  -> no selected common closed domain or Green form
```

This advances source custody by replacing the ambiguous label “absorbed source
action” with a typed status and an exact reopener. A future packet must supply
one complete density, fields and constraints, coefficients, all Euler/Noether
and BV maps, coupled Hessian/formal adjoint, common closed domain, causal Green
form and the global families/boundary-Dirac objects. Resemblance to one of the
scaffolded candidates is not enough.
