## 2. Classification theorem

**Theorem.** The field extension Phi tensor id_M has the above one-way A-to-B no-signalling property if and only if Phi is a random displacement channel:

    Phi(rho)=integral D(xi) rho D(xi)^* mu(dxi),                 (1)

where mu is a probability measure on R^2 and D are the same f-oscillator displacements. This is a classification of normal channels at the fixed Cauchy slice; it does not supply a relativistic Hamiltonian implementation.

**Proof, local Weyl reduction.** Write W(z)=exp(i(z_1 q+z_2 p)), with symplectic convention sigma(z,w)=z_1 w_2-z_2 w_1, so W(z)W(w)=exp(-i sigma(z,w)/2)W(z+w). Choose real u,g supported in A,B with <f,u> and <f,g> nonzero. Arbitrary position/momentum coefficients times u or g induce arbitrary oscillator labels a,b in R^2. A spatially supported full Weyl factorizes into its f-mode Weyl and symplectic-complement Weyl:

    U_A=W(a) tensor V_A,       W_B=W(b) tensor V_B.

The two full Weyls commute because A,B are disjoint. Hence the commutation phase of V_A,V_B cancels the oscillator phase exactly. Since Phi acts only on the oscillator,

    (Phi^* tensor id)(W_B)=Phi^*(W(b)) tensor V_B.

No-signalling says its conjugation by U_A is unchanged. Cancelling the same complement phase gives

    W(a)^* Phi^*(W(b)) W(a)
       =exp(i sigma(a,b)) Phi^*(W(b))                          (2)

for every a,b. The sign agrees with W(a)^* W(b) W(a)=exp(i sigma(a,b))W(b). Therefore Phi^*(W(b))W(b)^* commutes with all oscillator Weyls. Their irreducibility yields

    Phi^*(W(b))=chi(b)W(b),  chi(0)=1.                         (3)

**Proof, CP to classical noise.** Complete positivity makes the operator matrix [Phi^*(W(z_i)^* W(z_j))] positive for every finite list. By (3) its ij entry is chi(z_j-z_i)W(z_i)^*W(z_j). Conjugating this matrix by block diagonal diag(W(z_i)) gives [chi(z_j-z_i)I]>=0. Thus chi is an ordinary positive-definite function. It is continuous: its vacuum characteristic numerator Tr(Phi(|0><0|)W(z)) is continuous for a trace-class output, and its denominator <0|W(z)|0>=exp(-|z|^2/4) never vanishes. The classical Bochner theorem gives a probability measure with chi(z)=integral exp(i sigma(xi,z))mu(dxi). Choosing the displacement convention to reproduce this character yields (1); equality on Weyls extends to all bounded operators by normality and irreducibility. This last standard Fourier theorem is the only named representation theorem beyond Stone--von Neumann used here.

**Converse.** A fixed global displacement generated by phi(f),pi(f) conjugates every local Weyl by a scalar phase. It does not enlarge its spatial support. Averaging the same scalar classical random displacement therefore preserves every local algebra and cannot signal.
