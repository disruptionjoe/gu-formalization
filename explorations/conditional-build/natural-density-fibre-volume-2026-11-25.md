## 2. Absolute versus relative scalar naturality

Use congruence C_A(y)=A^T y A. A scalar rho(g,y) is natural here when rho(C_A g,C_A y)=rho(g,y) for all A in GL(V). Let f=log rho. Positivity is crucial for this global real logarithm.

Without an observing/reference metric, every zero-order natural scalar f(y) is constant: GL(V) acts transitively on the fixed-signature metric fibre. Hence its vertical derivative is zero. This does **not** say no natural absolute density exists. The pseudo-Riemannian vertical metric has a canonical positive absolute volume. Coordinate coefficients of a density, such as powers of det y times dy, are not scalar functions. The assertion applies to the scalar ratio of two same-type natural densities.

With an observing metric, simultaneous congruence reduces the isotropy to O(g), and nonconstant scalars exist. The known example

    f0(g,y) = -1/2 log |det(g^-1 y)|

is globally smooth. Both determinants have the same sign, so the ratio is positive; absolute value also expresses the convention without choosing an orientation. No logarithm branch, Spin structure, density-line trivialization, or fibre simple-connectedness is needed. This is prior existence, not a new repair.

## 3. All first normal jets, and the trace-reversal coefficient

At y=g, the vertical differential L_g(v)=D_y f(g,g)[v] is O(g)-invariant on Sym^2(V*). Exactly these linear functionals are

    L_g(v) = c tr(g^-1 v).

An elementary proof fixes g=diag(-1,1,1,1). Spatial rotations make an invariant dual symmetric tensor diag(a,b,b,b) and eliminate off-diagonal components; a boost in the 0-1 plane forces a=-b. Therefore only the inverse metric contraction remains. Congruence transitivity makes c independent of g. If f(g,g)=0, differentiating the diagonal identity additionally fixes D_g f(g,g)[h]=-c tr(g^-1 h). No invariant-theory claim about *all global smooth invariants* or diagonalizability of g^-1y is needed.

For general dimension n and nondegenerate trace-reversed metric

    K_lambda,y(u,v) = tr(y^-1 u y^-1 v)

    t_y=y,  K_lambda(t,v)=(1-n lambda) tr(y^-1v),

the same-metric connection reconstruction's Koszul identity is

    K_lambda(II(X,Y),v) = -v(X,Y)/2.

Tracing over the n horizontal directions gives

    K_lambda(H,v)=-1/2 tr(g^-1v),

Thus the condition grad_N f=H is equivalent to **c=-1/2**, independent of lambda, provided 1-n lambda is nonzero. For native n=4, lambda=1/2, H=t/2. The often-confused lambda=1 gives H=t/6 and does not match coefficients. Lambda=1/4 is degenerate and has no inverse-gradient statement.

## 4. What moving covariance does and does not select

## 5. New global theorem: no finite positive natural full-fibre mass

**Theorem.** Let g be Lorentzian on V. Let U be any nonempty O(g)-invariant open subset of the full metric fibre. Let mu be a smooth positive O(g)-invariant density on U. Then mu(U)=infinity. In particular any smooth positive zero-order natural rho(g,y) times the canonical absolute vertical volume has infinite mass. The same holds for the fibre disintegration of the stated ambient metric volume.

**Proof by disjoint boost translates.** Choose a nonzero g-null vector l and a Lorentz boost A with A l=k l, k>1, and det A=1. For the congruence action,

    q(y)=y(l,l),   q(C_A y)=k^2 q(y).

There is a relatively compact nonempty open set B in U on which a<q(y)<b with 0<a<b and b<k^2a, or the same inequality for -q. To see that such a point exists in any nonempty open U, q is a nonzero linear functional on Sym^2, so its zero hyperplane has empty interior. Restrict to a sufficiently small positive or negative interval and enlarge k if necessary. (For the full fibre near g, g+epsilon alpha tensor alpha with alpha(l) nonzero provides an explicit Lorentzian positive-q example for small epsilon.)

The sets C_A^j B, j=0,1,2,..., are pairwise disjoint because their q-intervals are (k^(2j)a,k^(2j)b). They stay in U by invariance and in the fixed-signature fibre by congruence. Invariance gives mu(C_A^j B)=mu(B), with 0<mu(B)<infinity from smooth positivity and relative compactness. Countable additivity proves infinite mass. The boost lies in the identity component, so orientation or time-orientation restrictions do not remove this argument. No orbit-volume decomposition, properness assumption, recurrence theorem, or inference from one measure-zero trajectory is used. QED.
