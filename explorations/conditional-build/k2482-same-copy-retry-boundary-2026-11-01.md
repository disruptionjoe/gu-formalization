## 1. Complete canonical instruments and exact limit

Let P be a rank-one target projector. For finitely many effects 0<=E_j<=I
with E_j P=P and fixed independent probabilities p_j>0 summing to1, define
accept maps rho -> sqrt(E_j) rho sqrt(E_j) and failure maps
rho -> K_j rho K_j, K_j=sqrt(I-E_j). Include the classical setting record;
ignoring it after each failure gives N(rho)=sum p_j K_j rho K_j. These are
complete CP instruments. They are supplied global implementations, not a
claim that an averaged effect has the original local realization.

Write Omega=sum p_j E_j and D for the projector onto intersection ker(E_j).
All the square roots reduce P and D, K_j P=0, K_j D=D. On Dperp, Omega has
minimum eigenvalue lambda>0. Thus

 D <= S_m=(N*)^m I <= D+(1-lambda)^m(I-D).

Proof: E_j>=0 implies ker Omega=intersection ker E_j. The one-step inequality
N*(I-D)=I-D-Omega <=(1-lambda)(I-D), with positivity, iterates; N*D=D.
K2481's telescoping formula gives output B_m=I-S_m. The bad accepted-output
effect is Omega-P on one attempt and N*P=0, so for every m>=1

 A_m=B_m-P;  B_infinity=I-D;  A_infinity=I-P-D.

For initial rho with nonzero bright mass, limiting output fidelity is
tr(P rho)/tr((I-D)rho). Every bright bad state outputs eventually with
probability1 and remains bad. Dark exclusion survives, not general fidelity
filtering. All-input eventual output is false when D!=0. The bright-sector
survival bound also gives expected attempt count at most1/lambda on normalized
bright inputs. A dark component has infinite waiting time without a cap.

Replacing all the label instruments by sqrt(Omega) and sqrt(I-Omega) has the
same limiting effects but can have different finite-cap effects. Do not use
(I-Omega)^m for S_m of the label-level instrument without proving equality.

## 4. Released repair: stop on first failure, require all passes

Keep the canonical target-preserving instruments, but stop and discard at the
first failure; accept only after m consecutive passes. Let
J(rho)=sum p_j sqrt(E_j) rho sqrt(E_j). If
Omega<=P+(1-nu)(I-P), then J*P=P and
J*(I-P)=Omega-P<=(1-nu)(I-P). Hence

 J*^m(I-P)<=(1-nu)^m(I-P),  J*^m P=P.

For initial fidelity F>0, output probability is F+x_m with
0<=x_m<=(1-nu)^m(1-F), and conditional infidelity is at most
(1-nu)^m(1-F)/[F+(1-nu)^m(1-F)]. At most m complete tests are performed.
