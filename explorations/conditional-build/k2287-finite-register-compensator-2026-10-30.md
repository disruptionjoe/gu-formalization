## 1. Modular charge computation replaces an unbounded square

Use Q=sum_(j=1)^20 N_j+2K and integer center m. Set D=2^q,L=D/2. An initially zero q-bit neutral register undergoes Hadamards, controlled phases exp[2pi i 2^j(Q-m)/D], and inverse discrete Fourier transform. For every integer total charge, including arbitrarily large Q, this computes

    y=(Q-m) mod D in {0,...,D-1}.

The m-dependent part is a KNOWN neutral phase; it is not an estimate of unknown error. With bit weights w_j=2^j for j<q-1 and w_(q-1)=-2^(q-1), define the signed residue s(y)=sum w_j b_j, in[-L,L). Apply

    exp[-i theta s(y)²]
     = product_j exp[-i theta w_j² b_j]
       product_(j<l) exp[-2i theta w_j w_l b_j b_l],  (1)

then reverse the charge computation. The register resets exactly in the ideal circuit, giving V_D=exp[-i theta s((Q-m) mod D)²]. This is equal to the desired unitary when |Q-m|<L. There is no physical charge cutoff, modular aliasing at large Q is retained and priced below.

The sign of the top weight is essential. Treating the residue as unsigned, forgetting a cross term, computing Q with pump weight1, or failing to uncompute changes the operation. These are actual circuit faults checked by the companion.

## 6. Followthrough: the modular phase is not an instantaneous stabilizer

A tempting extension fails: replacing C_m INSIDE the positive pump Hamiltonian by its bounded modular square does not preserve semiboundedness. Fix finite q,m,mu>0 and z!=0. In a two-field/pump occupation basis take u_n=|n,n,n> and v_n=|n+1,n+1,n-1>, n>=1, with other fields vacuum. Both have Q=4n. The pump H_z has zero diagonal there and off-diagonal magnitude |z|(n+1)sqrt(n)/sqrt(mu). A relative phase makes its Rayleigh expectation the negative of that magnitude.

For a proposed scalar shift f(Q)=gamma[s((Q-m) modD)²+d]+aQ+c, with fixed gamma>=0 and finite constants, s²<=L² gives

    <H_z+f(Q)> <=gamma(L²+d)+c+4an
                         -|z|(n+1)sqrt(n)/sqrt(mu) -> -infinity.

The two-state span is a COMPRESSION, not an invariant full shell; a finite-particle Rayleigh sequence suffices for this conclusion. Even an additional fixed linear stabilizer cannot dominate the n^(3/2) term. Thus this direct substitution fails globally, although the final UNITARY compiler is valid on all charges with the stated tail error. Other full-pump synthesis schemes remain open. This removes a false shortcut rather than claiming general hardware impossibility.

The actual remaining positive pump primitive B_z is the strongest next synthesis target; replacing it must preserve instantaneous semiboundedness and the full instrument, not merely approximate its finite shell action.
