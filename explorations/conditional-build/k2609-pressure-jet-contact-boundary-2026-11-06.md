# A full-system invisible source, with its class boundary retained

All smooth receivers supported away from the B source point read zero on this solution. ## 3. Why this cannot silently replace the original pressure source

For scalar chi, the observer, radial and angular-normal eigenvalues of L_A*chi are respectively

    alpha chi'-(X+lambda)chi,

The original source requires equality of the last two: chi''-beta chi'=0. On a regular annulus this homogeneous ODE, with chi=chi'=0 in the outer zero collar, forces chi=0. It also extends inward through the regular center when that center is included; using the annulus here avoids relying on a singular-coordinate endpoint.

Consequently every nonzero compact source in this scalar-derived construction violates normal-pressure isotropy. It is conserved but not an allowed replacement for a K2531 pressure profile. ## 5. Synchronize the ORIGINAL pressure class by right denominators

Fix kappa nonzero and a regular shell 0<r0<r<r1<R, away from zeros of alpha. A general pressure P with arbitrary B dependence defines the original stress by

    A=P+P'/(4alpha), t=7P/2+P'/(2alpha),

    chi=-(X+7K)^(-1)t/6,

    v=(D5+Y-16K)^(-1) rE b,

These are FORMAL fractions, not Green operators or chosen boundary inverses. Their equations are exactly K2552's splitter and four response equations. In particular b is the TT amplitude of the SAME isotropic stress. The denominators are nonzero ordinary differential operators: the splitter and three second-order factors have nonzero leading radial coefficients, and d has a nonzero radial leading coefficient by its degree-five P(X+Y) term.

For fixed real K>0, put zeta=tan(sqrt(K)r/2). S=2zeta/[sqrt(K)(1+zeta²)] and C=(1-zeta²)/(1+zeta²), so all radial coefficients lie in the differential field F=R(zeta,Y), with Y central and partial_r zeta=sqrt(K)(1+zeta²)/2. The ring F[partial_r] obeys partial_r a=a partial_r+a'. It is a domain and admits common right denominators. One elementary route is left Euclidean division; common LEFT multiples follow by a linear coefficient count for sums of left multiples. Formal adjoint, which sends partial_r to -partial_r and reverses products, supplies common RIGHT multiples. This constructs the fraction division ring and a common right denominator for any finite collection of its elements. See also the [differential-operator arithmetic documentation](https://docs.magma-maths.org/CommutativeAlgebra/DifferentialRings/euclidean-algorithms.html). This algebraic fact is applied to an ordinary differential ring over a field, not assumed for an arbitrary several-variable PDE ring.

Choose nonzero D in F[partial_r] so that each map above, including the identity, multiplied on the RIGHT by D is an ordinary differential operator. Write those finitely many operators as N_i, with D included. They still have rational-in-Y coefficients. Let N be their maximum radial order, and choose a nonzero polynomial q(zeta,Y) divisible by every coefficient denominator when each coefficient is viewed as a rational function of Y over R(zeta). Include denominator multiplicities. Set

    P=D q^(N+1) f,
    (chi,b,w,U,v,u)=corresponding N_i q^(N+1) f.

The order of multiplication matters. Every term in the Leibniz expansion of partial_r^j q^(N+1), j<=N, has a factor q^(N+1-j); at least one q survives to cancel the original coefficient denominator. Thus these are finite radial differential operators with POLYNOMIAL Y coefficients, rational zeta coefficients. They preserve all the exact fraction identities because the same factor is composed on the right of each map. Merely multiplying all equations on the left by q would introduce commutators and is invalid.

Choose a smaller shell avoiding the finitely many poles of the resulting rational zeta coefficients, and let f=f0(r)delta_b with a nonzero smooth bump f0 supported inside it. Finite differential action gives smooth compact radial coefficients times finitely many Y powers of delta_b. On these actual distributions all previously formal equations are now polynomial differential identities. Reconstruct

    z=R(v)-rE S0(w)/2,
    Q=R(u)-rE T(adj(M)vsrc U),
    h=z-rE sym(Q), e2=-A2^(-1) BQ.

K2552's full scalar/TT intertwinings prove EVERY original selected equation with the pressure j above, including skew and auxiliary rows. The fields vanish near the center and outer shell boundary, so extension by zero creates no radial boundary or center-flux distributions.
