## 2. Precise stronger test

Let A(t,x,D) be the actual properly supported **classical order-one**
pseudodifferential generator obtained by the already-reviewed constraint
polarization on a small non-null conic neighborhood of `(p,dr)`. The full
auxiliary inverse and smooth constant-rank kernel/complement frames exist
there. The classical symbol and all finite derivatives are smooth; the
construction uses ordinary classical composition and an elliptic microlocal
parametrix. Stay away from the transverse null stratum.

Its full finite principal matrix `H*=a1(0,p,dr)` contains the certified
20-dimensional geometric-spin7 block. The exact root certificates give
`tr H_mu=0` and positive `Re tr(H_mu²)`, hence a principal eigenvalue with
positive real part at **each** kappa1 root. No new full-spectrum computation
is required.

**Theorem.** For every finite Sobolev index sigma and every fixed finite losses
ell_initial, ell_forcing >=0, no uniform C,T>0 satisfy

    ||w(t)||H^sigma <= C[||w(0)||H^(sigma+ell_initial)
                  + integral_0^t ||(partial_t-A)w||H^(sigma+ell_forcing) dt]

for all smooth compact microlocal tests in this core and 0<=t<=T.
Microlocal here means asymptotic conic localization, not simultaneous exact
compact spatial support and exact Fourier band limitation.
The precise original graph/forcing formulation is in§5. This improves the
previous no-loss result by excluding **every fixed finite spatial loss**.
It is not a theorem that exact analytic solution germs fail to exist.

This belongs to the classical necessary-hyperbolicity setting; see
[Matsumoto–Yamahara](https://www.numdam.org/article/JEDP_1989____A8_0.pdf)
and [Metivier](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.4760) for related differential-system
results. The proof below supplies the required pseudodifferential and graph
scope rather than assuming that a differential theorem transfers unchanged.

## 3. Uniform finite-order packet expansion

Set k=h^-2, tau=kt, y=(x-p)/h, with spatial dimension d=13, and define

    (T_h f)(x)=h^(-d/2) exp(i dr.(x-p)/h²) f((x-p)/h),
    A_h(tau)=h² T_h^-1 A(h² tau) T_h.

For any chosen finite N, classical symbol expansion and Taylor expansion give

    A_h = H* + sum_(j=1)^N h^j P_j(tau,y,D_y) + h^(N+1) R_(N,h).       (1)

Each P_j is a finite matrix differential operator with polynomial y and tau
coefficients. The homogeneous leading term obeys exactly

    h² a1(h²tau,p+hy,h^-2 dr+h^-1 eta)
        = a1(h²tau,p+hy,dr+h eta).

Order-zero terms start at h², with lower classical orders at successive even
powers. Time Taylor terms carry powers h²tau. The actual stationary chart
needs no time variation, but (1) allows it.

Here is the needed uniform remainder statement: on any finite span generated
by polynomial multiples and derivatives of a fixed `chi in C_c^infinity`,
and for every required global-envelope L2/Sobolev seminorm, including output
outside the support of chi, R_(N,h) is bounded
by C(1+tau)^M using finitely many higher seminorms, uniformly for
`0<=tau<=c log k`. Constants may depend on N,c and the finite seminorms.
There is no uniform-in-N or analytic convergence assertion.

To obtain it, split Fourier envelope variables into `|h eta|<epsilon` and
the complement. In the first region Taylor's integral remainder is bounded
by finitely many powers of y,eta and tau and bounded symbol derivatives in
the fixed chart/cone. On the complement, smooth compact envelopes have
arbitrarily rapid Fourier decay, so any selected algebraic power of h is
available by taking sufficiently many envelope derivatives. Proper-support
and off-diagonal chart tails are treated by integration by parts in frequency.
The off-support tails are O(h^L) times the finite envelope seminorms for
any chosen finite L; they are not discarded on the basis of a pointwise
inside-support estimate. Spatial cutoffs equal one near the shrinking support.
Classical negative-order
and smoothing remainders can be put below any prescribed finite power.
Since h log k tends to zero, all points remain in the chosen chart/cone.
This proves the uniform logarithmic-window bound; a fixed-time O(h) expansion
alone would not suffice.

## 4. Corrections and contradiction

Let rho be the **maximum real part over the full finite matrix H*** and choose
an eigenvector v with eigenvalue lambda satisfying Re lambda=rho. The certified
block proves rho>0; the maximizing eigenvector need not belong to that block.
Finite-dimensional Jordan form gives

    ||exp(tau H*)|| <= C exp(rho tau)(1+tau)^m, tau>=0.

Choose nonzero smooth compact chi and set

    w0=chi(y) exp(lambda tau)v,
    (partial_tau-H*)wj=sum_(a=1)^j P_a w_(j-a),  wj(0)=0, j>=1,
    W_h=sum_(j=0)^N h^j wj.                                             (2)

Each correction stays in a finite span of polynomial derivatives of chi,
with the same compact y support. Duhamel integration inductively gives every
needed finite seminorm the bound
`C_j exp(rho tau)(1+tau)^M_j`. Choosing the maximal full spectral abscissa
prevents a correction from introducing a faster exponential. Jordan blocks
only add polynomial factors; no diagonalizability is assumed.

By (1)–(2), cancellation of coefficients through h^N gives

    ||(partial_tau-A_h)W_h|| <= C h^(N+1) exp(rho tau)(1+tau)^M.           (3)

At tau=c log k all corrections are smaller than w0 by
`O(h poly(log k))`; the leading final norm is nonzero. These are approximate
solutions with controlled forcing, not exact exponentially growing solutions.

Take ell=max(ell_initial,ell_forcing) and choose c rho>ell and
`(N+1)/2>ell`. For example q=ceil(ell), `c rho=q+1`, `N=2q+2` works.
At final time `t_k=c log(k)/k ->0`, modulation gives the factor k^sigma.
After dividing by the final H^sigma norm, the right-side initial term and
integrated forcing term in the proposed estimate are respectively

    O(k^(ell-c rho)),
    O(k^(ell-(N+1)/2) poly(log k)).                                     (4)

The k from partial_t=k partial_tau cancels dt=d tau/k. Our explicit schedule
bounds the exponents by -1 and -3/2. Both ratios vanish, while the normalized
left side tends to1. This contradicts a fixed C,T. All parameters N,c are
chosen after the proposed finite losses; no uniform claim across losses is
needed. Negative/fractional finite sigma are covered by the same frequency
localization and Fourier-tail bounds.

### A broad norm-repair class is excluded

Suppose a proposed state norm Y on the same microlocal graph core has finite
Sobolev bounds, expressed in the w coordinates,

    ||w||H^(-a) <= C1 ||w||Y <= C2 ||w||H^b,   a,b finite nonnegative,    (5)

on one fixed compact chart containing test-path and residual supports,
and its claimed bounded local evolution obeys the same Y Duhamel estimate
for graph forcing. Combining inequalities gives the forbidden theorem with
sigma=-a and finite loss a+b. In particular, no strongly continuous semigroup, or locally bounded evolution
family with variation of constants, can have this norm while its generator
agrees with the actual graph generator on these smooth test paths. The upper
embedding also covers their time derivatives and actual graph residuals, so
the paths are C1 in Y and generator-continuous. Variation of constants is then
justified using the local bound M_T; no globally bounded semigroup, invariance
of compact support or finite propagation is assumed. The Duhamel identity is
a mathematical consequence for these test paths, not a demand that every
residual can be generated by an independent physical matter source. Physical
matter-source surjectivity is not a premise of this norm-repair exclusion.

This covers an invertible elliptic finite-order pseudodifferential change of
variables with a finite-order inverse/parametrix on the test cone, whenever
the resulting norm and residual domains have (5). Low-frequency/smoothing
corrections do not alter the high-frequency packet contradiction. Thus a norm
can be unbounded and non-equivalent to the original graph norm yet still fail
if it differs only by finitely many Sobolev orders. This is the new repair-class
exclusion; it does not repeat the earlier bounded-equivalent-metric result.

It does **not** cover an arbitrary unbounded PT/C operator. An infinite-order
or exponentially weighted topology, a non-elliptic projection removing the
channel, a different boundary-selected solution space, or a quotient that
does not contain this core need not obey (5). They remain unmodeled/undetermined
for this candidate. A name such as PT, fakeon or Krein is not a domain theorem.
