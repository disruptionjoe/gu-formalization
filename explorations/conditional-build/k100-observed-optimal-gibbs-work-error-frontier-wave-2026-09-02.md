## Optimal finite-prefix record

For odd `N`, let the two branch states be products of Bernoulli occupation
probabilities `1/4` and `3/4`. On a word of Hamming weight `k`, the branch-one
to branch-zero likelihood ratio is

```text
L(k)=3^(2k-N).                                              (1)
```

Thus `L(k)>1` exactly when `k>N/2`. The K99 majority effect is therefore the
Helstrom-optimal equal-prior measurement, not merely one convenient readout.
Its error is

```text
e_N = sum_{k>N/2} binom(N,k)(1/4)^k(3/4)^(N-k),             (2)
```

with `e_1=1/4`, `e_3=5/32`, and `e_5=53/512`.

## Exact finite-work frontier

The K99 flip costs `epsilon/2` per cell in the one branch, so
`W_N=N epsilon/2`. Retaining only the first term of (2) gives the exact
strictly positive lower bound

```text
e_N >= binom(N,(N+1)/2) 3^((N-1)/2) / 4^N > 0.             (3)
```

Together with K99's Hoeffding certificate `e_N<=exp(-N/8)`, equations
(2)--(3) give a two-sided reliability frontier. Every finite prefix and hence
every finite `W_N` has nonzero optimal error. Exactness in this product family
requires `N -> infinity`, which forces `W_N -> infinity` and growing apparatus
capacity.

This does not prove that every possible measurement architecture has infinite
work cost. It proves that no different finite-prefix effect can rescue the
named K99 architecture: majority already is optimal.
