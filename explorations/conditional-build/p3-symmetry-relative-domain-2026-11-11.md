# Boundary symmetry constrains dimensions only through admissible modes

**Result.** For the structurally defined subfamily with a complex Fredholm
realization and a compact symmetry preserved by the operator and its domains,
the relative-domain index formula lifts to the complex representation ring.
Consequently, preserving an index residue modulo three under every permitted
finite domain change requires every **attainable signed relative dimension** to vanish
modulo three. This is not implied merely by reality, quaternionic pairing,
or the existence of a gauge representation.

This constrains `P3xsymmetry` and composes with `X-P2-P3`. It neither identifies
that analytic index with P3's physical count nor decides the whole class C.
No specific action, source-family count, spectral section or boundary connection
is constructed. The mathematics is standard exact-sequence/representation
algebra applied to the missing transfer premise, not a claim of a new theorem
in index theory.

## 1. Equivariant relative index

Let `D:E→F` be a bounded complex Fredholm operator; for a closed unbounded
operator take `E=Dom(D)` with its graph norm. Let a compact group `G` act
continuously and linearly on `E,F`, commuting with `D`. Let `E0⊂E` be a closed
G-invariant finite-codimension subspace, with `W=E/E0`. The usual exact sequence
is now G-equivariant:

`0 → ker D0 → ker D → W → coker D0 → coker D → 0`.

Here `D0=D|E0`, and `[x]↦[Dx]` defines the connecting map. The earlier
[phase/domain result](x-p2-p3-phase-domain-index-2026-11-11.md) proves Fredholmness
and exactness; equivariance follows directly from `Dg=gD` and invariance of
both domains. All spaces in the sequence are finite dimensional. Compactness
allows an invariant Hermitian inner product by averaging, so each finite exact
sequence splits as representations. Taking alternating classes gives

`Ind_G(D0)=Ind_G(D)-[W]` in `R_C(G)`.

For two G-invariant commensurable domains `E1,E2` of the **same** operator,
assume their intersection `E*` is closed and finite codimensional in both,
and their graph realizations are Fredholm. Applying the formula twice gives

`Ind_G(D|E2)-Ind_G(D|E1)=[E2/E*]-[E1/E*]`.

The complex dimension homomorphism gives the prior integer formula. A finite
rank change of a boundary projector is relevant only after it is shown to
produce these analytic domain hypotheses. An abstract irreducible summand is
not automatically an attainable boundary modification. This calculation also
does not cover a change in asymptotic Fredholm component.

## 2. Sharp residue statement, with attainability retained

Fix a reference admissible domain and a connected collection of permitted
finite transitions. Each oriented transition has actual integer index change

`delta_e = dim_C(E_to/E*) - dim_C(E_from/E*)`.

Reversing an allowed transition negates this number. Let `H⊂Z/3` be the subgroup
generated by the residues of the **actual permitted transitions**. Then every
index difference along a permitted path lies in H. Index residue is constant
throughout this collection iff every permitted edge has `delta_e=0 mod3`.
Proof: path differences telescope; conversely each permitted edge is a path.
The subgroup is a bound on reachable differences, not a claim every element
is attained from every domain in a finite collection.

If `W=⊕ m_alpha V_alpha`, write `d_alpha=dim_C V_alpha`. Dimension is
`Σ m_alpha d_alpha`. For a pure removal the change is its negative. An inventory
of allowed irreducibles gives the coarse subgroup generated by their dimensions,
but equality with the actual transition subgroup requires admissibility and
availability of independent elementary moves. Example: a one-dimensional
irrep may occur only in admitted triples. Its abstract dimension generates all
of Z/3, while the allowed triple removals preserve the residue. For general
commensurable changes, signed differences may cancel even when neither quotient
individually has dimension divisible by three.

Thus `d_alpha∈3Z` for all contributing irreps is a sufficient representation
criterion. It is necessary only under the stronger elementary-move assumption.
The exact necessary and sufficient condition remains on `delta_e`.

## 3. Reality and quaternionic pairing are different restrictions

Suppose the relevant finite quotient W inherits an antilinear map `J` from a
domain-preserved real structure. This inheritance must be checked; an interior
J need not preserve a chosen boundary connection or domain.

- If `J²=+1`, `W=C` with ordinary conjugation is allowed algebraically. Complex
  dimension one is not excluded by this reality condition.
- If `J²=-1`, its complex dimension is even. In a complex basis write
  `J=A conjugation`. Then `A conjugate(A)=-I_n`, and determinants give
  `|det A|²=(-1)^n`. Since A is invertible, n is even. Conversely direct sums
  of `J(z1,z2)=(-conjugate(z2),conjugate(z1))` realize every even dimension.

This is complex dimension: a quaternionic line has complex dimension two and
real dimension four. In a Fredholm complex, the parity conclusion requires J
on source and target, commuting with D, and preserving the domain, so kernel,
cokernel and finite relative quotients inherit it. We do not infer those
conditions from the word "real" in class C.

Evenness is not three-divisibility: `2,4,6` have residues `2,1,0`. If two-complex-
dimensional changes are independently repeatable, all residues occur among
their repeated differences. With bounded availability, only the actual moves
may be claimed. A single attainable change of size two already prevents
universal residue preservation across that edge.

## 4. Analytic controls with symmetry actually preserved

Use the already proved half-line model `D_1=∂x+1` on `H¹(R_+)→L²(R_+)`, now
tensored with a finite-dimensional Hermitian G-module V of complex dimension r.
The operator commutes with its constant G action. Its unrestricted kernel is
`e^{-x}V`, it is surjective, and its index is r. The zero-trace domain is
G-invariant and has index zero. Explicitly the zero-initial solution
`u(x)=∫_0^x e^{-(x-t)}f(t)dt` is bounded from L² to H¹, while `e^{-x}v`
realizes every trace v. Thus the relative quotient is **actually V** and the
index change is `-r`; this is more than a representation inventory.

For r=1 take the trivial compact group and conjugation. For r=2 take the
standard quaternionic J above and compact G=Sp(1) in its complex two-dimensional
representation. D and both domains preserve J and G. The changes are -1 and -2,
respectively. Both controls are abstract first-order analytic families, not
class-C actions, not GU boundary spectral sections, and not a reconstruction
of the missing physical boundary data. They establish sharpness of the stated
symmetry hypotheses only. No existing RP³ eta result is changed.

## 5. Transfer to the program and the unresolved discriminator

`P3xsymmetry` moves from open to constrained with this requirement: identify the
boundary-preserved symmetry, its real structure, and **attainable** relative
index classes, then show their dimensions vanish mod3 if residue preservation
is claimed. A scalar whole-operator phase supplies none of these inputs.
The absolute index value still needs a reference realization; even invariance
of its residue does not determine that residue or a family count.

The declared class C supplies APS-type end data and a boundary connection but
not a specific adapted boundary operator, preserved subgroup, domain real
structure, admissible finite transitions, or connected asymptotic components.
Those omissions delimit the transfer, not research permission. The next
substantial discriminator is the symmetry actually surviving the admitted
boundary connection and which relative modules its admissibility rule allows.
The independent full-natural/gauge symbol-space weld remains open; its existing
observer-subgroup controls do not prove completeness of full-covariant tensors.
Do not replace either task with another abstract parity or rank census.
