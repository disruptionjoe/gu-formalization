## K1276: the low-degree parity floor

The connected split-`D7` invariant ring is

```text
R[e1(x^2),e2(x^2),e3(x^2),e4(x^2),e5(x^2),e6(x^2),p]
```

with generator degrees `2,4,6,8,10,12,7`. A monomial containing an odd
power of `p` has ordinary Cartan degree at least seven. Consequently every
invariant polynomial of degree below seven is independent of `p` and belongs
to the outer-even subring. Degree seven is sharp because `p` itself is odd.

## K1278: elimination cannot manufacture parity without new data

Let an auxiliary involution be `y -> R y` and suppose

```text
S(-p,Ry)=S(p,y),      y*(-p)=R y*(p).
```

Then `S_eff(p)=S(p,y*(p))` obeys `S_eff(-p)=S_eff(p)`. The control
`S=(y-p)^2+y^2` has the unique equivariant solution `y*=p/2` and even value
`p^2/2`. By contrast, freezing the non-equivariant component `y=+1` gives
`1-2p+p^2`; its odd term is paid for by the imported component orientation.
Thus unique equivariant elimination is not an odd-response owner, while a
boundary or branch choice can be one only if the theory explicitly owns it.
