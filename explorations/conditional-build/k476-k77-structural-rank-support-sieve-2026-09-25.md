## Theorem

For a matrix with fixed zero/nonzero support, its structural rank is the size
of a maximum matching in the associated source-target bipartite graph. Hence a
support graph whose maximum matching is smaller than the required K475 rank
cannot realize an acyclic arrow for any coefficient values. This is the Hall-
deficiency rejection.

For K77, `D2` must have a matching covering every one of the 10,752 `C2`
sources, while `D1` must have a matching covering every one of the 35,840
`C0` targets. These are necessary support conditions only. A support that
passes may still suffer numerical cancellations, violate `D1 D2=0`, fail the
closed-domain condition, or lack action custody.

Exact small controls distinguish a full-column support, a Hall-deficient
support of deficiency one and a full-row support. None is an actual K77 block.

## Bookends

The support sieve is cheaper than numerical rank and can reject a future
packet before expansion. The strongest contrary construction is a support
with a perfect matching whose chosen coefficients cancel; structural rank is
generic possibility, not actual rank. The weakest seam is support ownership:
an inferred sparsity pattern is not a substitute for the action's serialized
coefficient blocks.
