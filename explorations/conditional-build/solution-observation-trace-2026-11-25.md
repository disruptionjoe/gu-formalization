## 1. The clean local theorem: exact null-slab solutions

Let D=sum A_j partial_j be any homogeneous constant-coefficient first-order system on a finite-dimensional vector carrier E. Let S={y=0} be a fixed section. Suppose a real nonzero conormal ell and a vector b satisfy

    sigma_D(ell)b=0,       Qb != 0,

where Q is the actual fixed zero-order observation contraction. On a bounded product tube U=U_x times U_y meeting S, take f in C_c^infinity(R) with f(0)=1 and define

    u_epsilon(x,y)=f(ell(y)/epsilon)b.

Then

    D u_epsilon=epsilon^(-1) f'(ell(y)/epsilon) sigma_D(ell)b=0

**exactly**, and q u_epsilon=Qb on U_x for every epsilon. After a fixed linear normal-coordinate change taking ell(y) to y1, Fubini gives

    ||u_epsilon||^2_{L2(U)} <= C epsilon,
    ||u_epsilon||_{D,U}=||u_epsilon||_{L2(U)} ->0.

The trace is a fixed nonzero element of L2(U_x), or of any fixed Hausdorff target retaining that smooth constant section. Thus q is **nonclosable**, not just unbounded, on this actual smooth solution core. If b itself belongs to that core, v_epsilon=b-u_epsilon is also a solution with qv_epsilon=0 and v_epsilon->b in solution norm: the hidden kernel is not closed.

This is a characteristic scalar modulation, not the Nov24 arbitrary normal cutoff. The null derivative annihilates b, so the solution equation is preserved. It works even when only one normal characteristic coordinate is concentrated; codimension10 is not needed once such a visible normal characteristic exists.

For the entire full18 module, take ell real null in N*, and b=(b_H,0_N;nu=0) with b_H in B_ell: componentwise c_ell b_H=0, Gamma_H b_H=0. Then also i_ell b=0. Each of the nine generators

    c_ell Id, ell Gamma, J i_ell, J c_ell Gamma;
    ell Id, J c_ell; i_ell, c_ell Gamma; c_ell

annihilates this input, and so do their input Omega partners. Hence the **same exact solution sequence works for every scalar coefficient choice**, including singular strata, in either separate normal signature(6,4) or(4,6). B_ell has the owned dimension192 and is nonzero in either auxiliary half.

Scope limits:

## 2. Smooth common polarizations away from the single conormal

To obtain full-spatial L2 states we need a smoothly varying exact kernel vector for nearby null covectors, not just the value at ell.

Use g to identify covectors and vectors consistently; c(p)c(n)+c(n)c(p)=2g(p,n)I. Fix a real null normal n with g(ell,n)!=0. For a null p near the ray of ell with a=g(p,n)!=0, set

    P_p = c(p)c(n)/(2a),
    h_p v = v-[p g(n,v)+n g(p,v)]/a,
    E_p = {v: g(v,p)=g(v,n)=0}.

P_p is a projection with image ker c(p), h_p is the metric projection onto the nondegenerate12-dimensional screen E_p. Both are smooth and degree0 in p. Let Gamma_E and J_E be Clifford contraction/insertion on E_p, with Gamma_E J_E=12I. For the visible b0 above define

    V(p)=(h_p tensor P_p)b0,
    w(p)=V(p)-(1/12)J_E Gamma_E V(p),    nu(p)=0.

Then

    c(p)w(p)=0 componentwise,
    i_p w(p)=0,
    Gamma w(p)=0,
    w(ell)=b0.

Proof: V lies in E_p tensor ker c(p); for vectors in E_p, Clifford multiplication anticommutes with c(p), so Gamma_E V and each component of J_E Gamma_E V remain in ker c(p). Gamma_E J_E=12 cancels the gamma trace; all vector slots remain screen-transverse. At ell, h and P fix b0 and Gamma b0=0. Thus every full18 symbol annihilates w(p), including all input-Omega partners. On a small compact angular neighborhood, w and Qw are uniformly bounded and Qw remains close to Qb0!=0. This gives an exact smooth common polarization, with no eigenvector diagonalization or WKB approximation.

The screen property also makes w(p) distinct from a pure exact-gradient polarization p tensor v when w(p)!=0. This fact does not by itself define a physical gauge quotient or make q gauge-invariant.

## 3. Full-spatial L2 actual solutions with real frequencies

Write ambient coordinates as (t,x,y_+,y_-), with x in R3, y_+ in R6 and y_- in R4. The quadratic form is

    -tau^2+|eta|^2+|a|^2-|b|^2.

On the positive real branch define

    tau(eta,a,b)=sqrt(|eta|^2+|a|^2-|b|^2)>0.

For smooth compactly supported spatial-frequency amplitudes A(eta,a,b) satisfying sigma_D(p)A=0, p=(tau,eta,a,b), the integral

    u_A(t,x,y)=integral exp(i(t tau+x·eta+y_+·a+y_-·b)) A(eta,a,b) d eta d a d b

is an exact smooth solution of every D whose symbol kills A. Our amplitudes use the common w(p) from §2, so they work for **every full18 member**, without assuming D_t invertible or semisimple. The Fourier support stays away from tau=0; no imaginary temporal frequency is admitted. At each t this field is spatially Schwartz, and positive-component Plancherel gives

    ||u_A(t)||_L2(R13)=||A||_L2,
    ||u_A||_L2([-T,T] times R13)=sqrt(2T)||A||_L2.

Throughout, notation `||A||_Hs` means the initial-data Sobolev norm
`(integral (1+|xi|^2)^s |A(xi)|^2 dxi)^(1/2)`, with xi=(eta,a,b). It does **not** mean derivatives of the amplitude A with respect to frequency.

This norm preservation is a direct scalar-phase fact on the declared sheet, not a positive symmetrizer or well-posedness assertion for all data of D. Jordan generalized modes and imaginary-frequency sectors are outside this subspace.

### Exact coarea measure

Fix the observed frequency (tau,eta), let c=tau^2-|eta|^2 and write

    b=r phi,        phi in S3,
    a=sqrt(r^2+c) theta,    theta in S5,
    r>sqrt(max(-c,0)).

Since d a d b=rho^5 d rho d theta r^3 dr d phi and d rho/d tau=tau/rho,

    d a d b = d tau d mu_(tau,eta),
    d mu_(tau,eta)=tau(r^2+c)^2 r^3 dr d theta d phi.       (1)

The trace Fourier transform is exactly, up to a fixed Fourier convention constant,

    (q u_A)^hat(tau,eta)=integral Q A(eta,a,b) d mu_(tau,eta).      (2)

For observed frequencies in a compact K subset {tau>0}, a normal dyadic shell r~R has measure comparable to R8. This is a **noncompact mass-shell fibre at fixed observed frequency**. It is the mechanism missing from the positive one-time comparator.

### Nonclosability for initial H^s, s<4

Choose g in C_c^infinity(K), nonzero; chi nonnegative smooth supported in (1,3/2), nonzero; and h(theta,phi) a nonnegative smooth angular bump of integral1 near the normal null direction ell=(theta0,phi0). Make its support small enough that the angular mean of Qw at the limiting normal rays is nonzero. For large R let

    M_R(tau,eta)=integral chi(r/R)h(theta,phi) d mu_(tau,eta),
    A_R(eta,a,b)=g(tau,eta) chi(r/R)h(theta,phi) w(p)/M_R(tau,eta).

All amplitudes are smooth compactly supported in real spatial frequency; each defines an exact solution. Uniformly on K,

    M_R asymptotic tau R8 [integral chi(s)s7 ds],
    ||A_R||_H^s <= C R^(s-4),       s>=0.                (3)

The same estimate follows for any fixed real s by frequency comparability on this support. Equation(2) gives

    (q u_R)^hat=g(tau,eta) V_R(tau,eta),
    V_R -> V_infinity
      =integral h(theta,phi) Qw(0,0,theta,phi) d theta d phi !=0,       (4)

uniformly on K. Here homogeneity of w is used: p/r tends uniformly to the normal null vector (0,0,theta,phi), and rho/r->1; the normalized radial factor cancels. Consequently

    q u_R -> v=Fourier_inverse(g V_infinity) !=0

in global observed L2(R_t times R3_x). For s<4, the initial H^s norm tends to0, establishing **nonclosability** on this exact real-sheet solution core. In particular s=0 and s=1 fail. By (3) and scalar-phase Plancherel, the ordinary ambient L2 graph norm on every finite time slab tends to0 as well, since Du_R=0.

For a prescribed finite observation time interval, choose g so that v is nonzero there (time translation is available), and restrict the convergence. A nonzero inverse Fourier transform of compactly supported g cannot vanish on an open time interval for every x; no concentration estimate depending on the observation window is required.

### Critical endpoint s=4

Choose R_j=2^j R0. The chi supports are disjoint in r, so the amplitudes A_(R_j) are H4-orthogonal. Their H4 norms are uniformly bounded, while their traces converge to the same nonzero v. Set

    B_N=(1/N)sum_{j=N}^{2N-1} A_(R_j).

Then

    ||B_N||_H4^2 <= C/N ->0,
    q u_(B_N) -> v !=0 in observed L2.

Thus the **s=4 endpoint is also nonclosable**. This is an actual sequence of smooth finite sums, not a distributional amplitude supported on a zero-measure initial-data set. Real temporal frequencies and the common field constraints are maintained throughout.

## 4. Exact repair on this declared sheet: closable for s>4; bounded at9/2

The negative theorem is not an all-topologies no-go. Weighted coarea gives its sharp repair.

For arbitrary vector amplitudes on this real sheet (hence also for constrained common-kernel amplitudes), Cauchy--Schwarz in (2) uses

    J_s(tau,eta)=integral (1+|eta|^2+|a|^2+|b|^2)^(-s) d mu.

This is finite at infinity iff s>4. Indeed the integrand's radial tail is comparable to r^(7-2s)dr. For s>4 put A=1+|eta|^2+|c|, u=r^2, u0=max(-c,0), v=u-u0. Apart from sphere constants,

    J_s=(tau/2) integral_(u0)^infinity u(u+c)^2
              (1+|eta|^2+c+2u)^(-s) du
       <= C_s tau integral_0^infinity (v+A)^3(A+2v)^(-s) dv
       <= C_s tau A^(4-s).                             (5)

A is comparable, with fixed constants, to 1+tau^2+|eta|^2, and tau<=sqrt(A). Therefore

    ||q u_A||_H^(s-9/2)(R4) <= C_s ||A||_Hs(R13),     s>4.       (6)

At s=9/2 this is a global observed L2 bound. For 4<s<9/2 it gives a continuous distributional trace into a negative Sobolev space. If A_n->0 in Hs and q u_(A_n)->v in L2, then (6) implies v=0. The trace is therefore **closable**, despite being unbounded as an L2-valued operator in that interval. The same distributional estimate against test functions supported in a finite time interval proves local/finite-time closability; this is not inferred merely by postcomposing an arbitrary closed operator with restriction.

The global L2 boundedness order9/2 is sharp on the nonzero common-polarization sector. For any nonzero trace fixture, spatial-frequency dilation corresponds to

    u_lambda(t,z)=lambda^(13/2)u(lambda t,lambda z).

The initial homogeneous Hs norm scales as lambda^s while the observed L2(R4) norm scales as lambda^(13/2-4/2)=lambda^(9/2). High-frequency dilation disproves an L2 bound below9/2. Finite observation time keeps a nonzero fraction after focusing the fixture inside the interval. This unboundedness argument alone is not a nonclosability argument; the fixed-output-frequency shell sequence above supplies the stronger conclusion only at s<=4.

Summary for this declared oscillatory sheet, with the smooth compact-frequency kernel-polarized core:

| Initial positive Sobolev norm | Literal trace into observed L2 |
|---|---|
| 0<=s<=4 | nonclosable (dyadic fixed-output-frequency witnesses) |
| 4<s<9/2 | closable but unbounded; continuous into H^(s-9/2) |
| s>=9/2 | bounded; (6) gives additional observed regularity |

The bounded half of this table is **not** a theorem for the entire solution space of an arbitrary full18 member. Generalized Jordan modes, other sheets, an ill-conditioned two-sheet decomposition, source constraints, coefficient variations and lower terms require separate analysis. A failure on this invariant subclass, however, propagates to every larger solution domain that contains it with a norm no stronger than the tested one.

### Critical logarithmic refinement (followthrough, same exact solution class)

The power threshold is not a claim that a full extra fractional derivative is necessary. Give initial data the critical weighted norm

    ||A||^2_(4,beta)=integral <xi>^8 [log(e+|xi|)]^beta |A(xi)|^2 dxi.

The fixed observed-frequency fibre integral is finite precisely when beta>1, since its tail is

    integral^infinity dr/[r(log r)^beta].

For beta<=1, shell R_j=2^j has norm squared at most C j^beta. Use positive weights a_j=j^(-beta)/S_N over j=N,...,N^2, with S_N their sum. Then S_N->infinity (at beta=1 it is asymptotic log N), the weighted sum of the exact amplitudes has squared norm at most C/S_N, and its trace converges to the same v because all j>=N traces converge uniformly in observed L2. Thus trace is nonclosable for every beta<=1.

For beta>1, the change of variables in (5) now bounds the fibre moment by

    J_(4,beta)(tau,eta)
      <=C_beta tau [log(e+sqrt(A))]^(1-beta),
    A=1+|eta|^2+|tau^2-|eta|^2|.

Indeed the remaining tail is bounded by the integral from A to infinity of
`v^(-1)[log(e+sqrt(v))]^(-beta)dv`. Since A is comparable to <(tau,eta)>^2 and tau<=sqrt(A), this gives a continuous observed distribution norm with squared multiplier

    <(tau,eta)>^(-1) [log(e+|(tau,eta)|)]^(beta-1).

In particular, observation is continuous into H^(-1/2), hence closable into global or finite-time local L2. No finite logarithmic power makes global L2 observation bounded: frequency dilation gives the divergent ratio lambda^(1/2)/(log lambda)^(beta/2). This is an explicit non-power norm repair of closability, not source selection, and keeps the separate9/2 boundedness demand visible.

## 5. Positive comparator, full-spacetime pitfall and density controls

### Positive normal geometry

The already owned positive one-time comparator has |zeta|^2=tau^2-|eta|^2 and compact normal spheres. Its fixed observed-frequency coarea mass is finite. Its global9/2 boundedness threshold remains the owned result. At any initial Hs order the fixed-frequency operator is distributionally controlled after an appropriate observed negative Sobolev weight; thus a failed global L2 bound below9/2 does not give the indefinite fixed-output-frequency nonclosability mechanism. The normal6,4 model also admits the9/2 bound on its selected real sheet, but has the additional s<=4 nonclosability defect. This is the precise comparison; do not describe ordinary positive wave solutions as having the new defect merely because their lower-energy trace is unbounded.

Explicitly, the positive weighted fibre mass is
`J_s^+=|S9| tau (tau^2-|eta|^2)^4(1+tau^2)^(-s)` on tau>|eta|. It is at most a constant times `<(tau,eta)>^(9-2s)`, for every fixed real s. The same Cauchy--Schwarz argument gives `Hs(initial)->H^(s-9/2)(observed)` on that positive sheet for every s, so its L2-valued core trace is closable even below9/2. This elementary comparison is a direct extension of the already owned coarea calculation, not a claim about the source normal geometry.

### Full-spacetime L2 is not an interchangeable input norm

For a time-regular exterior14 member, the owned symbol theorem makes D(p) invertible off the null cone. If u belongs to L2(R14) and Du=0 distributionally, Fourier transformation gives D(p) uhat(p)=0 a.e. Off the cone uhat=0; the cone has Lebesgue measure0, so u=0. The same is true for an ordinary flat Dirac operator. Thus a nontrivial homogeneous solution norm integrated over **all** time cannot simply be called the global L2 norm in this class. Our nontrivial solutions use initial spatial Hs or finite-time full-spatial L2; the local slab uses bounded tubes. Degenerate full18 members can have other kernels, so this zero-solution statement is restricted to the nonnull-invertible class.

### Smooth density weights

The local slab proof persists under every smooth positive density bounded above/below on the tube. This is enough to show that a regular natural density cannot fix this local actual-solution failure while retaining the same homogeneous equations and admissible slab solutions. Global unbounded/singular weights are a different norm and require their own estimates; no global weighting conclusion follows from norm equivalence on one compact tube.
