# Fixed-middle Burnside balance quotient and S3 biset discriminator

## Result

```text
x in Hom_H(A, Res B),
k in End_K(B),
y in Hom_K(B, Res C).
```

The existing left and right actions obey the defining intermediate-action
balance equation

```text
compose(x ; Res(k), y) = compose(x, k ; y).
```

Lean proves this equation, defines the equivalence closure generated by these
moves for one fixed intermediate object `B`, and descends correspondence
composition through the resulting quotient. For a fourth group and a third
correspondence, Lean also proves that the two parenthesizations agree in the
same three-times-iterated restriction target. The proof uses only functoriality
of restriction and associativity of completed-span composition.

This is stronger than a matrix analogy and narrower than a full coend: the
intermediate object is fixed, there is no coproduct over all `K`-objects, and
no universal mapping property is asserted.

The existing restriction, restriction-correspondence,
correspondence-composition, and additive Burnside span modules already
constructed iterated composition but explicitly left coend/biset composition
open. The present result is therefore narrowed to the genuine balance
relation, its fixed-middle quotient, and associativity inside those restriction
functors.

`H=< (01) >`. It computes

```text

```

by closing exactly the relations `(x*g,y) ~ (x,g*y)`. There are two balanced
classes, exactly the two double cosets. This case matters because treating
`G/H` as a quotient group is invalid: the probe supplies both three distinct
conjugates of `H` and an explicit representative-dependence witness for the
failed quotient multiplication shortcut.
