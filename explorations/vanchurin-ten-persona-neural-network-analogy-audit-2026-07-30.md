# Vanchurin ten-persona neural-network analogy audit

## Result first

The side quest found **useful construction methods, not a neural
interpretation of GU**.

No proposed analogy reaches the literal or strong-correspondence bands. The
two best formal-method transfers, both capped at \(F=3.00\), are:

1. use Vanchurin's direct-plus-implicit derivative as a prompt to enumerate
   every *actual* \(A\)-dependence in the frozen GU action and pull each
   derivative back through its own typed map; and
2. use covariant gradient descent's cotangent-to-tangent conversion as a
   prompt to construct GU's missing connection-current musical from the
   ambient metric/Hodge operator and invariant adjoint pairing.

The second point needs a precise correction. The spinor Krein pairing
\(K_G\) participates upstream in constructing \(J_D\). It does not by itself
raise an adjoint-valued density-dual connection current. That later musical
is induced by \(G\), the Hodge/density convention, and
\(\kappa_{\mathfrak g}\).

A third, weaker prompt is finite gather/scatter bookkeeping for the already
declared restriction/emission pair. It is only a mnemonic: GU's
\(s^*/s_*\) construction is distributional, bundle-valued, and
moving-support dependent.

The audit also recovered a determinant-line question already present in the
repo's anomaly work. It is **not new**, is not the next blocker, and does not
put P1 and P3 on one line. At most it motivates a later comparison between:

- a candidate physical determinant/Pfaffian line carrying the P1
  \(L_\sigma\) transport; and
- a separate auxiliary relative line for P3's virtual comparator pair.

That comparison is meaningful only after the operator branch, reality,
polarization, gauge fixing, closed domain, and Fredholm family are built.

The high-information consequence is therefore an ordering result:
finish the local typed source-action calculus first; postpone global line and
index questions until the needed analytic objects exist.

## Plain English

Vanchurin studies systems in which signals change and the rules connecting
the signals can also change. His papers are unusually careful about which
variables live in which spaces, how an effect reaches a variable through a
chain of dependencies, and which metric converts a derivative into a
permitted motion.

That bookkeeping is useful for GU because the current build has exactly
those unfinished interfaces. Some action terms depend directly on the
connection \(A\); others depend on \(A\) through \(F_A\) or \(D_AU\). The
resulting variation is a covector, while the bridge consumes an ordinary
adjoint-valued one-form. A defect term must also be pulled down to \(X\) and
emitted back to \(Y\) with the correct support.

Vanchurin does not solve any of those GU maps. He gives us good patterns for
writing and attacking them. The analogy earns its keep only when it becomes
a typed GU calculation that can fail.

## Fidelity contract

Each row receives four integers from 0 to 5:

- \(O\): object/carrier fidelity;
- \(D\): equation or variational-operation fidelity;
- \(S\): symmetry, grading, variance, and type fidelity;
- \(C\): physical or mathematical consequence fidelity.

The declared aggregate is

\[
F
=
\min\left(
\frac{O+D+S+C}{4},
\min(O,D,S,C)+1,
\text{applicable caps}
\right).
\]

The weakest-axis term prevents one familiar equation from hiding a failed
carrier or consequence. Actual rows are capped at \(3.00\) because no GU
target map is constructed. An unresolved Layer-0 homonym is capped at
\(2.00\). Count inference, target coding, positive-metric/Krein
identification, scalar-to-index promotion, P3/physical-line collapse, and
splitting the shared \(L_\sigma\) datum are hard rejections.

Bands remain useful for controls:

| \(F\) | interpretation |
| ---: | --- |
| 4.50--5.00 | literal operation or constructed isomorphism |
| 3.50--4.49 | strong structural correspondence |
| 2.50--3.49 | partial structural construction prompt |
| 1.50--2.49 | heuristic |
| below 1.50 | false friend or rejected inference |

The script checks declared scores, metadata, caps, and planted rejection
rules. It does not execute Vanchurin's equations or verify a GU map.

## Efficient carry-forward sequence

The ten lenses and hostile review jointly recommend this order:

1. Build a typed dependency graph for the frozen action, with an explicit
   true/false \(A\)-dependence flag for every coefficient and map.
2. Compute \(J_D\), \(J_F=D_A^!Q_F\), the Green/boundary term, and the
   \(G/\star/\kappa_{\mathfrak g}\) connection-current musical; then settle
   the \(J_D\), \(J_D+J_F\), or independent-current cancellation fork.
3. Recheck the exact \(P_{\rm IG}\) parent, Schur elimination, coefficient,
   and induced \(A/U\) currents.
4. Choose the proved stabilizer branch or construct the moving
   \(\epsilon_{\rm IG}\) orbit before asking for equivariance.
5. Compute the zero-order placement intersection for
   \(P_0,\rho(\Phi),Y_K,Y_C,C\)-reality, gauge representations, and
   right-\(\mathbb H\)-linearity.
6. Derive and verify the local gauge/Noether identity for that typed current.
7. Complete the moving restriction/emission derivatives and recompute
   \(\mathcal E_A,\mathcal E_U,\mathcal E_{\epsilon_{\rm IG}}\).
8. Run datum ablations only against typed interfaces: ablate \(L_\sigma\)
   once, the action weld separately, and P3's \(\nu/\widehat e_n\) roles
   without reading a count.
9. Revisit physical versus relative determinant/Pfaffian line compatibility
   only after reality, polarization, gauge-fixing, subprincipal, domain, and
   Fredholm gates close.
10. Defer coupled-Hessian fast/slow and complete gravity-response tests until
    an admissible stationary background exists.

This sequence advances the source action and external datum without
inserting a target value or dropping any of the five carried legs.
