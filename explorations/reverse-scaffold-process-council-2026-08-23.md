# How to improve the reverse scaffold

> **GU-COMPARATOR-ROUTING — scope before inference.** This artifact borders
> conventional comparators. Any result about a standard Higgs/VEV, ordinary
> family index or net chirality, SO(10) `126` Majorana mechanism, anomaly
> selector or VEV-only breaking binds only that named model. Read
> `lab/methods/source-native-comparator-routing.md` before reusing this.

`GU-COMPARATOR-ROUTING-CLASSIFICATION: INTERNAL_STRUCTURAL_ONLY`

Seven seats, one primary recommendation each, **not merged**. Where seats
conflict the conflict is preserved and marked. The plan prioritizes and
refuses rather than averaging.

## The state being improved

The scaffold now has: six frozen R6 benchmark anchors; frozen R5/R4/R3 demand
packets per anchor; R2 typed at metric-only ceiling; R1 blocked; a seven-owner
demand ledger; and, as of today, the `FITTING_CONSTRUCTION` grade with the
four-bucket disposition scheme and its exhaustion rule.

What it has never produced is a construction. Every descent so far terminated
in a demand interface.

---

## Seat 1 — Reverse-scaffold methodologist

**A rung may not terminate in a demand.** Freezing what is needed is the cheap
half of a descent and the program has done only that half, six times. A rung
now terminates in one of exactly two things: a `FITTING_CONSTRUCTION`, or a
precise impossibility naming what would have to be supplied. "Here is the
demand interface" is what you write when you have stopped working, and it
should stop counting as a completed rung.

## Seat 2 — Construction engineer

**Adopt a minimum-viable-object standard.** Constructions do not get built
because every attempt reaches for the general object. Build the *smallest*
structure that satisfies the frozen demand — one background, one wedge, one
constraint, fixed everything else — and let generality be a later swing. The
K-series is the cautionary case: it repeatedly attacked invariant, general,
whole-class questions and closed them, when a single explicit instance would
have been decisive and cheaper.

## Seat 3 — Phenomenology custodian

**Build the external-tension register — the mirror of the source-claim
register, for the physics side.** Bucket B3 has never been used and is
plausibly the largest untapped bucket in the program. For each phenomenology
target the register records what is actually *measured*, what is
*model-dependent*, and what is *currently contested in the literature*. The
program is presently holding GU to a standard it has never audited: several of
its targets are quantities the incumbent field itself disputes or extracts
through an assumption-laden pipeline. Until that register exists, "GU must
reproduce X" is an unexamined premise on every row.

## Seat 4 — Composition theorist

**Mechanize the interface check, and do not say "pathway" until it passes.** A
set of fitting constructions is not a pathway; it is a pile. The claim
"conditional build with a clear route" is earned only when rung *N*'s output
type equals rung *N+1*'s input type, end to end. Type each construction's
interface explicitly and check the chain by machine, exactly as the descent
already checks that every demand lands on a named gap.

## Seat 5 — Adversary

**Every fitting construction must carry a nontriviality witness: something it
forbids.** This is the hole in the grade as written. FC-1 through FC-6 all
test whether a construction is *consistent* — built, non-conflicting,
source-compatible, routed, ceilinged. None tests whether it is *contentful*. A
sufficiently weak object passes all six trivially, because it conflicts with
nothing by virtue of saying nothing. A construction that forbids nothing
constrains nothing and is not a construction. **This should be added as FC-7,
and it is the single most important change on this list**, because it is the
one that decides whether the new grade becomes a research instrument or a
laundering channel.

## Seat 6 — Scheduler

**Rewrite A1's selection rule in the bucket vocabulary and give the loop
bounded swings.** The hourly cannot execute open-ended construction; it needs
an item with an exit condition. Selection becomes: take the head of the B1/B3/B4
queue, attempt it within a stated bound, and record the outcome as
construction, impossibility, or re-disposition to B2. The exhaustion rule then
enforces itself mechanically rather than relying on anyone remembering it.

## Seat 7 — Historian

**Put an expiry on the scheme itself.** This program has built the six-axis
template, the base categories, the conditional-physics ledger, the demand
ledger and the owner ledger. Every one is well made. Every one became a filing
system rather than a generator. The scaffold should carry a generation
obligation — a stated number of new fitting constructions per period — and if
it is not met, the scheme is declared non-generative and revised rather than
extended. A method that cannot produce output on a schedule is a taxonomy.

## Priority plan

**P1 — FC-7, the nontriviality witness.** *(Seat 5.)* Add to the
`FITTING_CONSTRUCTION` grade: every admitted construction must state at least
one thing it forbids — an excluded value, an excluded structure, or an
observable that would falsify it. A construction that cannot name one is not
admitted. Cheapest change on the list and it must land **before** anything is
banked at the grade, because a laundering channel is far harder to close after
it has contents.

**P2 — a rung terminates in a construction or an impossibility, never a
demand.** *(Seat 1.)* This is the change that converts the scaffold from a
specification device into a generator, and it is the direct answer to the
question asked.

**P3 — the external-tension register.** *(Seat 3.)* Largest untapped work,
zero R1 dependency, and it is the only recommendation here that expands what
the program can legitimately claim rather than tightening how it claims.

**P4 — mechanized interface/composition check.** *(Seat 4.)* Required before
the phrase "clear and plausible pathway" may be used of the whole build. Until
then the correct phrase is "a set of fitting constructions."

**P5 — rewrite A1 in bucket vocabulary with bounded swings.** *(Seat 6.)*
Makes the exhaustion rule self-enforcing.

**P6 — generation obligation with expiry.** *(Seat 7.)* Adopt the obligation;
set the first review after the scheme has had a fair run.

## Explicit refusals

- **Do not order the construction queue by size alone** *(against Seat 2's
  ordering, while keeping its standard.)* The minimum-viable-object standard
  is adopted — build the smallest object that satisfies a demand. But ordering
  the *queue* by size conflicts with P4: the smallest available object is
  often the one whose interfaces compose with nothing. Order by interface
  position in the chain; choose minimally *within* that.
- **Do not gate P2 on P4.** A rung may produce a construction before the
  composition machinery exists; it simply may not be called a pathway yet.
  Sequencing the generator behind the checker would reproduce exactly the
  stall this council was convened to fix.
- **Do not let B3 relax any obligation before its register entry exists.** A
  tension may only soften an obligation once cited and scoped. Refused as a
  general licence.

## Standing disagreements

- **Seat 2 vs Seat 4** on queue ordering: smallest-first versus
  interface-first. Partially resolved above by separating the *standard* from
  the *ordering*; the residual disagreement is real and is left standing.
- **Seat 5 vs Seat 6** on throughput: the nontriviality bar raises the cost of
  every admission and the scheduler wants volume. Resolved *for now* in favour
  of Seat 5 on the grounds that an unfalsifiable bank is worth less than a
  small honest one, but Seat 6's objection is recorded and should be revisited
  if admissions stall.
- **Seat 7 vs Seat 1** on whether the scheme deserves the investment at all.
  Left open; P6's review is the designated test.

No scientific ledger verdict, canon, source ownership, prediction credit, or
public posture changes. P1 proposes an amendment to a method installed the
same day; it is recorded here and applied in the grade document.
