## Theorem

**Conditional sigma-sign nonselection theorem.** Under premises 1–4, the image
of `b` is exactly `{-1,+1}`. Consequently the supplied sigma bridge transmits
the orientation choice but does not select one physical cosmological-term sign.
A unique sign can follow only after at least one frozen premise is changed—for
example by supplying an independent selector that removes one sigma orientation
or by replacing the odd bridge with a different physical law.

## Proof

Choose either admitted orientation `sigma`. Premise 1 also admits `-sigma`.
Premise 2 gives

`b(-sigma)=-b(sigma)`.

Because the codomain is the two nonzero signs, the two values are distinct and
exhaust `{-1,+1}`. Thus `b(Sigma)={-1,+1}`, so no singleton sign is selected.
Changing the convention from `b(sigma)=sigma` to `b(sigma)=-sigma` exchanges
the labels but leaves the two-element image unchanged.

Premise 3 prevents an invalid shortcut around this two-line argument. W219's
unique native compact fundamental symmetry fixes a kinematic grading given the
Cartan split; it does not choose one element of the external orientation
torsor. The dynamical object that might carry a selection is absent, so W211's
frame-level invariant-space computation cannot be promoted into a native
interacting sign theorem in either direction.

Premise 4 closes the independent gauge-algebra shortcut. A VEV in the adjoint
cannot be sent linearly to a gauge-invariant real cosmological term because no
invariant linear functional exists. At quadratic order the unique invariant
bilinear form carries an unfixed coefficient, including its sign. For an
invariant potential of degree at most four, a nonzero radial local minimum has
negative shift

`V(v*)-V(0)=-alpha^2/(4 beta)<0`,

but `V(0)` remains a free constant, so the absolute value can have either sign.
The explicit degree-six polynomial

`V(s)=-s-(1/4)s^2-(1/54)s^3`

has `V(-6)=+1`, `V'(-6)=0` and `V''(-6)=1/6>0`. Hence even the shift lock is
not universal beyond its degree boundary. No alternative sign selector is
supplied. The sigma-only nonselection conclusion follows. ∎
