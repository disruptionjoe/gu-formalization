\section{Imaginary-packet energy and observation}
\label{sec:imaginary}

Let $t=i\psi$ in the $J$ packet, with real coefficients $\psi$.
Work on $I_t\times\RR^3\times V$, with $V$ sufficiently small around
$h=\eta$ that $\dd t$ has positive ambient square. Initial free data are
smooth with compact support in $\Sigma=\RR^3\times V$.
The packet is closed under the full Hessian by coefficient conjugation;
its metric and real-packet linear sources vanish. This is a statement
about the linearized equations, not nonlinear closure.

In a stationary frame write $C_R=E^0\partial_t+C_{\mathrm{sp}}$,
retaining all connection terms. For a complement representative $s$ set
$f=N^TC_{\mathrm{sp}}s$ and recover
\begin{equation}
 \psi=s-NW^{-1}f.\label{eq:recoverJ}
\end{equation}
The primary-kernel lemma and exact invertibility prove this recovery
satisfies every distortion primary equation. It is a finite-order local
operator and preserves support. The reduced time form is nondegenerate.
The factor $i$ reverses the quadratic action's sign:
\begin{align}
 L_J^{(2)}&=-\tfrac12\psi^TE^0\dot\psi
                -\tfrac12\psi^TC_{\mathrm{sp}}\psi,\nonumber\\
 H_{J,\mathrm{red}}^{(2)}[s]
 &=\frac12\int_\Sigma
       \left(s^TC_{\mathrm{sp}}s-f^TW^{-1}f\right)\dd\mu_\Sigma.
 \label{eq:HJ}
\end{align}
This is the Noether Hamiltonian, not the sign of an unreduced mass matrix.
Autonomy gives its conservation on any smooth solution with vanishing
boundary flux; the unboundedness argument needs only phase data.

\subsection{Two exact primary columns}
Choose the free direction $s_0=e^{10}1$ in a rational orthonormal frame
with radial index $10$. It has a nonzero time symplectic partner:
$s_0^TE^0E^0s_0=-48$, and is transverse to the primary kernel.
The complete primary sources and columns are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccccc}
\toprule
Derivative & Primary receiver & Source & Potential & Energy coefficient\\
\midrule
$e^4$ & $e^0\gamma_0\gamma_4\gamma_{10}$ & $2$ & $+\kappa$ & $-2/\kappa$\\
$e^1$ & $e^0\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_{10}$ & $2$ & $-\kappa$ & $+2/\kappa$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here $e^4$ is positive vertical traceless and $e^1$ horizontal spatial.
Each of the four background cubic columns on these receivers is exactly
zero, and XOR routing excludes additional primary receivers. Thus these
are columns of the full inverse problem, not a truncated corner.
For instance the relevant imaginary-slice terms
$-2z\partial_4\psi_0-\kappa z^2/2$ give
$z=-2\partial_4\psi_0/\kappa$ and Hamiltonian coefficient
$-2/\kappa$. A separate 128-state Clifford representation reproduces
both columns and all their source receivers \cite{ImaginaryEnergy}.

\begin{lemma}[Compact oscillatory amplification]
\label{lem:oscillation}
Suppose the exactly reduced local quadratic form on a fixed compact
support has principal amplitude term $Q(h,\dd\rho)(\partial_\rho a)^2$,
with $Q$ of strict constant sign and $\dd\rho\ne0$ there, and all
remaining terms have at most one derivative of the free amplitude.
Then exact local recovery from $a_n=b\sin(n\rho)$, $b\ne0$ smooth and
compactly supported, gives
\begin{equation}
 H[a_n]=\frac{n^2}{2}\int Q(h,\dd\rho)b^2\dd\mu+O(n).
 \label{eq:oscillation}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Recovery differentiates the amplitude at most once. Expanding the form
leaves the displayed $n^2\cos^2(n\rho)$ term; cutoff derivatives and
bounded smooth lower-order coefficients contribute at most $C_1n+C_0$.
Use $\cos^2(n\rho)=(1+\cos(2n\rho))/2$.
A vector field $X$ with $X\rho=1$ and integration by parts bound the
oscillatory integral by $O(n^{-1})$ on the fixed support. Its contribution
is therefore $O(n)$. The leading integral has the strict sign of $Q$.
\end{proof}

Choose a degree-zero fibre function
$y(h)=\ell(h)/h_{00}$, with $\ell(\eta)=0$ and
$\dd y|_\eta=e^4$. On a sufficiently small patch,
$r(y)=0$ and $|\dd y|^2>0$. Set
$s_n=s_0b\sin(ny)$ and use the complete curved recovery
\eqref{eq:recoverJ}. Then
\begin{equation}
 H_J[s_n]=-\frac{n^2}{\kappa}
       \int |\dd y|^2b^2\dd\mu_\Sigma+O(n).
 \label{eq:Jnegative}
\end{equation}
For the companion use $s_n=s_0b\sin(nx^1)$. Continuity of the exact
inverse preserves its opposite principal sign on a small patch, so its
energy is $+c n^2+O(n)$ for $\kappa>0$, with $c>0$.
The signs exchange on the mirror branch. This proves the imaginary part
of \cref{thm:main}; reversing the action sign also only exchanges signs.
No spectral cutoff, finite-difference inference, or global evolution
assumption is used.

\subsection{Section visibility and failure of energy descent}
Let $j_*(x)=(x,\eta)$. For $\kappa>0$, choose $b$ nonzero on the section
in the fibre-oscillating family. Its free field vanishes there, but exact
recovery gives
\begin{equation}
 j_*^*(t_n)=-\frac{2in}{\kappa}b(x,\eta)\dd x^0
                 \gamma_0\gamma_4\gamma_{10}.
 \label{eq:visible}
\end{equation}
This is an allowed imaginary grade-three coefficient. On the negative
coupling branch the spatial family supplies the analogous visible
negative-energy data.

Conversely choose the fibre support disjoint from the section. Local
recovery preserves that property, so the entire smooth germ at the
section vanishes while the energy is nonzero. The zero field has the
same observed germ and zero energy. Hence no function of that germ can
equal this Hamiltonian on the core. The nondegenerate reduced time form
also prevents treating these data as its presymplectic null directions.
This argument applies to smooth fields. It neither substitutes for a
physical reduction nor excludes nonlocal observation, integrating out
fields, or a narrower admissible domain.

\section{Real-packet energy with full metric decoupling}
\label{sec:real}

The real construction \cite{RealEnergy} uses a flat spatial three-torus, time $x^0$, and a small fibre neighbourhood
$V$ of $h_0=\diag(4,-1,-1,-1)$. Choose $V$ invariant under the three base
spatial sign reflections and within the positive-time patch.
The torus supplies finite volume for homogeneous base fields; compact
support below is strictly inside $V$.

\subsection{A character absent from the metric carrier}
For $\epsilon_i\in\{\pm1\}$ put
\begin{equation}
 R_\epsilon=\diag(1,\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2,\epsilon_3),
 \qquad h\mapsto R_\epsilon^ThR_\epsilon,
 \qquad \chi(\epsilon)=\epsilon_1\epsilon_2\epsilon_3.
 \label{eq:reflection}
\end{equation}
The group $G=(\mathbb Z/2)^3$ acts on the base, metric fibre, and Clifford
bundle. Naturality of the DeWitt metric, radial field, projectors, and
connection makes it a symmetry of the chosen background and action.
Its combined ambient orientation is preserved:
\begin{equation}
 \det R_\epsilon\,\det(\operatorname{Sym}^2R_\epsilon)
          =(\det R_\epsilon)^6=1.\label{eq:orientation}
\end{equation}
Thus Hodge, real structure, and time covector are preserved too.
The ten homogeneous metric components transform by
\begin{equation}
 u_{00},u_{ii}:1;\qquad u_{0i}:\epsilon_i;\qquad
 u_{ij}:\epsilon_i\epsilon_j\ (i\ne j).\label{eq:characters}
\end{equation}
None has character $\chi$.

Let $P_\chi=\frac18\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g)U_g$ on distortions.
The complete mixed operator, its fibre-integrated adjoint, and the
primary recovery intertwine $G$. It follows that
\begin{equation}
 A^\dagger t=0\quad\hbox{whenever }P_\chi t=t
 \label{eq:metriczero}
\end{equation}
for base-homogeneous fields with arbitrary time dependence.
Translation invariance makes the metric Euler expression homogeneous,
so the absence of homogeneous metric receivers proves vanishing against
arbitrary base metric tests, including lapse and shift. Interior support
kills the induced boundary contributions.
The stationary distortion operator preserves this character. Hence
$u=0$, with every time jet zero, satisfies the full linearized metric
equation on this sector. This is exact decoupling, not omission of an
equation. Products of two $\chi$ fields may source the trivial character,
so no nonlinear truncation of this character is claimed.

Choose an equivariant smooth complement to the time kernel; finite-group
averaging of an auxiliary positive coordinate form provides one. This
coordinate choice is not a physical norm. With $f=N^TC_{\mathrm{sp}}s$,
\begin{equation}
 t=s-NW^{-1}f,\qquad
 H_{R,\mathrm{red}}^{(2)}[s]=-\frac12\int
       \left(s^TC_{\mathrm{sp}}s-f^TW^{-1}f\right)\dd\mu_\Sigma.
 \label{eq:HR}
\end{equation}
The sign differs from \eqref{eq:HJ} because these coefficients have no
common factor $i$. The symplectic restriction to the real $\chi$
character remains nondegenerate: distinct real characters are
symplectically orthogonal and the total reduced form is nondegenerate.

\subsection{The full primary inverse for two real witnesses}
Let $\Gamma=\gamma_0\gamma_1\cdots\gamma_{13}$ and take
$s_0=e^0\Gamma$. Its complete 18-coordinate time block satisfies
$N^Ts_0=0$ and $s_0^TE^0E^0s_0=-13$.
For derivatives along vertical traceless $e^b$, with $b=4,11$ and
$\eta_4=1$, $\eta_{11}=-1$, the complete source has coefficient one
on the kernel vector
$r_b=e^0\gamma_{\{0,\ldots,13\}\setminus\{b\}}$.
Of the twelve target kernel directions, eleven form its connected
potential block; the remaining direction decouples.
On that block the exact matrices have structure
\begin{equation}
 W_b=\begin{pmatrix}
 \lambda G_b&(v+8w)h_b\\
 (v+8w)h_b^T&-\eta_b\kappa
 \end{pmatrix},\quad
 \lambda=\kappa-\frac{116a+4c}{3},\quad
 h_b^TG_b^{-1}h_b=-\eta_b\frac2{33}.
 \label{eq:realblock}
\end{equation}
Both Clifford implementations rebuild the complete derivative blocks and
all five potential matrices; symbolic recovery solves every row.
A Schur complement with $\tau=116a+4c-3\kappa$ gives the real principal
energy coefficient
\begin{equation}
 Q_b=\frac12 f^TW^{-1}f
   =-\eta_b\frac{11\tau}{2[11\kappa\tau+2(v+8w)^2]}
   =-\eta_b\frac{C}{\kappa},\label{eq:realQ}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 C=\frac{11\kappa^2(116\alpha+4\beta-3)}
 {2[11\kappa^2(116\alpha+4\beta-3)+2(v+8w)^2]},
 \qquad 0.388<C<0.389.\label{eq:realC}
\end{equation}
The strict bounds are outward rational intervals on the exact root;
numerator and denominator are separated from zero. Thus the two fibre
directions have opposite signs. These coefficients depend on the
background; the imaginary witness's zero cubic columns are not being
generalized. On \eqref{eq:mirror} they exchange signs.

\subsection{Character projection preserves the energy signs}
Choose a generic point near $h_0$ with all three $h_{0i}$ nonzero.
Its eight reflection translates are distinct. A small ball $B$ has eight
disjoint translated closures within $V$. Such points approach $h_0$,
so both strict coefficient signs persist in small neighbourhoods.
For each sign choose a smooth phase $\rho$ whose differential equals
the corresponding covector at the centre; shrink $B$ to keep it nonzero
and its coefficient of strict sign. For a cutoff $b\in C_c^\infty(B)$
put $s_n=s_0b\sin(n\rho)$, then project
$\widetilde s_n=P_\chi s_n$ and recover with the full curved operator.
On $B$, the projected field is $s_n/8$, hence is nonzero.
Locality and disjoint supports give the exact identity
\begin{equation}
 H_R[\widetilde s_n]=\frac18H_R[s_n].\label{eq:disjoint}
\end{equation}
Indeed all cross terms vanish and action symmetry makes the eight
unscaled diagonal terms equal. Recovery commutes with the same action.
The free first-order term in \eqref{eq:HR} and all lower-order recovery
terms meet \cref{lem:oscillation}. Hence
\begin{equation}
 H_R[\widetilde s_n^-]=-c_-n^2+O(n),\qquad
 H_R[\widetilde s_n^+]=+c_+n^2+O(n),\qquad c_\pm>0.
 \label{eq:real-asymptotic}
\end{equation}
Their complete metric force vanishes by \eqref{eq:metriczero}.

\subsection{All finite-order formal boundary conditions}
In this sector the boundary system \eqref{eq:generator} becomes
$\dot s=\cT s$ and $\cB s=0$, with $u=0$.
The reduced time-form inverse is pointwise and $W^{-1}$ is algebraic.
Thus both $\cT$ and recovery $\cR$ are finite-order differential
operators with smooth coefficients, preserving $\chi$.
For every integer $j\ge0$, the field $\cT^j\widetilde s_n$ has support
in the same compact union of balls. Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \cB\cT^j\widetilde s_n=0\quad(j\ge0),\qquad
 \partial_t^j u=0.\label{eq:allorder}
\end{equation}
These formal time jets solve the differentiated coupled equations and
all boundary compatibility conditions to each finite order. In particular
they avoid the $C_1,C_2$ failure of the zero-distortion acceleration data
in \cref{sec:boundary}. This completes the real assertion of
\cref{thm:main}.

A differential core and compatible jets do not select a closed generator
or prove convergence of its time series. The ambient ultrahyperbolic
domain problem remains. The conclusion is precisely that a completion
retaining this core and agreeing with its quadratic energy cannot make
that form semibounded. A nonlinear interacting Hamiltonian or quantum
operator is a further object requiring its own construction.

\section{What a physical selection must supply}
\label{sec:selection}

Ordinary conjugation gives the exact bosonic identity
$I(g,R+J)=I(g,R-J)$. The derivative in every $J$ direction therefore
vanishes at $J=0$, for arbitrary $g,R$.
The real-only restriction is a consistent nonlinear bulk restriction
when the boundary data respect it. Its energy is nevertheless indefinite
on the core just constructed. No closure under added fermionic terms is
asserted. Conversely the imaginary packet alone is not generally a
nonlinear restriction: an independently checked $RJJ$ third variation
on the specified background directions equals $-124/3$
\cite{RealReduction}.

A naive ambient compact-coefficient projection is a different proposal.
In the positive occupation-state matrix adjoint, $i1$ is compact, but its
spatial scalar profile forces a recovered
$i\gamma_0\gamma_1\gamma_{10}$ component with leading coefficient
$2\partial_1\phi/\kappa$, which is noncompact in that split.
The projection is not preserved by the tested primary recovery.
This tested ambient split is not the source's actual
$\operatorname{Spin}(6,4)$ shielding construction.

The observation obstruction in \cref{sec:imaginary} also has an off-shell
counterpart. A smooth bump $f$ away from the section and the grade-one
fields $U_+=e^0\gamma_4$, $U_-=e^0\gamma_{10}$ have zero full section
germ. Their wedge squares vanish; parity kills their derivative density.
The curvature-linear term cancels between opposite amplitudes, leaving
\begin{equation}
 I[\epsilon fU_\pm]+I[-\epsilon fU_\pm]-2I[0]
      =\pm\kappa\epsilon^2\int f^2\operatorname{vol}_Y.
 \label{eq:actiondescent}
\end{equation}
This proves failure of bare action descent on the smooth carrier.
It is distinct from, and weaker than, the constrained energy argument.
Neither result tests a proved nonlocal physical observable or an
integrating-out procedure.

A proposed physical completion must specify which fields and equations
it retains, how its restriction is propagated, and how its observed
energy and physical pairing are defined on one domain. A positive
conserved norm could coexist with an unbounded Hamiltonian; a lower energy
bound is an additional requirement. These calculations supply explicit
witnesses against which that requirement can be tested. They do not show
that every possible source completion, background, or physical quotient
must retain the witnesses.
