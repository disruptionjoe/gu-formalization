# Structural No-Go for Chirality-Safe Good-Stable Compactification

## A conditional compact-image theorem for neutral, operator-type, and charged-extremal order parameters in $\operatorname{Sp}(32,32)$

**Joseph Hernandez**
Independent Researcher

## Abstract

Consider a finite-dimensional indefinite module whose internal symmetry is the
quaternionic group $G=\operatorname{Sp}(32,32)$ and whose generation/mirror
grading is generated by a nonelliptic element $Z$. A common stability proxy asks
whether the stabilizer of an order parameter admits an invariant
positive-definite majorant. In finite dimensions this is equivalent to relative
compactness of the stabilizer's image. We prove that the desired combination
cannot be realized by three broad classes: $Z$-neutral vectors, grading-even
operator-type order parameters, and vectors at an extremal $Z$-weight. The first
two preserve the stated grading but retain the unbounded one-parameter group
$\exp(tZ)$ in their stabilizer. The third fails the majorant test regardless of
its discrete parity because it retains a nonzero nilpotent radical of the
parabolic defined by $Z$. We also show that an operator implementing a
maximal-compact reduction cannot be fixed by the grading. It need not be purely
grading-odd unless homogeneity is separately assumed; non-invariance is the
conclusion actually required.

The theorem is standard real-reductive and highest-weight structure assembled for
one fixed grading problem. Its contribution is the scoped synthesis, the explicit
escape taxonomy, and a correction of a tempting but false fixed-frame converse:
a relatively compact subgroup need not commute with one preferred Cartan
implementer. Applied to the currently enumerated Geometric Unity condensate
channels, the result excludes the neutral, operator, and charged-extremal routes.
That application is conditional on the stated representation census and record
branch. The paper does **not** establish interacting unitarity, a physical vacuum,
or the full native GU good-stability criterion. Non-extremal charged order
parameters and non-majorant notions of physical positivity remain open exits.

## 1. Result at a glance

The mathematical result is a three-part obstruction:

| order parameter | element forced into its stabilizer | obstruction to a positive majorant |
|---|---|---|
| $Z$-neutral vector or set of vectors | $\exp(tZ)$ | unbounded hyperbolic one-parameter group |
| grading-even operator $O$ | $\exp(tZ)\subset C_G(O)$ | same unbounded subgroup |
| maximal or minimal $Z$-weight vector | a nonzero nilpotent radical $\mathfrak n_+$ or $\mathfrak n_-$ | its exponential has unbounded polynomial growth |

For the fixed $\operatorname{Sp}(32,32)$ model, the familiar compact-reducing
operator $P$ has centralizer $\operatorname{Sp}(32)\times\operatorname{Sp}(32)$,
but it anticommutes with the standard block representative of $Z$. More generally,
no conjugate compact-reducing implementer can commute with $Z$. A compact reduction
of this operator type therefore breaks the grading-invariance condition.

The strongest honest conclusion is:

> Under the finite-dimensional invariant-positive-majorant proxy, none of the
> neutral, grading-even operator-type, or charged-extremal order parameters can
> simultaneously preserve the specified generation/mirror grading and leave a
> relatively compact stabilizer image. For the currently enumerated GU candidates,
> this closes those three routes, not every mathematically imaginable route.

Two distinctions are load-bearing:

1. “Majorant-good” below is a defined finite-dimensional proxy. It is not a synonym
   for the full interacting GU good-stability or ghost-clearance problem.
2. The abstract theorem is independent of GU. The claim that the present GU
   condensate inventory falls inside the three classes is a separate,
   reconstruction-grade application premise.
3. “Compactification” denotes reduction of the noncompact **internal symmetry** to
   a compact-image stabilizer. It does not mean compactification of spacetime or
   of extra dimensions.

## 2. Why this question is easy to overstate

Indefinite inner-product models invite three shortcuts that are not valid.

First, one can silently replace a physical indefinite theory with a positive
Hilbert theory. The existence of an invariant positive majorant is a precise and
useful mathematical test, but it does not construct an interacting physical state
space, scattering operator, observable algebra, or projection rule. Metric-operator
and Krein-space literature likewise treats the choice, boundedness, and physical
meaning of a positive metric as additional structure, not as an automatic
consequence of an indefinite form [Mostafazadeh 2005, 2006].

Second, one can confuse a preferred maximal compact subgroup $K$ with all compact
subgroups. Maximal compact subgroups of a connected real reductive group are
conjugate, not identical. A tilted subgroup $gKg^{-1}$ can have compact image
without commuting with the implementer chosen for $K$. Any proof that uses

$$
\text{compact image of }H
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\iota(H)\subset C_{\operatorname{GL}(E)}(P)
$$

for one fixed $P$ is therefore wrong. This paper does not use that implication.

Third, from $[P',Z]\neq0$ one cannot infer $\{P',Z\}=0$ without an extra
homogeneity premise. The correct general conclusion is only that $P'$ is not fixed
by the grading. That is enough to say that its condensate breaks the grading. The
stronger word “odd” is reserved below for an actual $-1$ eigenvector of the grading
involution.

## 3. Mathematical setting

### 3.1 Linear group and stabilizers

Let $E$ be a finite-dimensional real vector space and let

$$
\iota:G\hookrightarrow \operatorname{GL}(E)
$$

be a faithful representation of a connected real reductive group. We use the
underlying real representation even when $E$ originates as a complex or
quaternionic module. For a vector $w$ in a finite-dimensional $G$-representation
$R$, write

$$
G_w=\{g\in G:R(g)w=w\}.
$$

For a finite set $\mathbf w=(w_1,\ldots,w_m)$, $G_{\mathbf w}$ is the common
stabilizer. For an operator $O\in\operatorname{End}(E)$ acted on by conjugation,
the stabilizer is its centralizer

$$
C_G(O)=\{g\in G:\iota(g)O=O\iota(g)\}.
$$

All compactness statements refer to the closure of the image under $\iota$.
Faithfulness matters: without it a noncompact subgroup could be invisible in the
module on which the majorant is being tested.

### 3.2 Positive-majorant proxy

An **invariant positive majorant** for a subgroup $H\leq G$ is a positive-definite
real inner product $h$ on $E$ satisfying

$$
h(\iota(a)x,\iota(a)y)=h(x,y)
\qquad(a\in H).
$$

When $E$ also carries a nondegenerate indefinite form $\eta$ preserved by $G$, one
often writes $h(x,y)=\eta(x,Cy)$ for a suitable fundamental symmetry or metric
operator $C$. The direct definition through $h$ avoids assuming the very $C$ whose
existence is at issue.

The two formulations agree in the finite-dimensional real setting used here. If
$h$ and $\eta$ are both $H$-invariant, define the invertible $h$-self-adjoint
operator $A$ by $\eta(x,y)=h(x,Ay)$. Then $A$ commutes with $H$, and functional
calculus gives

$$
C=A|A|^{-1},
\qquad
C^2=1,
\qquad
\eta(x,Cy)=h(x,|A|y)>0.
$$

Conversely, an invariant $C$ with $\eta(\cdot,C\cdot)>0$ directly supplies $h$.
For the quaternionic specialization one may start with a positive quaternionic
Hermitian form and average it over the compact closure; the same compactness
equivalence follows on the underlying real module.

An order parameter is called **majorant-good** in this paper when its stabilizer
admits such an $h$. The phrase “good-stable compactification” in the title means
this internal compact-reduction proxy and nothing stronger.

### 3.3 The hyperbolic grading generator

Fix a nonzero semisimple $Z\in\mathfrak g$ such that $d\iota(Z)$ has a nonzero real
eigenvalue. Then

$$
t\longmapsto\iota(\exp tZ)
$$

is unbounded. Such an element is hyperbolic, hence nonelliptic; it cannot lie in
the Lie algebra of a compact subgroup in a faithful finite-dimensional
representation.

In the application, $d\iota(Z)^2=1$ after normalization on each
generation/mirror pair. Conjugation by $d\iota(Z)$ is therefore an order-two
operation on $\operatorname{End}(E)$:

$$
\sigma_Z(O)=d\iota(Z)O\,d\iota(Z)^{-1}.
$$

An operator is grading-even when $\sigma_Z(O)=O$, equivalently
$[O,d\iota(Z)]=0$, and grading-odd when $\sigma_Z(O)=-O$, equivalently
$\{O,d\iota(Z)\}=0$. An arbitrary operator can have both even and odd parts.

For an arbitrary representation $R$ in which $dR(Z)$ is diagonalizable over
$\mathbb R$, a vector is **$Z$-neutral** when $dR(Z)w=0$. A $Z$-weight vector
has $dR(Z)w=\lambda w$. It is
**$Z$-extremal** when $\lambda$ is maximal or minimal among the real
$Z$-weights occurring in $R$.

“Chirality-safe” is used narrowly: the condensate does not spontaneously break the
specified generation/mirror $\mathbb Z_2$ grading. It is not a claim that a full
chiral quantum field theory, anomaly cancellation, or a mass spectrum has been
constructed.

## 4. The elementary compactness equivalence

**Proposition 1 (invariant majorant versus compact closure).** Let
$H\leq\operatorname{GL}(E)$. An $H$-invariant positive-definite inner product on
$E$ exists if and only if $\overline H$ is compact.

**Proof.** If $h$ is invariant, $H$ lies in the orthogonal group $O(E,h)$, which is
compact in finite dimensions. Hence $\overline H$ is compact.

Conversely, if $\overline H$ is compact, choose any positive-definite inner product
$h_0$ and average it over normalized Haar measure:

$$
h(x,y)=\int_{\overline H}h_0(ax,ay)\,d\mu(a).
$$

The average is positive definite and $\overline H$-invariant. $\square$

This proposition is elementary. It is included so that the paper's stability proxy
does not hide an imported theorem or an ambiguous use of “compact.”

**Lemma 2 (two visible noncompactness witnesses).** Suppose $H\leq G$.

1. If $H$ contains $\exp(tZ)$ for all $t\in\mathbb R$, then
   $\overline{\iota(H)}$ is not compact.
2. If $H$ contains $\exp(tX)$ for a nonzero $X\in\mathfrak g$ such that
   $d\iota(X)$ is nonzero nilpotent, then $\overline{\iota(H)}$ is not compact.

**Proof.** The first image is unbounded by the definition of $Z$. In the second
case, $\exp(td\iota(X))$ is a nonconstant polynomial in $t$ and is therefore
unbounded. $\square$

## 5. The three-class no-go

Let

$$
\mathfrak g=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\mathbb R}\mathfrak g_\alpha,
\qquad
\mathfrak g_\alpha=\{X\in\mathfrak g:[Z,X]=\alpha X\}.
$$

Set $\mathfrak n_+=\bigoplus_{\alpha>0}\mathfrak g_\alpha$ and
$\mathfrak n_-=\bigoplus_{\alpha<0}\mathfrak g_\alpha$. For a hyperbolic element
of a noncompact semisimple real group these are the nilpotent radicals associated
with the opposite parabolics defined by $Z$; see the standard Cartan and restricted
root constructions in [Helgason 2001; Knapp 2002]. We state the exact hypotheses
needed rather than rely on a label:

- $\mathfrak n_+$ and $\mathfrak n_-$ are nonzero;
- their nonzero root elements act as nonzero nilpotent endomorphisms under the
  faithful representation $\iota$.

These hypotheses hold for the $\operatorname{Sp}(32,32)$ grading used in Section 7.

**Theorem 3 (neutral/operator/extremal obstruction).** Under the setting above:

1. If every member of a finite set $\mathbf w=(w_1,\ldots,w_m)$ is $Z$-neutral,
   then $G_{\mathbf w}$ is not majorant-good.
2. If $O\in\operatorname{End}(E)$ is grading-even, then $C_G(O)$ is not
   majorant-good.
3. If $dR(Z)$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb R$ and $w\neq0$ is a maximal or
   minimal $Z$-weight vector in a finite-dimensional $G$-representation, then
   $G_w$ is not majorant-good.

**Proof.**

1. Since $dR_i(Z)w_i=0$, $R_i(\exp tZ)w_i=w_i$ for every $i$ and every $t$.
   Thus $\exp(tZ)\subset G_{\mathbf w}$. Lemma 2 and Proposition 1 apply.
2. Grading-even means $[O,d\iota(Z)]=0$, so
   $\exp(tZ)\subset C_G(O)$. Apply the same two results.
3. Suppose $dR(Z)w=\lambda w$ and $\lambda$ is maximal. If
   $X\in\mathfrak g_\alpha$ with $\alpha>0$, then

   $$
   dR(Z)dR(X)w=(\lambda+\alpha)dR(X)w.
   $$

   No weight above $\lambda$ occurs, so $dR(X)w=0$. Hence
   $\mathfrak n_+\subset\mathfrak g_w$. Choose a nonzero root element
   $X\in\mathfrak n_+$. By hypothesis $d\iota(X)$ is nonzero nilpotent, so
   Lemma 2 and Proposition 1 exclude a majorant. For a minimal weight, use
   $\mathfrak n_-$. $\square$

The third argument is the local mechanism behind the standard fact that
highest-weight directions are stabilized by parabolic structure. Literature on
highest-weight faces and stabilizers develops the broader setting [Khare 2017].
The proof above is included because only the weight-raising implication is needed.

### 5.1 What the theorem does not quantify over

Theorem 3 is not a no-go for every vector in every representation. In particular,
it does not cover:

- a non-extremal $Z$-weight vector;
- a vector that is not a $Z$-eigenvector;
- an order parameter whose physical stability is not modeled by an invariant
  positive majorant on $E$;
- an infinite-dimensional or unbounded-operator problem where domains,
  completions, and topology are load-bearing.

The exclusions are real, not ceremonial. For example, a timelike vector in the
standard representation of $\operatorname{SO}(2,1)$ has compact stabilizer
$\operatorname{SO}(2)$ but is not an eigenvector of the boost that mixes its time
and spatial directions. This is a concrete counterexample to any stronger claim
that “boost-charged” alone forces noncompact stabilizer.

## 6. Compact reduction cannot preserve the grading

The previous theorem covers grading-even operators directly. A related statement
targets the desired maximal-compact reduction.

Let $P'\in\operatorname{End}(E)$ be an involutory implementer whose centralizer
$C_G(P')$ is relatively compact. In the classical matrix realization, such an
operator implements a Cartan involution on the Lie algebra and its centralizer is
a maximal compact subgroup.

**Corollary 4 (compact-reducing implementers break the grading).** No such $P'$
commutes with $d\iota(Z)$.

**Proof.** If $[P',d\iota(Z)]=0$, then
$\exp(tZ)\subset C_G(P')$. The left side is unbounded, contradicting relative
compactness of the centralizer. $\square$

The conclusion is **not** that every $P'$ anticommutes with $d\iota(Z)$. It is that
$P'$ is not grading-even. If $P'$ is known independently to be homogeneous under
$\sigma_Z$, it is then grading-odd. Without that premise it can have mixed parity.
Either way, a condensate proportional to $P'$ is not invariant under the grading.

Corollary 4 also avoids the false fixed-frame statement discussed in Section 2. It
does not ask a compact subgroup to commute with one preferred $P$. It asks whether
the **operator producing that subgroup** can itself preserve the fixed grading.

## 7. The $\operatorname{Sp}(32,32)$ specialization

### 7.1 Notation and dimensions

We use the standard notation $\operatorname{Sp}(p,q)$ for the quaternionic
symplectic group preserving a quaternionic Hermitian form of signature $(p,q)$
[Sedano-Mendoza 2018]. The repository's older notation
$\operatorname{Sp}(p,q;\mathbb H)$ denotes the same real group here.

For $G=\operatorname{Sp}(32,32)$, a maximal compact subgroup is

$$
K=\operatorname{Sp}(32)\times\operatorname{Sp}(32).
$$

Using $\dim_{\mathbb R}\operatorname{Sp}(n)=n(2n+1)$ gives

$$
\dim G=64(129)=8256,
$$

$$
\dim K=2\cdot32(65)=4160,
\qquad
\dim(G/K)=4096.
$$

### 7.2 One hyperbolic pair

On one positive/negative pair in a definite basis, take the following real
$2\times2$ slice (tensored with the appropriate quaternionic identity in the full
module):

$$
\beta=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\
0&-1
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
z=d\iota(Z)=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Then $z^2=1$ and

$$
\exp(tz)=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cosh t&\sinh t\\
\sinh t&\cosh t
\end{pmatrix}
$$

has eigenvalues $e^t$ and $e^{-t}$. Thus $Z$ is a genuine nonelliptic generator,
not merely a discrete label. Conjugation by $z$ is nevertheless order two on the
operator algebra because $z^2=1$. The continuous boost and the discrete operator
grading are related but not identical objects.

The standard compact-reducing implementer is $P=\beta$. It satisfies

$$
P^2=1,
\qquad
\{P,z\}=0,
\qquad
C_G(P)=\operatorname{Sp}(32)\times\operatorname{Sp}(32)
$$

after assembly over the 32 quaternionic pairs. This representative is genuinely
grading-odd. Conjugation $X\mapsto PXP^{-1}$ implements the standard Cartan
involution on the matrix Lie algebra, and the fixed algebra is the Lie algebra of
the displayed centralizer. Corollary 4 gives the frame-independent statement:
every conjugate implementer with relatively compact centralizer fails to be
grading-even, although it need not anticommute with $z$ in the chosen frame.

### 7.3 Restricted-root structure

The adjoint action of $Z$ decomposes $\mathfrak{sp}(32,32)$ into zero, positive,
and negative restricted-root spaces. Positive and negative root elements are
nilpotent in the defining algebraic representation. Consequently the hypotheses of
Theorem 3(3) hold. No extrapolation from an orthogonal toy model is needed for the
written theorem; the low-rank orthogonal models in the repository are regression
fixtures for the same restricted-root mechanism, not substitutes for the proof.

## 8. Conditional application to the current GU condensate inventory

The mathematical theorem and the GU application have different grades. The
application uses the repository's current reconstruction of a
$\mathrm{Cl}(9,5)\cong M_{64}(\mathbb H)$ matter arena, its generation/mirror
grading, and its candidate condensate channels. The identification of this module
and the completeness of the candidate inventory are not proved here from a public
source action.

The current inventory maps into the theorem as follows:

| current candidate | mathematical class | result | application qualification |
|---|---|---|---|
| invariant scalar / neutral set | $Z$-neutral | retains $\exp(tZ)$; no invariant majorant | applies to the enumerated neutral candidates |
| channel-D operator proportional to the standard $P$ | operator-type compact reducer | compact centralizer, but the condensate breaks the grading; for the standard representative it is odd | whether this condensate is an allowed vacuum depends on the W235 record branch |
| mirror-only channel-S component, if nonzero | minimal $Z$-weight | retains $\mathfrak n_-$; no invariant majorant | the source census also reports no required singlet bilinear; that separate emptiness claim is not needed for the theorem |
| non-extremal charged vector or mixed-weight vector | outside Theorem 3 | open | no currently constructed GU-native example is established |

This supports the following **conditional application claim**:

> If the present representation dictionary is correct, if the W235
> record-conserved branch is the chirality-relevant branch, and if the current
> neutral/operator/extremal census exhausts the GU-native condensates under
> consideration, then none of those condensates yields a grading-preserving
> majorant-good compact reduction of $\operatorname{Sp}(32,32)$.

The words “under consideration” are deliberate. A future source action could
produce a new non-extremal order parameter, alter the relevant representation, or
show that invariant-majorant stability is the wrong physical predicate. Any of
those would leave the abstract theorem intact while defeating or bypassing the GU
application.

## 9. Relation to physical good-stability

The finite-dimensional proxy asks for an invariant positive-definite majorant under
a stabilizer. The native GU problem is substantially stronger and differently
typed. At minimum it would require a specified interacting source action, a
physical state space or constrained quotient, an observable algebra, and a
dynamical evolution or scattering operator. Questions of renormalization,
spectral condition, infrared behavior, gauge constraints, domains, and projected
unitarity do not appear in Proposition 1.

Therefore this paper does not prove any of the following:

- that GU has no good stable;
- that GU is nonunitary or physically inconsistent;
- that a compact stabilizer is sufficient for physical unitarity;
- that a positive metric operator exists for the interacting theory;
- that chirality, anomaly cancellation, or the observed generation count is
  dynamically derived;
- that a physical mass is generated whenever a grading-breaking order parameter is
  algebraically available;
- that the current GU condensate census is complete beyond its stated source
  reconstruction.

What it proves is narrower and usable: one common finite-dimensional route to
stability cannot be realized by three specified classes while preserving the fixed
grading.

## 10. Escape taxonomy and decisive next objects

The result leaves four distinct exits. They should not be blended.

### Exit A: a non-extremal charged order parameter

A vector at an interior $Z$-weight, or a vector mixing several $Z$-weights, need
not contain the raising or lowering radical in its stabilizer. The
$\operatorname{SO}(2,1)$ timelike example proves that a mixed-weight vector can
have compact stabilizer. To use this exit in GU one must construct such an order
parameter from the source representation and show that it is physically admissible
without importing the desired answer.

### Exit B: a non-majorant physical positivity mechanism

Physical positivity might live on a constrained subspace, quotient, boundary, or
interface rather than as a positive majorant invariant under the full finite
stabilizer. That would be outside Proposition 1. It must be supplied as an explicit
state space and evolution law; the label “PT-symmetric,” “Krein-unitary,” or
“firewall” is not itself a construction.

### Exit C: a different grading or representation

If the physical grading is elliptic rather than hyperbolic in the relevant faithful
module, the unbounded-subgroup argument no longer applies. Likewise, if the
$\operatorname{Sp}(32,32)$ representation dictionary is wrong, the application
must be redone. Either change is a scope exit, not a counterexample.

### Exit D: an incomplete native census

The theorem does not prove that every GU-native order parameter is neutral,
operator-type, or extremal. A source-derived non-extremal candidate would reopen
the application immediately. This is the most direct way to falsify the census
premise.

## 11. Adversarial checks and falsifiers

A reader should try to break the paper at the following points.

### 11.1 Theorem falsifiers

1. Exhibit a faithful finite-dimensional representation in which the stipulated
   $\exp(tZ)$ is bounded despite $d\iota(Z)$ having a nonzero real eigenvalue.
2. Exhibit a grading-even operator whose centralizer omits $\exp(tZ)$.
3. Exhibit a maximal $Z$-weight vector not annihilated by a positive
   $\operatorname{ad}(Z)$ root space.
4. Exhibit a nonzero root element stipulated to be nilpotent whose exponential has
   relatively compact image in the faithful module.

Any such example would contradict an explicit line of the proof, not merely its
interpretation.

### 11.2 Application falsifiers and scope exits

1. Derive a GU-native non-extremal charged order parameter with compact-image
   stabilizer and preserved physical grading.
2. Show from the source action that the relevant $Z$ is elliptic, acts trivially on
   the physical module, or is not the physical chirality grading.
3. Construct the native physical state space and evolution law and show that its
   positivity criterion is not equivalent to an invariant majorant on $E$.
4. Refute the current $\mathrm{Cl}(9,5)$ representation dictionary or its
   condensate census.

These would weaken or bypass the GU application but would not automatically refute
Theorem 3.

## 13. Prior art, novelty, and use

The ingredients are established mathematics:

- invariant inner products can be averaged over compact groups;
- Cartan involutions determine maximal compact subgroups, unique up to conjugacy;
- hyperbolic elements generate unbounded one-parameter groups;
- highest- or extremal-weight directions are stabilized by positive or negative
  parabolic structure;
- nilpotent one-parameter subgroups are noncompact in faithful linear
  representations.

Real reductive orbit theory and minimal-vector methods provide a broader setting
for compact and closed orbit questions [Richardson and Slodowy 1990]. Standard
Cartan, restricted-root, and parabolic facts are developed in [Helgason 2001;
Knapp 2002]. Highest-weight stabilizer structure is treated in more generality in
[Khare 2017].

Accordingly, this paper does **not** claim a new theorem of general Lie theory. Its
novelty claim is narrower:

1. it assembles the standard mechanisms into one auditable obstruction for the
   fixed $\operatorname{Sp}(32,32)$ generation/mirror grading;
2. it separates the theorem from the GU-native census and from interacting
   positivity;
3. it identifies the exact non-extremal and non-majorant exits;
4. it corrects the fixed-frame compactness fallacy and the noncommuting-versus-odd
   overstatement;
5. it turns an informal “compactification kills chirality” slogan into explicit
   hypotheses, quantifiers, and falsifiers.

The practical use is diagnostic. Any proposed model in this class must point to
which hypothesis it violates and exhibit the replacement object. This prevents a
compact stabilizer, a positive metric, or a chiral grading from being assumed by
terminology alone.

## 14. Limitations

The limitations are part of the result:

- finite dimensionality is essential to Proposition 1 as stated;
- faithfulness of the majorant-tested module is essential;
- the extremal theorem assumes nonzero positive/negative root spaces acting
  nilpotently in that faithful module;
- the theorem covers a union of three classes, not all order parameters;
- “chirality-safe” means preservation of a specified algebraic grading, not a
  complete chiral field theory;
- the GU application depends on a reconstruction-grade module dictionary, native
  census, and the W235 record-conserved branch;
- the interacting source action, vacuum equations, physical state space, and
  observable algebra remain unbuilt;
- the paper has internal computational rechecking but no external replication or
  formal proof assistant certificate.

## 15. Conclusion

In a faithful finite-dimensional module, an invariant positive majorant exists
exactly when the acting subgroup has compact closure. A hyperbolic grading makes
three failures immediate once the stabilizer is inspected correctly: neutral
vectors and grading-even operators retain the unbounded grading flow, while
extremal charged vectors retain a nilpotent parabolic radical. An operator that
implements a compact reduction cannot itself preserve that grading.

For the fixed $\operatorname{Sp}(32,32)$ construction and the currently enumerated
GU condensate channels, this yields a structural, conditional no-go. It does not
say that every compact subgroup sits in one preferred frame, that every reducing
operator is purely odd, that GU lacks a physical good stable, or that interacting
unitarity has been decided. It says precisely where the standard
positive-majorant route fails and what a successful escape must construct.
